## What this document is

`automation_scope.md` covers all twenty automations across both halves of the business. This one covers **only the fulfilment half** — the work Whitney described, that her team performs on client accounts, and that has **no row anywhere in the audit spreadsheet**.

It exists because of a structural asymmetry that will be visible in the room on Thursday: the twelve-row client-filled audit is entirely Gisselle's seat, in one department category out of thirteen, while seven people do the work. On 2 Sept, roughly 17 minutes covered the whole spreadsheet and roughly 21 minutes covered work with no row in it at all. **The fulfilment side is the larger conversation and the smaller document.** This is that document.

Every quote is verbatim with its timestamp. Where a claim is E2M inference rather than something the client said, it is labelled as inference.

## 2.1 B1 · Monthly reporting → executive summary — BUILT as MVP 4

> *"we have Tina on the team, basically, like, dedicating the first few business days of the month to pulling these reports together for clients. It's not automated in any way right now. She's like pulling in screenshots from the different ad accounts, pulling in screenshots of best performing ads. We're adding in additional context about like the why behind it all. And then also like recording a video walkthrough to kind of like explain it to the client."* — 2 Sept @33:01

**The framing that prices better than hours.** Tina is *simultaneously* the sole LeadLock/GHL designer building every landing page and writing every email and SMS nurture sequence for every in-flight onboarding. The reporting block consumes the first business days of every month and there is no redundancy. **Every client who signs in the first week of a month waits on their build behind a screenshot deck.** That is revenue timing on new business, not admin cost.

**Whitney has already specified the solution and named her reference implementation:** *"I've been playing around with Victor and our Slack... he can connect to lead block accounts, Google ads accounts, meta ads accounts, and just have an overarching like view of everything. So I would love something like that that can create more of just like an executive summary on a monthly basis."*

**What gets automated.** A one-page client-ready HTML/PDF generated from a metric set plus the client's own account record:

1. A headline sentence — what happened this month, no jargon, no acronym not already in their own report.
2. A metric table using their own metric names, each with the prior period and the delta, each carrying a visible `MOCK` / `LIVE · GHL` provenance chip. No screenshots.
3. **A trend plus its cause** — the trend read off the numbers, the cause joined to MVP 2's ad-account change log and rendered as a clickable citation resolving to the timestamped moment in the call where Carmen makes the change. If no logged change plausibly explains the movement, the cause slot renders *"No logged account change explains this — needs a human"* and the run enters the abstain queue. **It will not manufacture a reason.**
4. A recommendation with a named owner and a flag for whether it needs client approval.

**Scope discipline: do not replicate the deck.** Designing their layout blind against a sample nobody has seen means Tina spots the mismatch in ten seconds. The deliverable Whitney asked for is the thing she wants *instead* of the deck, and that half has a verbal spec already in hand: *"something clearer for clients to understand as opposed to like so many screenshots of the metrics."* **Automate the prep, keep Tina on camera.**

**Cut from this card:** the 90-second walkthrough-video script. The video was requested and never arrived, so register and length were pure guesses — the least-grounded leg of any selected card. It returns for +0.2d the day the video arrives, and it comes back as a script for a human to record, never as synthesised narration.

**Success criteria that matter on this card:** one Letter page whose section order and metric names match the real Canva report side by side; zero unlabelled numbers; **zero unsourced causes across five fixtures** (three where a logged change explains the movement, two where nothing does and the card must abstain); and the citation resolving all the way to the moment in the call.

## 2.2 B2 · Content and marketing repurposing — BUILT as MVP 5

**The only item she tied to revenue.** The current chain is five manual hops with a founder decision at every one: she recalls a strong client moment from a strategy call → flags it to Gisselle → Gisselle puts the transcript into ChatGPT → Whitney refines → records → then commissions the carousel separately → then the email marketing separately. Every hop stalls whenever she is on a client call, which since the account manager resigned is all day.

**The mechanism she asked for, in her words** (26 Aug @20:00): *"even if I'm just, like, giving it a voice note of, like, hey, I want to reference this call. Here's more context. Here's more history. This is the direction that I want to take it. And then be able to, like, multi-purpose that into a lot of content."*

**What gets automated.** A staged pass on the same intake and the same review surface as everything else:

- **Stage 1 — quote mining with hard verbatim verification.** Mines her own three categories (praise of the work, growth of the business, how things feel) and verifies every quote character-for-character against the source before it reaches the card. A quote the verifier cannot locate is dropped and logged, never printed. If the call contains no result-praise at all, the card says so and stops instead of inventing three scripts.
- **Stage 2 — three reel scripts,** each with a hook, three to five beats, a CTA, on-screen text, a runtime estimate, and honest citations (clickable `?timestamp=` where the source has a player URL, bare `@MM:SS` where it does not, with the difference visible). Two safety fields print on screen: `claims_needing_proof` for any number the script asserts that no verified quote supports, and `client_named: false` — scripts are drafted anonymised (*"a Texas wedding venue"*) and naming the client is a deliberate toggle that simultaneously drafts the permission ask.
- **Stage 3 — carousel copy,** only after a script is approved, derived from the approved and edited text rather than the original draft.
- **Stage 4 — the marketing email,** drafted into `ai@weddingindustryads.com`, only after the carousel is approved.

Her edit path is a reaction plus a thread reply: the reply is appended to the direction and Stage 2 re-runs against the same verified quote set. **Nothing posts. Nothing sends.**

**It is also the harvest step on an asset they generate and discard.** Every strategy call where a client says something good is a case study that currently evaporates. Their public proof is thin — one spoken ~13× ROAS anecdote, no named case study with attributed metrics — which is a direct constraint on close rate when selling to tech-challenged small businesses who need reassurance. Every validated quote is written back to the client record as a permission-tagged entry, which is what makes this card compound rather than terminate.

**Two things to ask rather than assume:** whether she will publish marketing built from a client's words without telling that client (adjacent to the human-touch constraint — a fabricated-sounding testimonial in the wedding industry is a real problem, not a cosmetic one), and whether reels are her format of record or a leftover.

## 2.3 B3 · Creative briefs and first-draft copy — CUT

**Whitney's own unprompted #1 nomination, and the hardest call in the plan.** Said in the same breath as the pain it solves — *"it always feels like we're like chasing the team"* — and never revisited on 2 Sept.

She had already validated the mechanism herself: *"if they're prompted and say, here's the meta ad strategy that ChatGPT came up with, like that, that's more of a push for them than just starting from a blank page."* (2 Sept @32:16)

**Why it matters structurally:** onboarding runs through ~8 sequential client-approval gates across Carmen, Jacqueline, Ivy, Tina and Danielle, with one person per function and no redundancy. Every gate that starts from a blank page adds a day of latency to a client who is already paying. Time-to-first-lead is what retention turns on.

**Why it was cut, and why the reason is permanent rather than a scheduling problem.** The per-client ChatGPT Project cannot be shared, because WIA runs on one shared `hello@` login with no Team or org account, and Project sharing strips files. That removes both the house brief format and **the only reference for what the team currently accepts as a usable draft**. Its input corpus is the only one in the portfolio that does not exist yet. It was also the third demonstration of a capability already shown twice.

**What survives:** the **input half**, inside MVP 1 at ~0.15d. The messaging-and-positioning call becomes a demonstrated call type, and the per-client brief Gisselle hand-assembles into a ChatGPT Project today builds itself — which retires the band-aid Whitney said she does not see as a long-term solution.

**What is queued:** the **output half** — Ivy's creative brief and Tina's landing-page copy block — is the **named restore-first item** at +0.3d, triggered by the Ridgefield inventory returning shipped artifacts. It is first back regardless of what else is restored. The fifteen-RSA-headlines leg is cut outright: Carmen already *"comes to the call already prepared with keyword research."*

**Honest risk if restored: adoption, not build.** It lands on four people nobody has interviewed, and a generated brief can read as a mandate rather than a gift.

## 2.4 B4 · Landing-page and funnel QA — BUILT as MVP 3

**The last clause of B3, and the only automation candidate in the entire set with zero credential dependency on the target systems.**

**Evidence this is recurring, not hypothetical, is their own funnel.** On 26 Aug @41:28 a manual due-diligence pass found a mobile-only CTA resolving to a 404. Whitney: *"we actually caught that a couple of days ago"* — then immediately *"Did I email you or did I slack you about that, Gisselle, already? I don't remember."* Sixty seconds later a second broken link surfaced, in the investment guide. **Two defects in one glance at one company's own funnel, both found by humans by accident, one of them then lost in the handoff.**

Their one documented live defect is **mobile-only**, so a desktop-only crawl reports all clear on their actual bug. Mobile (393×852, DPR 3, touch, mobile UA) is the primary pass; 1440×900 desktop is a single secondary pass.

**What gets automated.** One Slack command returns one card with five blocks:

1. **Blockers** — deterministic and entirely code: non-200 pages, primary-CTA or nav links resolving to 4xx/5xx, a form whose action 404s, a missing or zoom-locked viewport meta, no primary CTA inside the first mobile viewport, a LeadLock page with no reachable privacy-policy URL. Each line carries the resolved URL, the literal status code and the CSS selector it came from.
2. **Warnings** — tap targets under 44px, body text under 16px, console errors, failed subresources, mixed content, missing title or H1, page weight, form-field composition, consent-checkbox state, and whether GTM, GA4, the Meta pixel and the Google Ads conversion tag actually fire in the page's own network traffic.
3. **Conversion readiness** — one verdict per page (`ship` / `ship_with_fixes` / `do_not_ship`), one sentence, up to five ranked fixes, each citing a finding id from block 1 or 2. A fix citing nothing is dropped before the card posts, and the drop count prints.
4. **LeadLock go-live / A2P readiness** — privacy-policy reachability, SMS consent language, opt-in control unchecked by default, terms link, and the page's canonical final URL captured for the campaign swap.
5. **Disclosure footer** — `Facts: N deterministic · Judgements: M (labelled) · Not checked: K · Model claims dropped: D`.

**The A2P block is deliberately kept despite advice to cut it.** It is the only place Danielle appears anywhere in the portfolio, and Whitney described the cross-track dependency at length and unprompted (2 Sept @33:01): the privacy-policy page gates A2P approval, which gates LeadLock going live, which forces the campaign URL swap back to Carmen and Jacqueline. Cutting it would leave the engagement with zero coverage of one of the four single points of failure she named herself.

**Scope discipline:** pages WIA built and hosts in GHL, on a client-confirmed URL list. 25 pages max per run, one request per second per host, 20-second page budget, robots.txt respected during discovery. **No writes to anything, ever** — not a page, a CMS, GHL, ClickUp or Canva. No client-facing output path exists, which is the cheapest possible mitigation of the mis-tiered-write leak risk. Crawling a client's live production property uninvited is a third-party action.

## 2.5 B5 · Onboarding orchestration — PARTIAL

The ~8 sequential client-approval gates, the two parallel tracks (ads and LeadLock), Danielle's DNS and A2P work, and the cross-track dependency where LeadLock going live forces the ads URL swap.

**Diagnosis, and it is the most important sentence in this section: this is primarily a state-visibility and accountability problem with generative work embedded in it.** "Chasing" means nobody can see state. That is fixed by a status surface plus nudges, not by a model. **Do not sell an agent where a dashboard is the answer** — and note that the dashboard must be **Slack-native**, because ClickUp has no adoption (*"the team has never, like, fully adopted ClickUp other than to just, like, check off the tasks sort of thing"*, 2 Sept @42:16) and the word "dashboard" appears zero times across both transcripts despite being in the 11 Aug sold scope.

**What is built for Thursday:** the setup fan-out only, as MVP 1's `signature_event` call type. One trigger — the Dubsado contract-signed plus invoice-paid pair landing in Gmail — produces the offer-appropriate ClickUp onboarding list instantiated with assignees and due dates, the client Drive folder with the two-tier internal/client-facing structure, the per-client Slack channel, the team announcement, and the welcome-email draft carrying the countersigned contract and the kickoff-call booking link.

**What is Apollo:** the lifecycle itself — gate state, the nudge loop, and the cross-track dependency alert when LeadLock goes live.

**Note on the trigger's fragility:** most contracts are in Dubsado today, but Whitney has already committed to migrating that into GHL offer by offer. The Gmail alert signal survives that migration; a Dubsado API integration would not. There is no Dubsado API in any case.

## 2.7 B7 · The client knowledge layer — BUILT, as the spine

Per-client ChatGPT **Projects** on a shared `hello@` login with no Team or org account. Knowledge base seeded with the onboarding questionnaire and the messaging-and-positioning interview transcript, used to draft Meta strategy, Google strategy and landing-page copy. The team reviews and tweaks.

**They independently invented the concept, proved internal appetite, and identified its failure mode before we did.** Minu warned that knowledge bases are easy to create and rot within a couple of months. Whitney: *"Yeah, no, I mean, that's what I see coming, right?"* And: *"we don't see it as a long-term solution."* She called it *"a band-aid"* (2 Sept @32:16).

**What replaces it:** `clients/<slug>/client_brief.md` plus an `updates/` append log — one file per client, written by MVP 1 and MVP 2, read by MVP 4 and MVP 5. This is the answer to the question she has asked across three calls and never had answered:

> *"the biggest thing that I'm looking for at this point, I feel like, is like, how is it all connected? How does it all kind of work together sort of thing... is there one place that we're going, and then there are just like, different tabs?"* — 26 Aug @19:21

It is also what justifies a platform engagement over point automations, and it is the concrete form of the turnover-proofing thesis the engagement was sold on: **when the next account manager starts, the agency brain is a file they can read, not five people they have to interview.**

**Also a security and continuity issue worth raising gently:** a shared password with no audit trail means offboarding anyone requires a rotation. Awkward for a client explicitly buying turnover-proofing.

# PART 4 — What stays human, by design

1. **Nothing client-facing sends without human review.** *"we'll review any drafts before they go out"* (2 Sept @8:01), standing since 11 Aug. Every fulfilment card terminates in a Slack review card. MVP 4 and MVP 5 have **no send function in the code path** — not a disabled flag, no function.
2. **The human touch on client relationships stays human.** Whitney on the calls, because she said she wants to be: *"I like being on those calls. I feel like it makes that high touch for the clients, and they feel, like, really special and taken care of"* (2 Sept @19:59). Access-gathering stays a live screen-share, because they have tried it every other way over the years.
3. **Tina stays on camera** for the report walkthrough. The video is client hand-holding, which the human-touch constraint puts off limits to automation permanently — and when the script leg is restored it returns as a script for a human to record, never as synthesised narration.
4. **Ivy owns design.** MVP 5 produces carousel *copy*, never carousel design — no Canva render, no caption burn-in, no thumbnail, no voice cloning, no AI-generated likeness of Whitney.
5. **Whitney remains the selector** for which calls become marketing. Auto-pushing three scripts per client call to an already inundated founder recreates the notification-treadmill failure identified for roll-call at low volume. Auto-flagging is a one-field change in Apollo week 1, not a Thursday claim.
6. **Flags flag; they do not chase.** *"Just needs to flag, we will manually follow up."*
7. **Nothing is written into a client-visible surface without passing the tier gate.** The `wia+ridgefield`-pattern subfolder is shared with the client, so a mis-tiered write leaks internal material. This is the hardest constraint in the engagement and it is enforced in code, not by convention.
