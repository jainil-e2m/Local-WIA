## Shared structure (all three)
- **Three levels of decision.** A *WIA Standard* applies unless the account gives a reason not to. A *WIA Decision Framework* depends on data. A *Client / Account-Specific Decision* is made per client. "Do not turn examples, benchmarks… into universal rules."
- **Every task starts from the client's "Client Marketing Profile"** plus approved source materials. No playbook defines what that profile contains. It's the per-client record every draft is meant to read from.
- **Source-integrity labels:**
  - Documented Client Fact
  - Voice of Customer
  - Strategic Interpretation
  - Platform (Ads) Data
  - WIA Benchmark

  Never invent awards, pricing, service areas, reviews, results or differentiators. If something is missing, flag it.
- **Recommendation format:** **Problem → Evidence → (Funnel Stage →) Likely Cause → Recommended Action → Metric to Monitor.**
- **Consistent stance:** don't over-optimise; treat Google and Meta recommendations (and their reps) as suggestions; judge results by qualified leads and bookings, not platform metrics.

## What this means for the three streams
- **Paid Media Reporting (PM-01/02/03).** The benchmark table above is the ready-made set of thresholds for a weekly green/amber/red watch. The recommendation format is a ready-made structure for the monthly executive summary. The playbooks also list what to report (Google §43) and in what order to diagnose (Google §44, Meta §33). **This beats Viktor on WIA's own terms:** WIA's documented thresholds instead of a generic model's.
- **Knowledge base (FF-01).** The "Client Marketing Profile" is what the knowledge base should *produce*. It's the per-client record all three playbooks consume. The five source-integrity labels are a ready-made provenance tag for every fact in it. They don't settle the open conflict rule (an approved document vs a later call), but they give a vocabulary for it.
- **Content engine and any drafted copy.** The instructions' **banned-pattern list** can be checked automatically on every draft before a human sees it (em dashes, "It's not X. It's Y.", "Imagine…", "Look no further" and so on). The Landing Page playbook's copy standards (§19) add more. *One small inconsistency:* the instructions ban em dashes outright, while the Landing Page playbook allows them "if genuinely consistent with the client's voice." For WIA's own marketing, Whitney's founder-voice doc should decide.
- **Scope note.** These playbooks are for **client** work (ads, landing pages). The content engine writes **WIA's own** marketing, so its voice source is Whitney's founder-voice doc and the consultant strategy doc (M04), not these.
