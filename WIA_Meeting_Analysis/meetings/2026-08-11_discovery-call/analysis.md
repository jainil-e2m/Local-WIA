## TL;DR
This was the first sales call after Whitney attended Vistara, E2M's AI event. WIA's real problem is **key-person risk, not hours**. Their account manager, Fallon (joined in May), just resigned. This has happened before, and every departure puts Whitney back on client accounts. Khushbu first pitched the $9,499 Apollo plan and proposed **skipping the audit**, because WIA "already has processes". Whitney said it still felt "very ambiguous" and asked how much time they would get back in three months. Nobody could answer that. So Khushbu switched, in the same call, to recommending the lower-priced **Audit & Blueprint**, with its price credited against any later Apollo plan. WIA left leaning towards the audit and promised a decision by the end of the week. Per the deal-state memory, they bought the audit.

## Decisions
- **Phase 1 target = Gisselle's role.** Replicate what the EA does, keep a human in the loop, and add a dashboard plus a "brain" layer. Both founders agreed (16:00–20:07).
- **Process knowledge comes straight from Gisselle** through 1:1 calls, so the founders don't have to be interviewed. Whitney: "I love the idea" (20:07).
- **Client experience stays human.** Daniela set this as a requirement: wedding clients hire WIA for "hand-holding and support" (13:02).
- **Phase 2 is marketing, sales and bookkeeping, and it needs its own audit** (Daniela, 18:25). These areas are *not* well defined, unlike fulfilment.
- **The entry point moved from Apollo-direct to Audit & Blueprint** (34:54). WIA called the audit "a great starting point" (45:43). The final decision was deferred to their internal meeting the next day.

## E2M performance
**What landed**
- Khushbu said WIA's documented processes mean the architecture already exists. That flattered them and matched Daniela's point that execution, not ops design, is the gap.
- Taking the process from Gisselle directly lowered the burden on the founders, and Whitney liked it.
- **The pivot to the audit (34:22–34:54) was the key move.** Khushbu named the real objection (unknown time and accuracy) before the client did, then offered a lower-risk step with the fee credited towards Apollo.
- The "custom isn't always the answer" story (a Claude Teams build for another agency) directly answered Whitney's worry about being inundated.
- Being open about running costs ($20–30 hosting, <$150 a month API across E2M's 400 clients) answered a question Whitney asked outright.

**What didn't**
- **The pitch started with product, not with WIA.** Agency OS, 17 agents, credits, the Hub, and an internal ads dashboard: roughly six minutes of catalogue before any link to Gisselle's actual tasks. The result was "very ambiguous".
- **Proposing to skip the audit was premature.** It rested on "they have ClickUp boards and templates". The later calls show the boards aren't how the team actually works. The same-call reversal was a recovery, not the plan.
- **Daniela's fulfilment and reporting question went unanswered by E2M.** It was the most important product signal on the call.
- **Mixed messages on the Hub:** it's included, but "the priority is not" to customise it. And the ads dashboard was shown as a phase-2 idea during a phase-1 pitch.
- **Viktor was dismissed** ("why would you want to pay for Viktor?") instead of being taken on board. Whitney later trialled it anyway.
- Assumptions were made without being checked: that WIA's PM tool is where E2M should post daily updates, and that WIA has an AWS account.
