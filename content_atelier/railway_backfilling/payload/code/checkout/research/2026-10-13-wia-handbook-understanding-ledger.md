## The editorial objective and audience

### H001 Select a valuable idea before developing copy

**Basis:** Handbook guidance.

**Evidence:** B003, B009–B011, B055–B058, B195–B199, B208.

**Understanding:** The system must explain why a conversation deserves to become WIA content. Relevance and accurate summarization alone are insufficient.

**Reasoning:** A polished brief can still leave Whitney supplying the missing strategic idea. The unit of success is a supported point a relevant prospect would value, not a count of completed fields or attractive hooks.

**Build implication:** Separate opportunity discovery and selection from briefs and finished copy. Review central points first.

**Check:** Can a reviewer identify the audience significance without relying on the hook or rewriting the central point?

### H002 Keep the exchange the reasoning and the wider significance connected

**Basis:** Handbook guidance.

**Evidence:** B010, B015–B016, B024, B056–B057, B067–B071.

**Understanding:** The source supplies a concrete exchange; the analyst identifies the decision or reasoning it reveals; the angle expresses its broader significance for a defensible audience.

**Reasoning:** “Messaging matters” loses the distinctive scene. “Never say no posing” misses the broader collaborative work. Zooming out should reveal meaning while retaining the example that makes it credible.

**Build implication:** Require a traceable chain from source exchange to demonstrated reasoning to central point and intended shift.

**Check:** The angle is neither a transcript paraphrase nor a generic principle that could have been generated without the source.

### H003 Serve awareness trust and offer understanding over time

**Basis:** Handbook guidance.

**Evidence:** B011, B022–B024, B039–B045, B053.

**Understanding:** Content should reach relevant people, build confidence in WIA's judgment, and make appropriate offers understandable. Education, supported opinions, founder perspective, process, and proof all have a place.

**Reasoning:** Commercial value can accumulate through trust and changed understanding. Requiring a sales argument in every item would narrow discovery and make useful founder content formulaic.

**Build implication:** Give each direction a clear primary audience effect; allow an absent offer connection or immediate buying action.

**Check:** The selection reason explains a marketing purpose without appending a product name as justification.

### H004 Keep the knowledge prompt examples and feedback separately maintainable

**Basis:** Handbook guidance.

**Evidence:** B002–B008, B052, B100, B164, B187–B194, B203–B205.

**Understanding:** The handbook consolidates reference knowledge, angle decisions, examples, application requirements, and references to separate prompt/schema/test assets. New examples and thresholds remain proposals.

**Reasoning:** Facts, preferences, approved decisions, and suggestions change under different authority. A single permanent prompt blob makes stale offer details and model-written examples look equally authoritative.

**Build implication:** Version distinct knowledge collections and record provenance, scope, status, and effective date. Treat the seven-file inventory as a proposed package composition, not proof that all seven files were delivered.

**Check:** A proposed framing cannot be retrieved as “Whitney-approved wording,” and changing an offer record does not require rewriting examples.

### H005 Respect experienced wedding business owners

**Basis:** Handbook guidance.

**Evidence:** B017–B019, B047, B079.R02, B079.R05.

**Understanding:** The audience includes venues, photographers, planners, videographers, and other wedding professionals, many of whom already understand marketing and their strengths. Possible situations include inconsistent inquiries, uncertain booking attribution, unclear campaign strategy, poorly communicated strengths, and limited follow-up capacity. These are segments, not universal assumptions.

**Reasoning:** Beginner assumptions can make technically correct education feel irrelevant or patronizing. The audience needs a useful decision, perspective, or explanation at an appropriate level of depth.

**Build implication:** Model audience situations as possible segments, not universal deficits. Specify what an experienced owner gains from the point.

**Check:** The angle does not assume every reader lacks leads, marketing knowledge, or an articulated differentiator.

### H006 Choose the broadest audience that preserves the insight

**Basis:** Handbook guidance.

**Evidence:** B020, B070, B085.R04.

**Understanding:** The recording participant and content audience are different fields. Some examples transfer across wedding services; others properly remain venue-specific or technical.

**Reasoning:** Automatically copying the participant's profile narrows useful process content. Automatically broadening to “all wedding pros” erases constraints that make a specialized point true.

**Build implication:** Store source participant separately and explain why the central point applies to the proposed audience.

**Check:** Broader relevance follows from transferable reasoning, not from transferring a particular client's results to others.

### H007 Treat wedding buying as distinctive without inventing universal psychology

**Basis:** Handbook guidance.

**Evidence:** B021, B038, B152, B190.

**Understanding:** Couples commonly make a meaningful, wedding-specific choice, compare options, imagine an experience, and seek confidence. Decision windows and routes vary.

**Reasoning:** These characteristics explain why relevance and trust matter, but do not establish fixed research durations, a mandatory sequence, or scientific behavioral laws.

**Build implication:** Retain service and situation qualifiers in buyer reasoning. Use decision-stage examples as illustrations where supported.

**Check:** The system does not claim every couple spends months researching or must pass through three campaign stages.

### H008 Recognize founder thinking and team expertise accurately

**Basis:** Handbook guidance.

**Evidence:** B014–B016, B048, B094.

**Understanding:** The handbook describes Whitney's 12-year wedding/events background and broader strategic thinking. Other team members' recommendations can also demonstrate WIA's expertise.

**Reasoning:** Founder content is a perspective, not a requirement that Whitney personally performed every action. Accurate attribution makes collaborative work credible.

**Build implication:** Store the contributing speaker, organizational attribution, and permitted narrator separately. Use “I” only for Whitney's actual experience or perspective.

**Check:** A Carmen or Danielle recommendation is not converted into “I told my client” in Whitney's voice.

## WIA beliefs offers and boundaries

### H009 Advertising starts with business and buyer understanding

**Basis:** Handbook guidance.

**Evidence:** B031–B033, B190.

**Understanding:** Advertising strategy begins with intended couples, their values, positioning, and the reasons a business is a meaningful choice. The message and destination matter alongside platform execution.

**Reasoning:** An ordinary copy or imagery discussion can reveal consequential strategic work. The content should show the specific relationship involved, rather than repeat a checklist of every marketing foundation.

**Build implication:** Look for the decision connecting buyer understanding to a campaign or page choice. Keep foundational help within WIA's role.

**Check:** “Ads amplify businesses” does not become an instruction that prospects must solve all foundational issues alone before seeking help.

### H010 A communication gap does not mean the owner lacks a differentiator

**Basis:** Handbook guidance.

**Evidence:** B034, B165.R02–B165.R03.

**Understanding:** Owners may clearly describe their strengths in conversation while their marketing fails to convey those strengths to couples.

**Reasoning:** Misdiagnosing the owner's knowledge changes both the message and service promise. WIA helps develop and translate clarity, rather than requiring a finished UVP as admission criteria.

**Build implication:** Separate “business knows its value” from “marketing communicates that value.” Preserve client insight as a contribution.

**Check:** The system does not frame WIA as rescuing an ignorant owner or send a prospect away to resolve positioning independently.

### H011 Collaboration is evidence of expertise

**Basis:** Handbook guidance.

**Evidence:** B035, B060, B068, B126–B130.

**Understanding:** WIA's technical expertise and the client's knowledge of buyers, service, and market inform the same decision. Useful questions can be the main content opportunity.

**Reasoning:** Agency value does not require originating every insight. Asking the question that changes or bounds a recommendation shows judgment and makes the experience of working together tangible.

**Build implication:** Identify what each participant contributed and the decision that followed. Extract question–answer–reasoning sequences.

**Check:** A process angle includes WIA's actual question or response and credits the client knowledge it uses.

### H012 Keep business metrics and advertising attribution distinct

**Basis:** Handbook guidance.

**Evidence:** B036, B095, B131, B165.R07.

**Understanding:** Clicks, inquiries, booked consultations, bookings, revenue, and ad-attributed revenue are different measures.

**Reasoning:** A compelling business improvement does not automatically identify its cause. Conflating metrics creates false proof even when the underlying number is real.

**Build implication:** Capture metric, unit, period, baseline, source, approximation, and attribution alongside any result claim.

**Check:** Total bookings are not relabeled as ad-generated bookings; a recollected inquiry anecdote is not computed into an ad conversion rate.

### H013 Systems support the owner's work rather than remove all participation

**Basis:** Handbook guidance.

**Evidence:** B037, B026.R04, B029.

**Understanding:** Systems may connect inquiry steps and reduce supported manual work while the owner serves clients. Automation does not guarantee sales or eliminate human service work.

**Reasoning:** The value is a concrete workflow and its supported benefit. “Everything runs itself” exaggerates scope and hides owner responsibilities.

**Build implication:** Identify the automated steps, required inputs, owner tasks, handoffs, and any observed workload change.

**Check:** A scheduling or follow-up feature is not turned into a guaranteed booking outcome or fully outsourced sales function.

### H014 Fit recommendations to the particular business

**Basis:** Handbook guidance.

**Evidence:** B038, B044, B165.R05–B165.R06, B190.

**Understanding:** Goals, budget, stage, offer, and audience shape strategy. Advertising hesitation and broader founder observations deserve context rather than blanket judgments.

**Reasoning:** A useful case illustrates how WIA thinks; it does not establish a universal campaign structure or prove hesitant owners have a poor mindset. Founder and cultural observations can be relevant without being forced into ad-platform advice.

**Build implication:** Preserve the factors behind the choice and distinguish interpretation from verified public facts.

**Check:** The system avoids universal prescriptions, invented public speculation, and claims that all advertising is frustrating or all hesitation reflects mindset.

### H015 ELEVATE makes ongoing strategic work tangible

**Basis:** Handbook offer description.

**Evidence:** B026.R02, B028–B030.

**Understanding:** The handbook describes ELEVATE as an ongoing done-for-you advertising partnership, potentially encompassing planning, Google/Meta management, creative, advertising pages, tracking, and LeadLock follow-up.

**Reasoning:** These work steps can support solution-selling content. Their presence in a reference does not prove each is included in every client's contract or that historical delivery is current.

**Build implication:** Link a demonstrated task to a dated verified offer record and actual contracted scope before claiming delivery.

**Check:** No guarantee of bookings, complete business management, or universal contracted inclusion is inferred.

### H016 Ads Accelerators are guided implementation

**Basis:** Handbook offer description.

**Evidence:** B026.R03, B030, B165.R03.

**Understanding:** The handbook describes three-month guided Google Search, Meta, or combined implementation paths, including positioning, initial development, support, and interpreting data.

**Reasoning:** Reducing the offer to a video course hides the hands-on value; claiming current terms from a past sales call ignores freshness. Guidance can help clients develop clarity rather than require it beforehand.

**Build implication:** Represent support and implementation separately from curriculum and verify current duration, inclusions, and availability before publishing specifics.

**Check:** Neither “self-serve course” nor “you must already have solved your UVP” is used as the default explanation.

### H017 LeadLock knowledge and specific demo evidence have different roles

**Basis:** Handbook offer description.

**Evidence:** B026.R04, B028, B155–B162.

**Understanding:** LeadLock is described as an inquiry-management/follow-up platform, with connected forms, tracking, pipelines, email/SMS automation, and supported scheduling. It is generally required for ELEVATE, with rare exceptions.

**Reasoning:** Product knowledge can support a general demo, but cannot establish which feature impressed a particular prospect. A feature, configured workflow, and outcome are distinct claims.

**Build implication:** Version capabilities and configuration evidence separately. Retrieve reaction-linked demo turns before attaching a prospect endorsement.

**Check:** Historical exceptions are not promised as the normal offer, and enthusiasm is not treated as proof of purchase or results.

### H018 Respect service scope around branding and sales

**Basis:** Handbook guidance.

**Evidence:** B014, B029, B026.R02–B026.R04.

**Understanding:** Positioning work can inform a designer; WIA is not thereby a visual-branding agency directing logos, fonts, and colors. Advertising does not establish full-service sales management.

**Reasoning:** Broader strategic framing should expand the significance of demonstrated work, not expand the services the business promises.

**Build implication:** Include an offer-scope check when central points or drafts imply who owns a downstream task.

**Check:** A page-messaging example is not sold as a full visual identity service or guaranteed click-to-booking delivery.

### H019 Keep changing facts in dated records

**Basis:** Handbook guidance.

**Evidence:** B030, B052, B167, B188–B190, B205.R05–B205.R06.

**Understanding:** Prices, availability, policies, team information, program/campaign specifications, numerical results, and public events need appropriate current verification. The handbook deliberately omits prices.

**Reasoning:** Historical examples can demonstrate editorial judgment while carrying stale facts. Recency of the handbook alone does not independently verify every offer statement.

**Build implication:** Record verified date, applicable scope, status, and superseding record. Gate publication-specific details on the latest applicable source.

**Check:** Historical pricing and staff details are not silently copied into current marketing.

## Strategic purpose and taste

### H020 Keep Savannah strategy distinct from founder expression

**Basis:** Handbook guidance.

**Evidence:** B039–B040, B045–B047.

**Understanding:** Savannah's strategy governs purpose, packaging, distribution, and CTAs. Whitney's founder voice governs expression.

**Reasoning:** A recurring founder theme is not a new pillar. A voice preference cannot replace the marketing job of the content.

**Build implication:** Retrieve strategy and voice as separate inputs with separate ownership and versioning.

**Check:** The application retains ATTRACT/WARM/CONVERT and does not invent additional pillars from recurring beliefs.

### H021 Assign one primary pillar by audience effect

**Basis:** Handbook guidance.

**Evidence:** B041.R02–B041.R04, B043, B069.

**Understanding:** ATTRACT creates relevant attention; WARM builds trust and understanding; CONVERT clarifies offer value and fit. Content type is a separate dimension.

**Reasoning:** The same process can build trust or make an offer concrete, depending on the intended point. Listing every pillar evades the editorial choice.

**Build implication:** Require one primary pillar, an established content type, and a purpose-specific rationale.

**Check:** A process example classified as CONVERT actually explains useful delivery or fit rather than merely mentioning WIA.

### H022 Discover alternatives without quotas or cosmetic relabeling

**Basis:** Handbook guidance.

**Evidence:** B044, B066, B071, B151–B152, B182, B186.

**Understanding:** One recording may support several distinct directions, or none. Multiple directions must differ in central point or intended shift. Fixed pillar percentages and weak output quotas are not authorized.

**Reasoning:** Three rewritten titles do not demonstrate broader discovery. Forcing a mix encourages invented opinions, trends, or proof.

**Build implication:** Group related source treatments, compare their audience effect, and return fewer selections with gaps when necessary.

**Check:** Changing the hook, client name, or pillar label alone cannot count as a new angle.

### H023 Use Savannah's sequence after selecting the idea

**Basis:** Handbook guidance.

**Evidence:** B045, B180.

**Understanding:** Finished content follows Hook → Problems, Desires, or Symptoms → Main Point → Transformation → CTA, adapted to purpose and format.

**Reasoning:** Transformation can be changed understanding when results are unavailable. This is a drafting structure, not a requirement to invent a before-and-after result during discovery.

**Build implication:** Keep this structure in format-specific development; carry evidence limits forward.

**Check:** A process story can describe a useful thought shift without fabricating increased bookings.

### H024 Earn the CTA and give captions an additional job

**Basis:** Handbook guidance.

**Evidence:** B053, B165.R04.

**Understanding:** CTAs follow the idea and current campaign objective. A reel caption should add context, an implication, or a fitting offer connection rather than repeat the reel.

**Reasoning:** Default ELEVATE/LeadLock/“DM ADS” endings make otherwise useful content repetitive and detach the response from its purpose.

**Build implication:** Leave CTA optional at discovery and select it during development using the current plan. Track caption contribution separately.

**Check:** The CTA is justified by this piece, and the caption contributes something beyond a paraphrase.

### H025 Confidence should come from reasoning and specificity

**Basis:** Handbook guidance.

**Evidence:** B016, B047, B079.R06, B096–B097.

**Understanding:** Desired expression is calm, warm, curious, clear, specific, and respectful. The reader should understand the reasoning behind the conclusion.

**Reasoning:** Confidence does not require provocation or certainty about unmeasured outcomes. Source notes can bound facts while the perspective itself remains clear.

**Build implication:** Evaluate framing for understandable significance and natural reasoning, not just punchiness. Keep factual qualifications attached to evidence.

**Check:** The angle feels useful to an experienced owner without academic phrasing, hype, or a hesitant disclaimer in every sentence.

### H026 Match first person to the actual source of experience

**Basis:** Handbook guidance.

**Evidence:** B048, B094.

**Understanding:** “We/our” describes WIA's work; “I” describes Whitney's personal experience or perspective when she is the source.

**Reasoning:** Writing in a founder voice can falsely make another team member's work autobiographical.

**Build implication:** Preserve attribution through narrator selection and drafting, rather than applying a universal first-person style rewrite.

**Check:** Organizational storytelling does not invent Whitney's participation in a call.

### H027 Vary openings without manufacturing drama

**Basis:** Handbook guidance.

**Evidence:** B016, B049, B165.R06.

**Understanding:** Observations, questions, supported opinions, verified results, and relevant industry moments can open content. Surprise and controversy are optional.

**Reasoning:** Always opening with a client conversation is another formula. Copying transcript fillers also confuses evidence fidelity with finished voice.

**Build implication:** Preserve verbatim dialogue in evidence while allowing varied, source-faithful public expression.

**Check:** The batch does not rely on identical openings or add unsupported cultural speculation for attention.

### H028 Reject formulaic hype and academic framing

**Basis:** Handbook guidance.

**Evidence:** B050.R02–B050.R07, B052, B165.R05.

**Understanding:** Avoid default “not X, Y” reversals, secrets, growth-hack language, generic lead-struggle openings, manufactured consensus, sweeping agency criticism, academic titles, em dashes, and forced fragments.

**Reasoning:** These constructions can disguise a weak point or exaggerate authority. The preferred move is to state the concrete observation and its significance naturally.

**Build implication:** Use this as framing review after strategic selection. Do not reduce taste to a banned-word filter or force every ad-investment story into frustration.

**Check:** Removing a cliché still leaves a substantive, supported idea with a real audience reason.

### H029 Use precedence without erasing the history

**Basis:** Handbook guidance.

**Evidence:** B052, B188–B190, B193–B194.

**Understanding:** The handbook's precedence is latest explicit Whitney instruction, current verified strategy/offer records, established guidance, historical approved examples, then model suggestions.

**Reasoning:** Historical approval is contextual; later explicit corrections may reject old constructions. A rejection of one technical angle is not a ban on technical education.

**Build implication:** Keep superseded records and case-specific scope. Apply a reusable rule only when feedback establishes one.

**Check:** A dated correction wins over conflicting historical wording, without turning one case into an invented global preference.

## Discovery selection and briefs

### H030 Understand the recording before selecting sentences

**Basis:** Handbook guidance.

**Evidence:** B059–B060, B068, B174.

**Understanding:** Identify call purpose, business, goal, decision, contributors, and whether the material describes proposed work, completed action, or reported results.

**Reasoning:** A sentence can be accurate yet mislead when removed from the decision it belongs to. Context often contains the team's actual reasoning.

**Build implication:** Produce a recording-context record and extract linked exchanges with enough surrounding turns to preserve meaning.

**Check:** A proposed workflow is not described as implemented, and material speaker ambiguity is resolved or held.

### H031 Inspect all five handbook lenses

**Basis:** Handbook guidance.

**Evidence:** B063–B066, B175.

**Understanding:** The lenses are WIA expertise, process/collaboration, offer value, audience insight, and proof/transformation. They guide inspection, not output quotas.

**Reasoning:** Different source moments have different uses. Client-only reactions can be valuable intelligence; verified changes in understanding can support a story without numerical results.

**Build implication:** Allow multiple lenses per exchange and internal findings alongside public candidates. Preserve proof-specific metric/attribution checks.

**Check:** The pipeline finds supported useful material outside straightforward educational explanations and does not force a result lens onto every call.

### H032 Retrieve WIA's contribution before claiming a process demonstration

**Basis:** Handbook guidance.

**Evidence:** B068, B094, B116–B120.

**Understanding:** State the client's problem, WIA's observed contribution, and the relevance to a prospect. A client-only excerpt requires retrieval before claiming WIA performed the fuller process.

**Reasoning:** Knowing WIA offers messaging help cannot fill a missing response in a particular recording. Audience research remains useful even when agency contribution is absent.

**Build implication:** Mark contribution as observed, missing, or unnecessary for the particular claim; request the precise surrounding segment when needed.

**Check:** General brand knowledge is not used to fabricate what WIA did in this exchange.

### H033 Develop one defensible central point and audience shift

**Basis:** Handbook guidance.

**Evidence:** B069–B071, B085.R02–B085.R04, B089.R02–B089.R05.

**Understanding:** A candidate has one point, a relevant audience, and something that audience should understand, believe, or do afterward.

**Reasoning:** Intended shift is more flexible than an immediate decision and more useful than a generic promise to “build authority.” It distinguishes adjacent treatments from duplicates.

**Build implication:** Make intended shift explicit; merge argument and take into central point. Attach offers only where they follow from the idea.

**Check:** The intended shift can be expressed specifically without requiring a buying action or manufactured tension.

### H034 Give every useful finding an explicit disposition

**Basis:** Handbook guidance.

**Evidence:** B072–B075, B177, B179.

**Understanding:** SELECT develops a supported worthwhile point; REFRAME redirects useful material; HOLD names a material evidence gap; INTERNAL retains research/service value; SKIP explains insufficient opportunity.

**Reasoning:** A single call can yield different dispositions. Product enthusiasm may merit a retrieval hold while a price objection remains internal. Keeping objections is part of honest learning.

**Build implication:** Return dispositions with reasons and relevant next actions, rather than only a flat list of public angles.

**Check:** Missing material is visible and actionable, and inconvenient product feedback is neither suppressed nor forced into a public lesson.

### H035 Check eligibility before comparing editorial scores

**Basis:** Handbook guidance.

**Evidence:** B076–B079, B081.

**Understanding:** Invented results, material misattribution, unsupported product claims, and current-instruction contradictions make the candidate ineligible until corrected. The proposed rubric has six dimensions.

**Reasoning:** Relevance cannot offset an unsupported central claim. Evidence strength and editorial appeal answer different questions.

**Build implication:** Apply a factual/instruction gate first, then score audience relevance, strategic value/brand fit, distinctiveness, appropriate depth, voice/framing, and source support.

**Check:** A high average cannot admit an invented endorsement or result.

### H036 Treat scoring thresholds as calibration proposals

**Basis:** Proposed calibration policy.

**Evidence:** B004, B078–B081, B199.

**Understanding:** The handbook proposes 1/3/5 ordinal anchors, intermediate 2/4, prioritization at least 4 on audience/strategy/distinctiveness, revision for any 1/2, and HOLD for material unsupported claims.

**Reasoning:** These are judgments, not approval probabilities or marketing forecasts. The source expressly leaves calibration open; there is no defensible invented approval-rate target yet.

**Build implication:** Preserve individual dimensions, anchor-based reasons, and policy version. Calibrate against actual founder reviews before treating thresholds as final automation.

**Check:** A score of 4 is not shown as “80% likely to work,” and a material evidence gap is not averaged away.

### H037 Explain why a selection beats its nearest alternative

**Basis:** Handbook guidance.

**Evidence:** B082, B178–B182, B208.

**Understanding:** Ranking should include a concise reason and a nearest rejected or deferred alternative, considering current plan and recent work.

**Reasoning:** Scores alone conceal the judgment Whitney needs to review. A strong direction may lose because an equivalent point was recently published.

**Build implication:** Store alternative central point, comparison reason, and deferral cause. Present concise rationale, not private step-by-step deliberation.

**Check:** Whitney can understand the choice without reconstructing the whole discovery process or seeing only identical self-scores.

### H038 A selected brief needs development direction as well as evidence

**Basis:** Handbook guidance.

**Evidence:** B083–B085, B091, B202.

**Understanding:** Selected angles need working title/point, why worthwhile, audience/shift, pillar/type, source demonstration, source/claim limits, and how to make the point tangible.

**Reasoning:** These fields support a selected idea rather than establish its eligibility. Development direction should explain the example or sequence to show, without prematurely generating a hook or script.

**Build implication:** Use a concise selected-angle contract with an expandable evidence/development section.

**Check:** A writer can develop the point without guessing what example substantiates it or inventing missing delivery steps.

### H039 Tension offer connection and immediate decision are optional

**Basis:** Handbook guidance.

**Evidence:** B087–B091.

**Understanding:** Real tension, offer connection, format, and secondary direction are optional. Argument/take merge; mechanism becomes source demonstration and development logic; reader decision broadens to intended shift.

**Reasoning:** A process story can earn attention through useful visible work without conflict. Mandatory tension pressures the model to invent a problem; mandatory buying action suppresses founder perspective and trust content.

**Build implication:** Allow explicit null optional fields; require a meaningful intended shift and source demonstration instead of filler.

**Check:** A worthwhile process angle remains valid with no tension, offer, CTA, or immediate buying decision.

## Evidence integrity and public use

### H040 Separate speech action interpretation and outcome

**Basis:** Handbook guidance.

**Evidence:** B060, B092–B093, B097.

**Understanding:** A recommendation proves reasoning was expressed, not implementation. A prospect reaction is audience evidence, not a measured outcome. Interpretation should be identified as such.

**Reasoning:** Process content needs no fabricated success story to justify it. Claims become misleading when a proposed next step is narrated as a completed result.

**Build implication:** Attach an evidence state and supported scope to each material claim, not just one blanket disclaimer for an angle.

**Check:** “Suggested adding a comparison” never silently becomes “added it and improved conversion.”

### H041 Preserve measurement qualifications

**Basis:** Handbook guidance.

**Evidence:** B095, B126, B131, B141, B165.R07.

**Understanding:** Keep units, period, baseline, approximation, and attribution. Do not compute causal advertising metrics from untracked recollections or ambiguous prices.

**Reasoning:** Precision added by the model can be less truthful than approximate source language. A numerical calculation does not improve an anecdote's attribution.

**Build implication:** Keep verbatim numeric context and require verified supporting records for derived claims.

**Check:** “Approximately one of four or five inquiries” stays an anecdote, not “a 20–25% ad conversion rate.”

### H042 Credit the source of words artifacts and decisions

**Basis:** Handbook guidance.

**Evidence:** B094, B116–B120, B136–B141.

**Understanding:** Client phrases, existing comparison charts, and insights remain client contributions. Team recommendations remain attributed to the actual speaker.

**Reasoning:** Miscrediting makes the agency appear more inventive while erasing the collaborative process the client wants showcased.

**Build implication:** Track proposer, owner of an existing artifact, and WIA's later recommendation separately.

**Check:** “Authentic, not staged” and Lauren's chart are not credited as WIA inventions.

### H043 Keep material ambiguity local and retrievable

**Basis:** Handbook guidance.

**Evidence:** B062, B096–B097, B141, B200.

**Understanding:** Retrieve original audio or a verified record for ambiguous numbers or phrases that affect the point. Keep uncertainty in evidence notes rather than making every public sentence tentative.

**Reasoning:** Confident reasoning and truthful boundaries can coexist. Guessing a plausible correction corrupts the evidence; excessive caution in the title does not resolve missing facts.

**Build implication:** Store the exact ambiguous span, why it matters, required evidence, and whether a narrower claim can proceed.

**Check:** An ambiguous “$30” is flagged, not expanded into a package price or calculated savings.

### H044 Use real recording locators and do not invent dates

**Basis:** Handbook guidance.

**Evidence:** B061, B101, B105, B115, B125, B135, B145, B156.

**Understanding:** Keep actual call ID/URL when supplied, filename, speakers, and elapsed timestamps. A shared batch label cannot uniquely identify a recording or establish its year.

**Reasoning:** Traceability depends on finding the actual dialogue. A polished date label or invented Zoom URL creates apparent precision without retrievability.

### H045 Editorial readiness is distinct from public permission

**Basis:** Handbook guidance.

## What this taste profile means in practice

The handbook supplies these established pillar/type relationships. These are editorial options, not quotas or a publishing schedule.

| Primary pillar | Intended purpose | Established content types |
|---|---|---|
| ATTRACT | Relevant new audience and interest | Polarizing opinions, founder-forward perspectives, campaign breakdowns, trends |
| WARM | Trust and deeper understanding | UVP, thought shifts, processes, behind the scenes, education, founder story |
| CONVERT | Offer value and fit | Solution selling, social proof |

The handbook's preferred content combines a recognizable wedding-business situation with a consequential question or decision. It respects the owner's expertise and shows enough of WIA's reasoning to make that expertise visible. The audience can leave with a clearer belief, a better understanding of the team's work, or a more tangible view of an offer. The point earns its attention through significance and specificity; it need not be surprising, adversarial, educational in format, or explicitly promotional.

The preferred depth is the decision layer. A small exchange can reveal how campaign focus, a buyer-facing phrase, or a keyword recommendation was shaped. The broader point should remain attached to that particular example. WIA's contribution may be a diagnostic question, a constraint it recognizes, a distinction it explains, or a recommendation it adapts after client input. This makes collaborative process valuable even when the client supplied the original phrase or artifact.

A plausible attractive headline is insufficient. “Your portfolio is costing you inquiries” has audience stakes, but its loss claim exceeds the imagery excerpt. The handbook specifically rejects that move without added evidence. Stronger editorial judgment means finding a worthwhile supported point, not strengthening unsupported consequences. Conversely, lack of measured results does not make process content weak: a recommendation can show judgment without becoming proof of improved performance.

The business agenda is qualified demand over time. Some ideas help unfamiliar owners pay attention; others deepen trust; others clarify what a service delivers and whom it fits. A batch of WARM process directions can be legitimate when the available evidence and plan support it. Repeated all-WARM output is a diagnostic question about discovery and inputs, not automatic permission to manufacture ATTRACT opinions or CONVERT proof.

## Recommended contract for the rebuild

This is a derived implementation design, not the missing companion schema from the handbook.

| Record | Fields needed | Why it exists |
|---|---|---|
| Knowledge record | ID, kind, text, source/version, date, scope, authority, verification/approval status, superseded-by | Prevent facts, preferences, and suggestions from being conflated |
| Recording context | Source ID/file, known call ID/URL, supplied date, call purpose, business/goal, participants, coverage extent, ambiguity | Preserve meaning and truth about source coverage |
| Evidence exchange | Turn/span IDs, speaker, elapsed time, verbatim text, surrounding context, contributions, claim state | Support each point with retrievable dialogue |
| Discovery finding | Situation, demonstrated decision/reasoning, broader significance, lenses, source references, possible public/internal uses, exact retrieval need | Search beyond topics before developing a brief |
| Candidate | Central point, intended audience/shift, primary pillar/type, selection reason, disposition/reason, source demonstration, development direction, claim limits | Make editorial judgment visible |
| Optional treatment | Genuine tension, natural offer connection, possible format, distinct secondary direction | Avoid required filler and invented conflict |
| Ranking comparison | Eligibility checks, six dimensional judgments/reasons, nearest alternative, batch/history comparison, plan fit, policy version | Explain why one direction is preferred |
| Human decision | Original point, exact feedback, disposition, revised point, correction category/scope, direction/wording/publication approvals | Learn the actual reason a founder changed the idea |
| Published history | Point, audience shift, process shown, example, pillar/type, offer, hook approach, date, source family | Assess repetition at the level of meaning |

A candidate's editorial disposition should be independent of factual verification and human approval. For example, a supported SELECT can still await public naming permission. A HOLD can be strategically strong but lack material source evidence. INTERNAL is a useful destination, not a low-quality version of SELECT. REFRAME should preserve the useful source while changing the point. SKIP should give a short reason, without filling the gap with an invented angle.

The six proposed scoring dimensions have these handbook anchors:

| Criterion | 1 | 3 | 5 |
|---|---|---|---|
| Audience relevance | Mainly the participant's concern | Recognizable segment issue | Clear reason the intended prospect cares |
| Strategic value and brand fit | Generic or misdirected | Pillar supported, WIA value weak | Audience effect and positioning advanced clearly |
| Distinctiveness | Familiar broad lesson | Some specific perspective | Fresh concrete significance and selection reason |
| Appropriate depth | Too technical, shallow, or overloaded | Usable with adjustment | Clear point and sufficient reasoning for an experienced owner |
| Voice and framing | Academic, hype, insulting, or formulaic | Clear but generic | Thoughtful, specific, confident, natural |
| Source support | Central claim absent or contradicted | Partial with precise retrieval need | Supports the proposed claim at its scope |

The initial threshold proposal should remain configurable. Source support of 3 with a material missing segment belongs in HOLD, regardless of excellent relevance or distinctiveness. A supported narrow claim can be developed separately from a broader held claim; the model must state that distinction rather than attach a disclaimer to an unsupported selected point.

## How the two LLM stages should change

**Moment identification:** give the model curated strategy, actual source context, known identities, and coverage constraints. Ask it to identify coherent exchanges across the five handbook lenses, record each participant's contribution, distinguish proposed/completed/reported material, and explain the broader significance. Keep internal research and retrieval holds. Do not require a title, immediate audience decision, conflict, or one moment per call. The extraction should preserve exact dialogue and reference IDs; an interpretation belongs in a separate field.

**Candidate generation and selection:** supply validated exchanges with surrounding context, current offer/plan records, relevant calibration contrasts, and actual recent history. Develop one or more meaningfully different points from strong evidence families. Check claims and current instructions before ranking. Require intended shift, disposition, source demonstration, development direction, and a concise reason against the nearest alternative. Make tension and offer connection optional. Return selected angles separately from holds, reframes, internal insights, and skips; disclose coverage/history limits and source-dependent pillar gaps.

**After selection:** draft chosen content using Savannah's sequence and Whitney's expression rules. Carry attribution and factual boundaries forward. Select CTAs from the actual campaign objective. Founder feedback should update the right knowledge record, not automatically rewrite the entire prompt or promote an illustrative hook into a permanent formula.
