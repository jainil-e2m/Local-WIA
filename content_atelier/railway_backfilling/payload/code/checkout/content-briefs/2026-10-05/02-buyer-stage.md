## Strategic assignment

**Audience:** Venue owners with established reviews and ad creative who keep recycling the same message across discovery and retargeting.

**Objective:** Help owners map a message to the question a couple needs answered at that stage.

**Funnel & pillar:** Warm · Lightbulb Moments · Buyer psychology

**Reader promise:** Know how to give discovery and consideration messages different jobs, and choose a review that answers a real buyer concern.

**Belief shift:** From “a good testimonial should lead every ad” to “choose the message that helps this audience make its next decision.”

**What makes this angle distinct:** This teaches message relevance across the couple’s decision journey. It does not diagnose landing-page performance or argue that testimonials are ineffective.

**Natural offer fit:** Elevate. The final invitation follows the CTA direction below.

## Core insight

A useful message meets the couple at their next decision. Help them see why the experience fits, then use relevant proof to address the uncertainty that remains.

## Audience pain

You have beautiful photos and glowing reviews, yet every ad sounds similar and you cannot tell which question it answers for a couple.

## Message spine

1. Ask what a couple needs to understand when they first discover the venue.
2. Show a specific experience they can picture themselves in.
3. Introduce a relevant review when it answers a remaining concern about choosing the venue.
4. Map discovery, consideration, and inquiry messages; test the placement rather than assume one opening suits everyone.

## Narrative framework

**Hook:** Begin with a venue using an excellent testimonial repeatedly and ask what a newly discovering couple still needs to understand.

**Problem:** Changing the photograph can leave the underlying message unchanged. Praise the existing proof while exposing the unanswered question.

**Main point:** Let discovery messaging make the experience and fit concrete. Use relevant proof wherever it addresses a meaningful concern; placement remains a test.

**Transformation:** Each message now helps the couple make a next decision rather than hearing the same general praise again.

**CTA:** Ask which question a couple needs answered before booking a venue tour.

## What we will generate

Scope: one founder email, one eight-slide carousel, and three distinct short videos, with supporting captions. The outlines below guide production; they are not a generated content pack.

### Email

A thoughtful distinction between showing that people loved a venue and helping a couple understand why it fits their own day.

**Reader job:** Identify the question their current venue copy leaves unanswered.

**Length:** 350–500 words; one hypothetical venue and two clearly different message jobs.

**Subject-line directions — proposed, not approved copy:**

- What your best review leaves unanswered
- Give each venue message a job
- Can a couple picture their day?

**Opening direction:** Attribute the observation to a team messaging review. Describe the proposed refinement without claiming it has been launched or won. Use a fictional venue for copy demonstrations.

**Outline:**

1. Acknowledge that a strong review is valuable proof.
2. Ask what the couple knows when they first discover the venue and what they still need to imagine.
3. Use an illustrative garden venue to show the experience: a ceremony, a relaxed transition, and a celebration that feels like them; label the scenario hypothetical.
4. Show a review topic that could address a later concern, such as rain-plan support; describe the topic without inventing a customer quotation.
5. Explain that relevance drives placement, and conclude with a one-question copy audit.

**Email CTA:** Ask the reader to reply with the question couples most often ask before booking a tour.

### Carousel

An eight-slide message map: experience, practical clarity, and relevant proof each answer a different question.

**Reader job:** Audit whether discovery and retargeting copy help the couple progress.

**Slide 1 · Hook — Your best review has a job**

Introduce message relevance rather than criticising testimonials.

**Visual direction:** A review-shaped card beside an unanswered buyer question.

**Slide 2 · Problem — New photo. Same answer?**

Repeated copy can leave discovery and warmer audiences with identical information.

**Visual direction:** Two illustrative ad cards with different images and the same generic message.

**Slide 3 · Main point — Discovery: help them picture it**

Show the distinctive experience and who it fits. Avoid an amenities list without context.

**Visual direction:** An illustrative garden-venue scene; no claim about a real client.

**Slide 4 · Main point — Make the experience specific**

Connect one true venue feature to what the day feels like; use a hypothetical example until assets and details are confirmed.

**Visual direction:** Feature → experience → fit, with a short labelled example.

**Slide 5 · Main point — Consideration: answer the concern**

Name a realistic question about the day, logistics, or support instead of assuming more general praise is enough.

**Visual direction:** A buyer-question card such as a rain-plan concern.

**Slide 6 · Main point — Choose proof for its relevance**

Select an approved review that speaks to that concern. If unavailable, show the review-selection prompt rather than fabricate a quote.

**Visual direction:** Concern matched to a proof topic; no invented star rating or testimonial.

**Slide 7 · Transformation — Build a conversation across ads**

Experience, clarity, and proof work together. Test placement instead of treating this as a rigid funnel rule.

**Visual direction:** Three message cards leading toward one inquiry step.

**Slide 8 · CTA — What is the next question?**

Invite a save and a comment about the question couples ask before a tour.

**Visual direction:** One clear question and restrained brand sign-off.

**Caption direction:** Explain that the proposed placement is a test, that reviews can work at different stages, and that the goal is a more useful message. Invite one question from the audience.

### Reel 1 · An amenity is not the whole experience

Turn a generic venue feature into an experience a couple can picture.

**Role:** Reframe the message

**Length:** 45–60 seconds; aim for approximately 95–130 spoken words, then time the read. Captions and production notes are separate from spoken copy.

**Hook direction:** Ask what a couple should imagine when they read the venue’s feature list.

**Spoken argument beats — develop into a script after brief approval:**

1. Choose one hypothetical venue feature.
2. Connect it to a concrete part of the day.
3. Explain why specificity helps the buyer assess fit.

**Visual direction:** Founder with an illustrative feature-to-experience card; approved venue footage is optional.

**Reel CTA:** Ask viewers which experience their venue should be known for.

### Reel 2 · Choose the review that answers the question

Show how a concern changes which testimonial topic is useful.

**Role:** Demonstrate relevant proof

**Length:** 45–60 seconds; aim for approximately 95–130 spoken words, then time the read. Captions and production notes are separate from spoken copy.

**Hook direction:** Start with a couple’s practical concern, rather than a fabricated glowing quote.

**Spoken argument beats — develop into a script after brief approval:**

1. Name an illustrative concern.
2. Describe the kind of approved review that could answer it.
3. Explain that proof should reduce uncertainty, wherever it is placed.

**Visual direction:** Question and proof-topic cards; do not create a fictitious customer quotation.

**Reel CTA:** Invite viewers to check which concern their featured review actually answers.

### Reel 3 · Audit the next message

A simple side-by-side check of discovery and consideration copy.

**Role:** Give a copy audit

**Length:** 45–60 seconds; aim for approximately 95–130 spoken words, then time the read. Captions and production notes are separate from spoken copy.

**Hook direction:** Ask whether the second ad says anything the first one did not.

**Spoken argument beats — develop into a script after brief approval:**

1. Write the question each audience needs answered.
2. Label the job of each current message.
3. Choose one unanswered question for the next copy test.

**Visual direction:** Two fictional message cards and one blank buyer-question prompt.

**Reel CTA:** Invite a save or comment with the next question to answer.
