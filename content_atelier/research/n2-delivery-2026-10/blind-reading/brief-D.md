# I want to know what made a couple book, as well as what they loved afterward

I want to understand what a couple was weighing when they chose your business. Their review can tell us what they loved about the experience. Asking about the booking decision can give us different information to consider when we write your ads.

## WIA perspective

In a team conversation about our own marketing, I brought up the research we ask new ELEVATE clients to share. Reviews give us useful insight into the experience a business delivers. I also want to understand what a couple was weighing when they decided to book. What mattered to them at that point, and what helped them feel ready to choose you? I recommend asking newly booked couples about that decision. Their answers can give us different information to consider when we write your ad copy and landing pages, alongside what we already know from your reviews.

## Supporting example

We were talking as a team about improving our own marketing when I brought up a question from ELEVATE onboarding: what research does a new client have to share with us?

What WIA did: I recommended asking newly booked couples what influenced their decision and considering what we learn when we write ad copy and landing pages. I wanted to hear about the moment they chose the business, as well as the experience they reviewed afterward.

What the client contributed: The information I want from couples is what they were considering before booking and what helped them make their choice.

## Strategic purpose

## Audience

Wedding business owners with strong reviews who already invest in marketing and want their ads to reflect why their best-fit couples chose them.

Their reviews already describe a valuable client experience. Understanding what couples were weighing before booking can give WIA different information to consider in ad messaging.

## Intended shift

- Understand: A review of the experience and an account of the booking decision answer different questions.
- Believe: The reasons couples chose their business are worth understanding alongside what those couples loved afterward.

## Narrative outline

- Hook (observation): A glowing review can leave an important part of the couple's story untold.
- Hook (question): What would you want to ask a couple right after they chose your business?
- Hook (story): A conversation about our own marketing brought me back to a question we ask new clients.
- Problems, desires or symptoms: Strong reviews already give the owner useful evidence of the experience they deliver.; The owner wants advertising that reflects what matters to best-fit couples.; The booking decision offers another moment to learn from.
- Main point: Show why Whitney recommends asking newly booked couples about their choice and how those answers could inform ad copy and landing pages.
- Transformation: Move from treating reviews as the whole story to valuing both the decision to book and the experience afterward.
- CTA: No explicit ask. Close on the value of understanding the couple's choice.

### Carousel: Give the reader a fuller explanation of the two moments and show where the booking-decision question fits into WIA's advertising thinking.

1. Introduce the two moments without diminishing reviews.: Your couples can tell you about two different moments: choosing you and working with you.

Both are worth understanding. When I'm thinking about your advertising, I'm interested in what mattered at each point.
2. Establish the value of existing reviews.: A thoughtful review gives us insight into the experience you delivered.

It can help us understand what couples appreciated and what stayed with them after the wedding. That's useful information when we're getting to know your business and thinking about how to communicate its value.
3. Locate the additional question before the experience.: The booking decision happened earlier, while the couple was still considering their options.

At that point, they hadn't experienced everything their review would eventually describe. I want to understand what they were weighing and what helped them feel ready to choose you.
4. Show the real WIA contribution.: In a conversation with our team about our own marketing, I brought up the research we ask new ELEVATE clients to share.

That led me to recommend asking newly booked couples about their decision. It's a moment that can give us different information from a review written after working together.
5. Make the recommended inquiry concrete.: I'd want to ask:

What were you considering when you decided to book us? What helped you feel this was the right fit for your wedding?

Those questions invite the couple to describe the decision in their own words.
6. Connect possible answers to WIA's advertising work.: When we're writing ad copy and landing pages, information about that choice gives us something specific to consider.

Which parts of the business mattered to the couple before booking? What did they need to understand? Their answers can help us think about what to communicate to couples who are still deciding.
7. Explain how the two perspectives complement each other.: I'd consider those answers alongside the reviews.

What helped a couple choose you and what they loved afterward may overlap. They may also highlight different parts of your business. Hearing about both moments gives us a fuller picture to work with when we think about your messaging.
8. Close on the principle without an ask.: You've earned reviews that speak to the experience you deliver.

There's also value in hearing how couples arrived at the decision to trust you with their wedding. Understanding that moment gives us another perspective on what your advertising should communicate.
