**opening:** Hi {{contact.first_name}},

When a couple leaves your venue after a tour, how much do you really know about where they are in their search? That came up on a recent call with a venue client.

I asked her whether couples usually leave with other venues still on their list, or ready to move forward.

**body:** Her answer was a distinction she pays attention to on every visit: is her venue the first place this couple has seen, or the last?

That one detail changes what she does next. When she knows hers was the final stop, she gets in touch within a week and asks, kindly and plainly, whether they've settled on a venue or haven't decided. A couple at the end of their search can answer that.

A couple who has just started touring is somewhere else entirely. They're still weighing what they've seen against the visits still ahead, and that question would arrive too early.

**What you notice on the tour is the most useful thing we can shape follow-up around.**

Automated follow-up emails were already in place, and she was still writing to couples herself. She brought up the idea of a text after the tour as well, and I told her we could work that in.

That's how we approach follow-up for venue clients. You're already bringing in inquiries, hosting tours and talking with couples yourself. The emails and texts around that work should draw on what you pick up during the visit, so a couple still comparing and a couple ready to decide each hear something that suits their moment.

**script:** A venue client and I talked about sending couples a short video of her, by text, right after they inquire.

She was already working on a video, and we were figuring out where it should go. I mentioned that inboxes get pretty chaotic, and couples are often more likely to see a text, and actually answer it, than an email. She said that's very true for her couples.

So the idea we talked through was simple. Her first text after someone inquires could link to a video with her on camera, introducing herself and the venue, and then cutting to footage from real weddings, or even a little behind-the-scenes look.

She pictured it as a personal welcome from her, a quick virtual tour right there in the message.

And honestly, I think it could go to everyone who inquires, not only the couples who've already booked a tour.

What I love about it is the job it's doing. It's a sneak peek of what they'll see in person. We even talked about saying it plainly: you need to see this venue before you decide on one.

That's what we want that first message to do. Give a couple a real reason to come see the place for themselves.
