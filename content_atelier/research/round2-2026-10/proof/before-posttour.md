## Reel 1 · story

**title:** First tour or last tour

**cover_text:** More venue tours, more couples going quiet.

**on_screen_hook:** What I asked a venue client about couples going quiet after tours

**script:** I had a call with one of our venue clients recently, and she was booking more tours, but more of those couples were going quiet afterwards.

So I asked her a question. When couples leave a tour, do they still have other venues to see, or are they ready to move forward?

And she explained that she gauges one thing: is her venue their first tour, or their last?

When she knows it's their last, she reaches out within a week and asks, really politely, if they've made their choice or they're still undecided.

I thought that was such a smart distinction.

Because a couple who's just started touring is still comparing, right? They're not necessarily ready for that question yet.

Now, she already had automated emails going out, and she was following up personally on top of that. She'd been thinking about texting couples after the tour, too, and I told her we could add that.

So when we shape follow-up after a tour, the fact that a couple visited is only part of the picture. We want to know what they're ready to do next, so every email and text supports the conversation you're already having with them.


**caption:** When more couples tour your venue and more of them go quiet, it's worth asking where they were in their search. / Couples found you, inquired and came out to see the venue in person. You walked them through it and helped them picture the day. Then some of them stop answering, and you're left guessing whether they're still deciding or have moved on.

I talked about this with one of our venue clients recently. Her tours were up, and so was the number of couples going quiet. I asked whether those couples left with other venues still to visit or ready to move forward.

She explained that she gauges where her venue falls in a couple's search. If it's their final visit, she follows up within a week and asks, kindly and plainly, whether they've made a decision or are still undecided. Someone at the start of their search isn't necessarily ready for that.

She already had automated emails working next to her own personal outreach, and when she raised adding post-tour texts, I agreed we could incorporate them.

What you learn on a tour tells us what the next message should make room for. The emails and texts support the contact you're already having with couples, at the point in the search they've actually reached. / -Whitney

## Reel 2 · stance

**title:** A finished tour isn't a decision

**cover_text:** Not every venue tour ends in the same place.

**on_screen_hook:** A finished venue tour doesn't tell you a couple is ready to book.

**script:** I don't think a completed venue tour tells you whether a couple is ready to book.

You can give two couples the same thoughtful tour, and they can leave in very different places. One still has other venues on the list. The other has seen everything and is close to choosing.

So before we shape follow-up for a venue, we want to know which couple we're talking to.

One of our venue clients does this really well. She had more tours and more couples going quiet, and when I asked her about it, she told me she reads whether her venue is early in a couple's search or the last stop. When it's the last stop, she checks in within a week and asks, nicely and directly, if they've picked a venue or are still deciding.

That question fits the end of a search. A couple who's still comparing isn't necessarily ready for it.

She was already pairing automated emails with her own personal follow-up, and when she brought up adding texts after the tour, I agreed we could fold those in.

That's how I think about follow-up. The emails and texts should carry what you learned on the tour, and you keep the conversation going with the couple.


**caption:** A couple can love your venue tour and still have other venues to see. / You give every couple a thoughtful tour. Some leave with more venues on their list. Some have already seen everything and are close to choosing. From the outside, the quiet afterwards can look exactly the same.

That's why I want to know what a couple was ready to do before we shape any follow-up.

One of our venue clients had more tours and more unanswered messages, so I asked her about it. She distinguishes the start of a couple's tour list from the end. When she knows she was the final stop, she checks in politely within a week to ask if they've picked a venue or are still deciding. That direct question fits because they've finished visiting.

She also raised adding texts after the tour to the automated emails and personal follow-up she already had, and I agreed those could be included.

For us, the starting point is what the venue already knows about the couple. When follow-up matches how far along they are, it leaves room for the conversations you still want to have with them. / -Whitney
