# How we find booking opportunities in a venue's coming season

Central point: We keep a venue's coming season in view by connecting it to reasons couples could choose it, including the team's ability to support a shorter engagement.

## Reel 1 (story)
Cover: Spring wedding ads for shorter engagements
On-screen hook: We proposed a shorter-engagement angle for spring wedding ads.
Script:
I brought up shorter engagements while we were planning a venue's spring wedding ads. The owners' response explained why that could work.

A little backstory for you. I had asked what made spring a good time to get married at their venue. They described how green the property is that time of year, and we were talking about bringing that experience into the copy.

Then I suggested speaking to couples who don't want a really long engagement. Could we help them see a spring wedding as something this venue could bring together?

The owners told me they could move very quickly because they had the full team already in-house. And they wanted that angle included.

That gave the proposed direction a practical reason to make sense. For a couple who wants to get married sooner, the team could be part of why they consider that season.

That's what I want us to keep looking for as we plan a venue's advertising. We have the owner's calendar in mind, but we also need a reason that timing could be right for the couple. In this conversation, the team's ability to move quickly gave us something meaningful to work with.
Caption:
Your next wedding season may have something to offer couples who want to marry sooner.
When you're already advertising, planning the coming season involves more than deciding which dates to mention. You want couples to understand what choosing that time of year could mean for their wedding, and why your venue could make it work.

In a conversation with a venue's owners, we explored a spring direction for their upcoming ads. They described the property's greenery, giving us a seasonal experience to draw from rather than a date alone.

Whitney also suggested inviting couples who wanted a shorter engagement. The owners explained that their team was already in-house and could move quickly, and they wanted that possibility represented in the copy.

The proposed invitation had a reason behind it: a couple hoping to get married sooner could consider the venue's ability to support that timing.

We want advertising conversations to connect those two perspectives. The owner is planning a season; the couple is deciding whether that season could suit the wedding they want. A venue's capabilities can help make that connection.
The coming calendar deserves advertising that gives couples a meaningful reason to consider it.

## Reel 2 (stance)
Cover: Sooner wedding dates need a reason to fit
On-screen hook: A shorter engagement can belong in a venue's ad strategy.
Script:
I think an approaching wedding date deserves a reason to be chosen, even when the engagement will be shorter.

When we're talking with a venue about the season ahead, I want us to think about why that timing could work for a couple. What could make them feel like, yes, we could have our wedding there, at that time?

We had a conversation with a venue's owners about spring ads, and I proposed a shorter-engagement direction. They explained that having their team in-house meant they could move quickly, and they wanted us to bring that into the copy.

So the invitation could be grounded in something they could actually offer. If a couple wants to marry sooner, the venue's team may give them a reason to consider that season.

I think that matters in an ongoing advertising partnership. We should be keeping the next season in view without assuming every couple is planning on the same timeline.

The owner's calendar and the couple's plans need to meet somewhere. Our role is to find a meaningful connection and give that proposed direction a place in the advertising.
Caption:
Couples can have a different timeline in mind from the one a venue is planning around.
You know the season you're preparing for and what your team can deliver. Your advertising can help couples see where their own wedding plans might fit, including when they would prefer a shorter engagement.

That possibility came into a spring-planning conversation when Whitney suggested an angle for couples who wanted to get married sooner. The venue's owners supported the idea because, as they explained, their in-house team could move quickly.

Their response gave the proposed copy direction a clear basis. We could connect the season to the venue's ability to support that kind of timeline, rather than simply asking couples to consider a date.

This is the attention we want a coming season to receive within an advertising strategy. A closer date can still be relevant when there is a reason it suits the couple and the venue can deliver the experience.

The invitation becomes more useful when both sides are represented: what the couple wants to do, and what the venue is equipped to bring together.
We want the advertising partnership to keep those possibilities in view as the calendar moves.
