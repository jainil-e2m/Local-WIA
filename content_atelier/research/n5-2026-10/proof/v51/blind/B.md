# How we decide whether to replace a client's existing page

Central point: Our work should earn its place in a client's advertising, with the same scrutiny we give the work already there.

## Reel 1 (story)
Cover: Our landing page wasn't an automatic replacement
On-screen hook: We built a page. We didn't switch all the ad traffic.
Script:
We built a landing page for a client, and we didn't move all their ad traffic to it.

A little context. They already had pages in their advertising, and we'd created another option. But building that option didn't automatically answer the question of where we should send couples.

On the call, I explained why we were comparing our page with the existing pages instead of switching everything over. We wanted to understand how our version would convert before making that change.

And I also told the client we were going to make updates to our page and send it back so they could take another look.

I think that matters when you're trusting someone with your advertising. You're bringing us in to make thoughtful decisions for your business, and sometimes that means taking another look at something our own team created.

We can believe in the page we've built and still give the pages you already have a fair comparison. Those two things can exist together.

What I want you to feel is that we're looking after your advertising, including being willing to improve our contribution before asking it to take the place of yours.
Caption:
Hiring an ads team doesn't mean every existing page needs replacing.
You've already put thought into how couples discover your business and where they go to learn more. When you bring in an advertising partner, you want that investment to be understood as the team considers what to add or change.

In one client conversation, I explained a decision involving a landing page we'd created. We were comparing it with the pages already being used rather than directing every ad click to our version simply because we'd built it.

I also outlined updates we planned to make before bringing the page back to the client for another review. Our contribution was open to refinement, just like anything else in the advertising.

That's an important part of how I think about the responsibility we're taking on. A team can have a clear recommendation without assuming every new asset should immediately replace an existing one. The reasoning has to serve the wedding business, including the couples considering it.

You should be able to see that care in the decisions we make about our own work, as well as the work we inherit.
-Whitney

Caption:
The responsibility for a new ad destination continues after we've built it.
For a wedding business, a page is part of the path a couple takes from discovering you to making an inquiry. Choosing where to send that couple deserves more consideration than who created the newest option.

We discussed exactly that in a client call. Our team had built a landing page, but the decision was to compare it with the client's existing pages before making a complete switch. Whitney then described refinements we intended to make and a further opportunity for the client to review our version.

That moment shows the distinction between delivering an asset and deciding the role it should play. The page can be finished enough to evaluate without becoming the automatic destination for every ad.

We want our recommendations to reflect the business we're advertising, rather than a preference for replacing what was there with something of our own. That includes being prepared to revisit our contribution when we're considering the next decision.

An owner should feel that new expertise is being applied with respect for their existing work and care for where their advertising goes next.
The same judgment should apply to what we create and what you bring to the partnership.
