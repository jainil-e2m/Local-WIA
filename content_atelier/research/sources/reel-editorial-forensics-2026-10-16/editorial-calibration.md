## How to use the supplied approved Reel examples

These six existing scripts are a small, undated taste sample, not a complete preference profile. They are known existing treatments for overlap review. They do not establish publication dates, engagement, current offers or facts about the present source conversations. Source dialogue controls all current-case claims. Do not reproduce an example's case as a fresh discovery.

Observe the creative choices, not just topics or vocabulary: a firm recommendation has an owner-facing reason; a recognizable business situation carries the teaching; a founder's work can demonstrate a belief; capable owners may want guidance or breathing room rather than criticism. Explanations can be substantial, but they serve one central thought instead of assembling a tutorial. Commercial relevance can follow naturally without every piece needing a sales pitch. Openings vary; do not impose one hook formula, mood or mandatory format.

The DJ-business example overlaps the current Week 3 source. Treat it as an existing case, not a new Reel because its title changes. Its 90-event wording is stronger than the call's forecast, so it cannot verify a completed outcome. The spending example uses different cost bases (media versus media plus agency fees), so it cannot supply a comparable ROI claim. Old audits, prices, offers and feature descriptions require current verification. Learn framing and restraint without inheriting factual mistakes.

Compare a candidate's scene AND thought with these examples. A familiar belief can earn a new treatment through a materially different scene, consequence or perspective. If evidence is insufficient to decide overlap, say so. No approval prediction or novelty claim based solely on changed words.
