# How Whitney edits (for writers and judges)

From Whitney Hodges' corrections in her ChatGPT export (June 2025 to October 2026). "Discouraged" rules are ones her own approved copy sometimes breaks: judge by frequency and fit.

## 1. Positioning
- Make the piece show what WIA knows, how it thinks and how it works with clients. A generic lesson with no WIA contribution fails, however well written.
- WIA is a specialist paid-ads agency (Google and Meta) for wedding businesses. Never imply it does branding, SEO, coaching, operations, full-service marketing or the client's selling.
- Ads amplify a business that already works; they don't rescue a broken one. Foundations (positioning, landing page, follow-up, tracking) come first.
- Never run down referrals, SEO, organic social or a single platform. Treat them as valuable foundations that paid ads build on.
- Never present advertising, a channel or a WIA offer as frustrating, risky or second-rate. Lead with possibility, not pain.
- Describe offers exactly. The Accelerators pair a done-for-you campaign launch with guided support while the client does the ongoing work; they are not a course and not full management. ELEVATE is the done-for-you service. LeadLock is a separate platform and must never suggest that more leads don't matter.

## 2. Audience
- Write to established, growth-minded owners who already market well and want more of the right inquiries. Never write to the desperate, the struggling or the "where is my next lead coming from" reader.
- Signal that reader through detail, don't label them. Leave room for owners let down by DIY, courses or agencies.
- Use the problem the reader actually believes they have, in words they would use. Don't re-teach what they already know.
- Make every example specific to weddings: couples, inquiries, tours, bookings.
- Never imply a prerequisite before someone can work with WIA.

## 3. Accuracy and claims
- Never invent a testimonial, quote, anecdote, statistic, price, deadline or result. Use only what the source contains; leave out or flag what can't be verified.
- Client stories: exact numbers, periods, timing and emotions; say what a figure does and does not include; credit the foundation, not ads alone. Keep clients unnamed unless approval is recorded.
- Proof shows what WIA can do; it never promises a reader the same result.
- Keep prices, spots and deadlines out of public copy unless already published, and only state scarcity that is true.
- Platform facts must be right (paid search doesn't change organic rankings). Don't overclaim maturity, popularity or automation.
- Own WIA's results plainly; don't hedge them into insignificance or present them as flukes.

## 4. Voice and style
Banned outright:
- Em dashes, from 26 December 2025 onward.
- The problem-swap hook (telling readers their real problem is a different one than they think), and the matching "need" swap. Banned from late April 2026, absolutely from August 2026.
- Overused openers announcing why an offer was created, and other stock assistant phrasing.

Discouraged (allowed sparingly; her approved copy uses them):
- "It's not X, it's Y" contrasts and "the goal isn't X" constructions. One per piece at most, and never as the default hook.
- "Not because X, but because Y".
- Chains of very short fragments.
- Emojis, hashtags and exclamation marks.

Always:
- Concrete over vague: name the real consequence. Every word must mean exactly what it says and fit the topic.
- Calm, warm, confident. Never bossy, needy, gushing or salesy.
- Short and tight: no over-explaining, no repetition within a piece.
- Use contractions. Emails flow in short paragraphs rather than bullet lists.
- "I" for Whitney's own experience, "we" when both co-founders or the brand speak. The sign-off matches whoever the call to action names.

## 5. Format and channel
- Each format in a campaign gets its own hook. A caption never repeats its Reel's hook or on-screen text. Sibling pieces repeat ideas, not wording.
- Carousels: real substance on every slide, usually 7 to 10 slides.
- Emails must make sense to someone who has seen nothing else; no references to Instagram posts. They end with a reply prompt, never a DM request.
- Threads posts are short and carry no call to action.
- Not every piece sells. A call to action must be a real next step that suits the channel and the offer's current status.
- Recorded video is used as said. Captions match the clip; never rewrite spoken lines.

## 6. Process: refine, don't replace
- When given Whitney's draft, change only what is weak or was flagged. Keep her wording, structure and length. Never swap in a fresh rewrite or quietly undo a line she approved.
- Background she shares is for the writer, not the copy.
- Give honest critique, not praise, and return the complete piece ready to edit.
- When judging, prefer the version that sounds like someone who knows this business, says something true and particular, and would survive her line-by-line read.
