## 1. The ideal client in Whitney's words

### Who WIA wants

- "Our ideal client is already running a thriving business, but they are ready to uplevel. They need ads to grow. And they know it. They believe in the power of ads… the clients who value us most are those who have tried to DIY it and gotten stuck or who have worked with another agency or freelancer… The missing pieces are usually the right strategy, messaging and execution." (B-strategy-1, 2025-10-16_68f1454c, 17 Oct 2025)
- "We're not here to fix broken brands. We're here to take amazing wedding businesses with awesome organic marketing and strong brands and amplify that through advertising" (A-social-1, 2025-11-18_691cc2e6)
- "Our most successful clients are great marketers" (C-email-1, 2025-09-24)
- The ready buyer is "Making sales, already seeing growth, putting real effort into organic marketing, knows they need to spend money to make money." The misconception she sees most is "Expecting immediate ROI." (C-email-1, 2026-02-16_69935d2f)
- The two buyers she distinguishes: "already successful wedding pros who really want to lean in on what's working and double down versus pros who are struggling and think ads can save them." WIA wants the first group (B-strategy-2, 2026-04-01_69cd7358, 2 Apr 2026).
- "marketing savvy… seeing initial success with a growth focused mindset"; "good at it and consistent", not "mastered" (A-social-1).
- **Strong-brand signals she lists:** they know their ideal client; they have a brand designer; a conversion-focused website; inquiries arriving through Instagram DMs; organic Google leads (A-social-1, 2025-12-23_694acab5).
- "I wish every lead came to us that warm and feeling like we're interviewing them not the other way around" (B-strategy-1, 2025-10-04).

### Who WIA does not want

- "If they are wondering where their next lead is coming from, this definitely isn't for them" (A-social-2, 2025-12-30).
- "Our programs are NOT for people who are struggling" (C-email-1, 2026-02-16).
- "I don't want to work with clients who have shitty websites" and "I don't want this to attract those wedding pros that are never showing up" (A-social-3, 2026-08-13, 2026-10-01).
- "If they are looking for a DFY agency, and all they want is Meta Ads, we aren't a fit" (E-sales-1, 2026-07-14).
- Not built "to 'save' a burned-out business" (attachments E10).
- **Geography:** the Americas preferred; the UK and Europe are acceptable if they target US couples; Australia and New Zealand are "probably out of the question" (B-strategy-1, 2025-10-04).
- **Budget:** applications require $500 a month per platform (B-strategy-2; E-sales-1).

### Nuance: keep the door open

"I don't want people to not apply because they are second guessing if their branding is strong enough" (A-social-2, 2026-02-03). "a lot of people actually have a pretty good idea of what's missing" (C-email-2, 2026-04-28).

### Client mix

"a preference in general to work with venues and planners" (A-social-2, 2025-12-29). Whitney's own count of photography and videography experience is small: three full-service videography clients, one full-service photographer, and "another handful or more of photographers" through VIP Days or the Accelerators (E-sales-1, 2026-09-23).

## 2. Prospects' and clients' own words (anonymised)

### Overwhelm and DIY

- "I am a Lone Ranger at our wedding venue"; "an advertising budget and mindset is a fight for me every time" (venue, E-sales-1, 2026-01-02)
- "The hamster wheel. And juggling it. To be authentic" (hair and makeup artist, E-sales-1, 2026-08-05)
- "I just don't want to have to take another course. I don't want this all to be on me." (September 2026 sales calls, C-email-2)

### Not knowing whether ads work

### Leads that don't convert

**Her own list of objections heard on sales calls** (B-strategy-2, 2026-04-23):
- the quality of creative and copy;
- responsiveness and skill, after bad agency experiences;
- the time and effort the Accelerators demand;
- photographers, videographers and planners doubting that ads work in their niche;
- not knowing whether to spend on ads, SEO, a rebrand or a new website;
- shame about what advertising says about the business.

## 4. Desires (her market-research summary, 4 Jun 2026)

"they want aligned high quality leads. They want to be the top choice. They wants sales to feel easy and inevitable"; "They want to mark smart decisions for their business"; "They have invested in ads before and have either felt like the support was generic, didn't get results, or it was a positive experience but they know there's still room for more improvement"; "a customized solution not a cookie cutter approach" (B-strategy-3, 2026-06-02_6a1efd89).

**Quality, not volume:** "They do want/need more inquiries, but they want quality inquiries" (B-strategy-2, 2026-03-23).

## 5. Survey results (use with caution)

The May 2026 client survey's multiple-choice options were drafted with ChatGPT, so these are endorsed options, not customer wording (B-strategy-2, 2026-04-23).

- Most-chosen problem: "Getting in front of enough of the right people consistently".
- Most important factors were split evenly: support and guidance; clear strategy and return; efficient progress and a smart move.
- Main hold-back: quality inquiries, "not just more leads".

## 6. Where to keep listening

The best audience language in the export comes from DMs, application forms, emails and sales-call notes, more than from calls. Whitney already asks new followers "What brought you to our page?" and "what about the ad made you decide to follow?" (E-sales-1, Aug 2026). The application and demo forms are worth capturing as question sets only, never with the answers attached to a person.
