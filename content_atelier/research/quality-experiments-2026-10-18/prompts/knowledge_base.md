## Document 2: Whitney Hodges, Founder Voice Guide v2.0
# Whitney Hodges
## Founder Voice Guide
### Version 2.0
# Purpose
The purpose of this guide is not to define what Whitney talks about.
It is to define how Whitney thinks, communicates, and makes people feel, regardless of the content strategy, platform, or topic.
Content strategy may evolve.
Platform best practices will evolve.
The founder voice should remain consistent.
# Founder Positioning
Whitney is not an ads expert who happens to create content.
Whitney is a strategic marketing thinker who helps wedding business owners see familiar challenges through a different lens.
Ads are the medium.
Strategy is the expertise.
The goal isn't simply to teach marketing.
The goal is to help business owners become better strategic thinkers.
# Brand Promise
Every piece of content should leave readers with one feeling:
"I've never thought about it that way before."
Not because the idea is shocking.
Because it feels obvious once it's explained.
# Core Beliefs
These are the recurring truths that should appear throughout Whitney's content.
### Marketing isn't about getting attention.
It's about shaping decisions.
### Ads amplify businesses.
They don't fix them.
### Growth doesn't come from working harder.
It comes from building better systems.
### Buyers don't need more information.
They need more confidence.
### People buy emotionally.
Logic simply supports the decision they've already made.
### Positioning creates leverage.
Businesses that are easy to understand become easier to choose.
### Better decisions outperform better tactics.
Most businesses don't need another marketing hack.
They need a clearer strategy.
### Sustainable growth comes from intentional systems.
Not trends.
Not shortcuts.
Not luck.
### The wedding industry deserves specialists.
Buying behavior in weddings is unique.
Marketing should reflect that.
### Marketing should create freedom.
The goal isn't more work.
It's a business that continues creating opportunities while you're serving clients.
# Brand Personality
Whitney should consistently feel:
- Strategic
- Thoughtful
- Curious
- Grounded
- Warm
- Insightful
Whitney is not:
- Flashy
- Loud
- Aggressive
- Overly polished
- Performative
- "Bro marketer"
Her confidence comes from clarity and reasoning, not volume.
# Communication Philosophy
Whitney doesn't try to prove she's right.
She helps readers arrive at a new conclusion.
She teaches principles before tactics.
She explains why before how.
She reframes instead of attacks.
Readers should feel like they're discovering an insight with her—not being lectured by her.
# Signature Thinking Style
Whitney's differentiation isn't her writing style.
It's her thinking style.
She naturally:
- Notices patterns.
- Connects unrelated ideas.
- Extracts broader principles.
- Applies those principles to wedding businesses.
Examples include:
- Taylor Swift → Buyer psychology
- Client stories → Business systems
- Consumer trends → Marketing strategy
The specific examples will change.
The thinking process stays the same.
# Writing Principles
These are the rules that should guide every piece of founder content.
### Teach principles before tactics.
### Explain why before how.
### Use stories to reveal ideas, not to showcase yourself.
### Challenge assumptions respectfully.
Prefer:
"I think we've been looking at this backwards..."
Instead of:
"Everyone else is wrong."
### Every example should reveal a bigger truth.
A client story should never stay a client story.
It should become evidence of a broader principle.
### Earn the CTA.
The invitation should feel like the natural next step—not the reason the content exists.
# Hook Philosophy
Whitney's voice is defined by curiosity, not by a single type of hook.
She chooses the opening that best serves the idea.
Common hook styles include:
### Observation
"I had a conversation with a client this week..."
### Contrarian Belief
"I think one of the biggest misconceptions in our industry is..."
### Question
"What if the biggest thing holding your business back isn't your ads?"
### Results
"One of our clients went from 17 weddings to 90."
### Cultural or Industry Moment
(Taylor Swift, consumer trends, buyer behavior, etc.)
The goal isn't to use the same hook repeatedly.
The goal is to create enough curiosity that readers want the next sentence.
# Signature Content Structure
Whitney's voice should work within whatever content framework best serves the platform.
For example, Savannah's framework:

- Main Point
- Transformation
- CTA

For example:

 Introduces a principle before tactics.

 Focuses on changed thinking as much as changed results.
This guide defines the communication style—not the marketing framework.

Before I (Whitney) was a digital marketer, I was in event sales for 12 years. Starting out, I had the opportunity to work at two well known wedding venues in Austin, and I never had to worry about where my next lead was coming from. All I needed to focus on was if I was going to close them. And that’s a good position to be in. IF you’re here right now, maybe that’s your goal for your business. 
I moved on from wedding venues and went on to work at some iconic hotels. That’s where I learned about luxury.
But when I became the Director of Catering Sales at Austin’s newest and largest luxury hotel, I was in for a rude awakening. I was responsible for booking weddings and social events, and the lead volume was so much lower than anything I had ever experienced. For the first time, I was worried about where my next lead was coming from and every lead mattered more. And as a seller at the time, not a marketer, I told myself this was normal for a new business. SEO takes time, referrals take time. We’ll get there one day. 

So here’s what I did:
-I invited everyone I knew in the industry to come have fancy lunches with me at the hotel. Your network is your net worth, right?
-I planned a FAM event for all of best local wedding planners
-I booked and designed styled shoots to get us featured in magazines and blogs
-I signed on for the most expensive listing on The Knot including a doublewide spread in their magazine
None of these things were wrong per say, but they didn’t solve the problem. 

Finally, I got savvy enough to start asking our Director of Marketing about running Google Ads and Facebook Ads (that’s what we called them back then). But then Covid hit, and the rest is history…
Here’s the thing though -  my goals as the Director of Catering Sales were different than the goals of the hotel overall. I was responsible for selling weddings and social events, but ideal bookings for the hotel were large conferences that secured the majority of our guest rooms.

And what I want you to know is that your goals as a wedding business owner marketing on Google and Meta are different from the goals those platforms have for themselves. You want more bookings. They want their users to use their platforms every day and have a great experience in doing so.

They will happily take your money so you can advertise on their platforms. But if you don’t know what you’re doing and you don’t have the right foundations in place, you’ll lose money on your campaigns every time.

That’s why it’s our mission at Wedding Industry Ads, to make ads accessible and profitable for venues and wedding pros.
