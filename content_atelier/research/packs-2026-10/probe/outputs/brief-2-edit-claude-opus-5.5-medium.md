**SUBJECT:** You don't owe your ad team a yes
**OTHER SUBJECTS:** Can I say no to an ad my agency made? | Your ad feedback can include a no
**PREVIEW TEXT:** Even after they've already made it, the call on what represents your business is still yours.

Hi {{contact.first_name}},

We'd sent one of our clients a few new ad creatives for his Meta ads. When we got on a call, he hadn't finalized anything yet, but he was honest with me anyway:

"I don't necessarily remember what they were, but I don't think that I love them."

Honestly? That's not an easy thing to say to the people who made the ads. Our team had put the work in, and when that's true, it can feel like agreeing is the polite thing to do.

So I told him two things. If there were any specific tweaks or edits he'd like to see, he should let us know. And then, in my own words on that call: "If you decide you don't want to use them at all, like no hard feelings there as well."

I meant both. A revision was on the table, and so was not using those particular ads.

**Our work on an ad doesn't make your approval something we're owed.**

When you hire an advertising team, you're bringing in expertise and taking real work off your plate. But it's still your wedding business that couples are seeing in their feed. That ad is often one of the first impressions they get of you, well before they visit your website or book a call.

That's why your reaction carries so much weight in a creative review. You know your brand. You know which details feel like you and which ones don't. If a proposed ad doesn't sit right, we need to hear it, whether you want to change it or set it aside entirely.

And it shouldn't be any harder to say just because someone already made it.

If I were the owner, the advertising partner I'd want is one I could be that honest with. One where a half-formed reaction to a new ad starts a good conversation instead of an awkward one.

Talk soon,
Whitney
