**PREVIEW TEXT:** Hiring an advertising team should leave room for your judgment about how your business is shown to couples.

Hi {{contact.first_name}},

On a recent call with a client, I brought up the new Meta ads we'd sent over for his review. He told me he didn't remember exactly what they looked like, but that he didn't think he loved them. He hadn't finalized his review yet, and he said so honestly.

I told him that if there were specific tweaks or edits he'd like to see, we'd love to hear them. And then I said, "If you decide you don't want to use them at all, like no hard feelings there as well."

A revision was one option. Not using those particular ads was the other.

**Our work on an ad doesn't mean you owe us an approval.**

When you hire an advertising team, you're bringing in people with real expertise who take work off your plate. But you're still the person whose business is being shown to couples, and you know your brand better than anyone we could hire to write about it.

It's easy to feel that approving a new ad is the polite thing to do once a team has put time into it. I understand that pull. I also think it quietly makes honest feedback harder to give, and honest feedback is the most useful thing a client can hand us.

A tentative reaction like his is something we can work with. It tells us where to look, what to ask, and whether the ads need a different direction entirely.

So I'd rather hear "I don't think I love these" early than get a polite yes that never feels right once the ads are live.

If you already invest in your marketing and know how you want your business represented, I'd want an advertising team that makes room for that judgment, including the times when a finished ad isn't the one you want out there.

Talk soon,
Whitney
