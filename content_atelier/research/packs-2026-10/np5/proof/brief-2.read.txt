SUBJECTS: Can I say no to an ad my agency made? || You don't owe your ad team a yes || Your ad feedback can include a no
PREVIEW: The work behind a new ad shouldn't decide your answer for you.
Hi {{contact.first_name}},

We'd sent a client some new ads for review. When we talked, they told me they didn't think they loved them, and that they hadn't finalized anything yet. Their answer should be a genuine choice.

I invited specific changes.

I also told them that if they decided they didn't want to use the ads at all, there'd be no hard feelings.

A revision was available. So was choosing not to use those particular ads.

**Our work on an ad shouldn't create an obligation for you to approve it.**

When you hire an advertising team, you're bringing in people with expertise and the capacity to do work you'd otherwise be handling. But you're still the person whose wedding business is being represented to couples.

You can appreciate what the team has contributed and have reservations about the proposed creative. Both can be true in the same conversation.

The owner's reaction gives us something real to respond to, and there should be room for an answer to remain undecided.

Creative review should give the owner a meaningful role in choosing what represents the business. That means being able to request a change, but also being able to decide that a draft isn't the direction they want.

The fact that someone has already made it shouldn't make that answer harder to give.

You know your brand better than anyone. Expert help with your ads should make it easier to say what you really think about a draft, long before anyone decides whether it goes live.

Talk soon,
Whitney

CAPTION: Honest feedback on a finished ad is harder to give than it should be.
You know your wedding business better than anyone. You know how you want couples to feel when they come across it, whether that's in a Google search, on your website or scrolling Instagram late at night.

So when your ad team sends over new creative and something feels off, it can be awkward to say so. They've put time into it. You don't want to seem difficult. You might just sign off to keep things moving.

Not long ago, our team sent a client new Meta ads to review. On our call, they said they weren't sure they loved them and hadn't finished looking yet.

When you can be that honest with the people helping with your ads, reviews get lighter. You stop approving things out of politeness, and what reaches couples is something you actually want representing your business.

TITLE: Your yes isn't owed
COVER: Making your ad doesn't earn us your yes | ALTS: ["Your ad team's effort isn't your approval", 'A no on a new ad should be easy to say']
HOOK: Should the work behind an ad earn your agency a yes?
SCRIPT: I don't think the work we put into an ad should ever buy us a yes from a client.

Here's what I mean. When you hire a team to run your ads, part of what you're hiring us for is making the creative and sending it over for review. And I get why that can make a no feel rude. Someone sat down and made this thing for you, right?

But you're the one who knows how your venue, your photography or your planning business should look to couples. That call belongs to you.

This came up with a client recently. They weren't sure they loved a few new ads we'd sent, and they hadn't finished reviewing them. I told them edits were welcome, and if they'd rather not use them, that's completely fine. No hard feelings.

Honestly, I'd rather hear that than get a polite approval on an ad you don't love. Your yes only means something when a no was a real option.

That's the kind of working relationship I think every owner deserves with their ad team: one where you can simply tell us what you think.
PERSONAL: ['And I get why that can make a no feel rude.', "Honestly, I'd rather hear that than get a polite approval on an ad you don't love."]
CAPTION: There's a quiet pressure that comes with reviewing work someone made for you.
You open the email, look at the new ad your team created, and something isn't quite right. Maybe the photo isn't the moment you'd choose to show couples. Maybe the words don't sound like the way you talk on a tour or a consultation call. But the team has already done the work, and you'd rather not make things awkward, so you think about approving it anyway.

I believe the effort behind an ad should never turn approval into an obligation.

When a client recently told me they weren't sure they loved some new ads we'd sent, and that they hadn't finished reviewing them, I said two things. Send any specific edits you'd like. And if you'd rather not use those ads, that's fine too.

That's how I want every creative review to feel for the owner on the other side of it. Your perspective on your brand is the reason the review exists in the first place.

When declining is genuinely on the table, saying yes means something. You approve the ads you actually want couples to see, and the conversation about your business gets more honest.
-Whitney
