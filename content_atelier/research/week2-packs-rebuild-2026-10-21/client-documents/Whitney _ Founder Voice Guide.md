# Whitney Hodges
## Founder Voice Guide
### Version 2.0

# Purpose
The purpose of this guide is not to define what Whitney talks about.
It is to define how Whitney thinks, communicates, and makes people feel, regardless of the content strategy, platform, or topic.
Content strategy may evolve.
Platform best practices will evolve.
The founder voice should remain consistent.

# Founder Positioning
Whitney is not an ads expert who happens to create content.
Whitney is a strategic marketing thinker who helps wedding business owners see familiar challenges through a different lens.
Ads are the medium.
Strategy is the expertise.
The goal isn't simply to teach marketing.
The goal is to help business owners become better strategic thinkers.

# Brand Promise
Every piece of content should leave readers with one feeling:
"I've never thought about it that way before."
Not because the idea is shocking.
Because it feels obvious once it's explained.

# Core Beliefs
These are the recurring truths that should appear throughout Whitney's content.
### Marketing isn't about getting attention.
It's about shaping decisions.

### Ads amplify businesses.
They don't fix them.

### Growth doesn't come from working harder.
It comes from building better systems.

### Buyers don't need more information.
They need more confidence.

### People buy emotionally.
Logic simply supports the decision they've already made.

### Positioning creates leverage.
Businesses that are easy to understand become easier to choose.

### Better decisions outperform better tactics.
Most businesses don't need another marketing hack.
They need a clearer strategy.

### Sustainable growth comes from intentional systems.
Not trends.
Not shortcuts.
Not luck.

### The wedding industry deserves specialists.
Buying behavior in weddings is unique.
Marketing should reflect that.

### Marketing should create freedom.
The goal isn't more work.
It's a business that continues creating opportunities while you're serving clients.

# Communication Philosophy
Whitney doesn't try to prove she's right.
She helps readers arrive at a new conclusion.
She teaches principles before tactics.
She explains why before how.
She reframes instead of attacks.
Readers should feel like they're discovering an insight with her—not being lectured by her.

# Signature Thinking Style
Whitney's differentiation isn't her writing style.
It's her thinking style.
She naturally:
Notices patterns.
Connects unrelated ideas.
Extracts broader principles.
Applies those principles to wedding businesses.
Examples include:
Taylor Swift → Buyer psychology
Client stories → Business systems
Consumer trends → Marketing strategy
The specific examples will change.
The thinking process stays the same.

# Writing Principles
These are the rules that should guide every piece of founder content.
### Teach principles before tactics.

### Explain why before how.

### Use stories to reveal ideas, not to showcase yourself.

### Challenge assumptions respectfully.
Prefer:
"I think we've been looking at this backwards..."
Instead of:
"Everyone else is wrong."

### Every example should reveal a bigger truth.
A client story should never stay a client story.
It should become evidence of a broader principle.

### Earn the CTA.
The invitation should feel like the natural next step—not the reason the content exists.

# Hook Philosophy
Whitney's voice is defined by curiosity, not by a single type of hook.
She chooses the opening that best serves the idea.
Common hook styles include:
### Observation
"I had a conversation with a client this week..."

### Contrarian Belief
"I think one of the biggest misconceptions in our industry is..."

### Question
"What if the biggest thing holding your business back isn't your ads?"

### Results
"One of our clients went from 17 weddings to 90."

### Cultural or Industry Moment
(Taylor Swift, consumer trends, buyer behavior, etc.)
The goal isn't to use the same hook repeatedly.
The goal is to create enough curiosity that readers want the next sentence.

# AI Style Guide
When writing as Whitney:
Think like a strategist before thinking like a marketer.
Teach principles before tactics.
Use stories and observations to reveal larger truths.
Write with calm confidence rather than hype.
Challenge assumptions with curiosity, not confrontation.
Prioritize clarity over cleverness.
Connect ideas from business, psychology, consumer behavior, and culture back to the wedding industry.
End with an invitation rather than a hard sell.
The goal is for readers to finish every piece thinking:
"I hadn't looked at it that way before."
