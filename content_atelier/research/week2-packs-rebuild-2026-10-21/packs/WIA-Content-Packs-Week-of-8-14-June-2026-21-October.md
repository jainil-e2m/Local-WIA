**Intended audience.** Wedding pros of every kind who boost posts, run Meta ads themselves, or "tried Meta" and decided it brings the wrong people.

Ask for clicks and you get people who click. If you want inquiries, you have to ask Meta for inquiries, then give every campaign one clear job in how couples actually decide.

## Brief

**Central point.** Meta does what your campaign tells it to do, so we tell it to find inquiries.

**Your perspective, in your words.** Meta is very literal. It'll do what you tell it to do. If all you're asking for is clicks to your website, it'll find you people who love to click. So we get really literal right back: we want this person to become a lead. Then we give every campaign one job, because a couple who has never heard of you needs something different from a couple who has already been on your site twice.

## Reel 1: Meta is very literal

**Cover:** Meta takes you at your word
**On-screen hook:** Meta does what you tell it to. So what are you telling it?

**Script:**

I was on a call this summer with a wedding pro who's spending real money on ads, and when I asked him what he was running on Meta, he said, "I feel like I'm boosting, you know?"

It was a video of him, and it was sending people straight to his website.

And here's what I told him. Meta is very literal. It will do what you tell it to do. So if your campaign is set up to get people clicking over to your website, it's going to go find you people who click. And honestly, it's really good at that.

But a click isn't an inquiry. And it's definitely not a booking.

So when we build Meta campaigns for our clients, we get really literal right back. We tell Meta, we want this person to become a lead. That means a lead form, or an inquiry form on a landing page, something Meta can actually track past the click. Then it can start learning who actually inquires, and go find more people like them.

That's why one of the first things we look at in a new client's account isn't the creative. It's what each campaign is actually asking Meta to find.

**Caption:**

Boosting a post is the easiest way to spend money on Meta. It's rarely the best way to get inquiries.

A lot of the wedding pros who come to us have boosted at some point. You post something you're proud of, the button is right there, and a few days later you've got more views and likes, maybe a few new followers. Then you look at your inquiries and nothing much has changed, and it's easy to decide Meta just brings the wrong people.

The way I see it, Meta takes your instructions literally. It looks for the people most likely to take the action your campaign is set up for. Set it up for engagement and it finds people who engage. Set it up for clicks and it finds people who click.

When we build Meta for our clients, the action we set it up for is an inquiry, so Meta can follow what happens after the click and learn which couples actually reach out. Then each campaign gets its own job: introducing you to couples who've never heard of you, asking for the inquiry, and answering questions for the couples who are already considering you.

It's the same platform with completely different instructions, and the instructions are where the strategy lives.

Save this for your engagement-season planning.

-Whitney

## Reel 2: Why we turned off her biggest Meta campaign

**Cover:** Why we turned off her biggest Meta campaign
**On-screen hook:** Her biggest Meta campaign had zero tracked leads.

**Script:**

When we start with a new client, one of the first things I look at is what their ads are already doing. And this summer, with a venue we'd just started working with, the Meta campaign spending the most money hadn't produced a single tracked lead.

When I looked closer, it made sense. That campaign was only showing ads to people who had visited her website in the last 45 days. Now, we love retargeting. We use it all the time. But asking a small group of recent website visitors to be the source of your new inquiries puts a lot of pressure on a really small audience.

So we turned it off while we built her new campaigns. It doesn't make sense to spend just to spend.

Then our team rebuilt her Meta around what couples need at each stage. One campaign introduces the venue to couples who've never heard of it, using content she already had that shows off the space and the vibe. One campaign's whole job is getting the inquiry, and that's where most of the budget goes. And retargeting keeps her in front of the couples who've already been on her site, while they're still touring other venues.

Three campaigns, each with its own job, and each one judged by that job.

Meta is great at following instructions. Somebody has to write good ones.

**Caption:**

Sometimes the most useful thing we do in a new client's account is turn something off.

This summer, we started working with a venue whose biggest Meta campaign, the one spending more than anything else, hadn't produced a single tracked lead. On paper it wasn't a bad idea. It was a retargeting campaign, and we love retargeting. But it only reached people who had visited the website in the last 45 days, and it was being asked to bring in new inquiries from that one small group.

So we turned it off and rebuilt her Meta with clearer jobs: one campaign to introduce the venue to couples who'd never heard of it, one built to get the inquiry, and one to stay in front of the couples already comparing venues.

Meta will keep spending a campaign's budget whether or not that campaign is set up to do the job you need. Nothing in the account will flag it for you. That's the kind of thing we look for in the first weeks with a new client, before we write a single new ad.

## Reel 3: Your warmest couples need their own ads

**Cover:** Your warmest couples need different ads
**On-screen hook:** Her retargeting campaign was bringing in more leads than her lead campaign. Here's why.

**Script:**

For one of our venue clients this summer, something interesting happened on Meta. The retargeting campaign, the one that only shows ads to people who've already engaged with the venue, was bringing in more leads than the campaign we'd built specifically to get leads.

Carmen, who runs Google Ads on our team and was managing this account at the time, had a simple read on it: those people had already been nurtured. They'd seen the venue before. By the time they saw that ad, they weren't meeting it for the first time.

And that's exactly why we don't treat every couple the same. Someone who's never heard of you needs an introduction. Someone who's been on your website and is still deciding needs something completely different. They need to know what it's actually like to work with you, how the process works, and the answers to the questions they haven't asked you yet.

So for those warm couples, we use content that goes deeper, like behind-the-scenes footage, real weddings, the questions couples always ask, and the reasons they'd choose you over the other names on their list.

And because we're running a mix of creative at every stage, the couples who are really considering you start to feel like you're everywhere, in the best way.

Meta can do all of that. It just needs to know who it's talking to and what each ad is supposed to do.

**Caption:**

The couples who already know you deserve their own ads.

When one ad has to do every job, it's introducing you to strangers, asking for the inquiry and trying to win over the couples who are already close, all at once.

For one of our venue clients this summer, the account itself showed why that matters. Her retargeting campaign, which only reaches people who've already engaged with the venue, was bringing in more leads than the campaign built to get leads. Our team's read was simple: those people had already been nurtured, and they didn't need to be introduced to the venue again.

So we give warm audiences content that goes deeper: behind the scenes, real weddings, how the process works and why couples choose you over the other names on their list. Cold audiences get the introduction. The couples in between get a clear invitation to inquire.

That's how we plan Meta for our clients. Every stage gets an ad made for it, and the couples who are seriously considering you start seeing you everywhere.

-Whitney
