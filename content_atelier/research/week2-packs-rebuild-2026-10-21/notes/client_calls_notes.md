### What T02 demonstrates about WIA (my synthesis)
1. WIA's job does not stop at lead volume. "More inquiries, more tours" is the moment WIA starts the next layer of work. ("And so now's the time.")
2. WIA reads the whole journey: ads -> LeadLock nurture (opens/clicks by email) -> tours booked -> cancelled/no-show -> booked. Ad leads tracked "in isolation".
3. Follow-up data is market research: the rain-plan email got the tour clicks -> what couples worry about -> a message to lead with in ads AND emails. Owner's tour conversations confirm it (#1 reason they lose couples).
4. The owner's knowledge is part of the strategy: tour timing constraints, two kinds of touring couples, date vs venue preference.
5. Whitney's sales instincts (12 years in venue/hotel sales, founder story) show up as concrete ideas: video intro from the owner by text, FaceTime on a real wedding day, real rain-plan photos, "you need to see us before you decide".
6. Honesty: some ghosting is normal with more visibility; WIA doesn't promise to fix it; transparent about a tracking issue.
7. Scope: WIA doesn't run the tours or the sales (belief "We can't fully take ownership of their client aquisition because we don't handle the sales for them") but brings recommendations that make the marketing do its job before the tour.
8. LeadLock = visibility ("what really allows us to be able to see what's working") + automatic response so owner isn't chasing tire kickers.
9. Retargeting outperforming conversion campaign because those people were already nurtured: evidence for "ads leads are colder"/"couples need multiple touchpoints" beliefs.
