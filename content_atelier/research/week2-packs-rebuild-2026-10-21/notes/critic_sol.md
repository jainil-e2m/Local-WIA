**Whitney would reject these as a complete pack rather than treat them as ready for a light edit.** They still reproduce the main problem she named on October 13: a client conversation becomes a lesson in diagnosing or running ads, especially in Pack 1 and parts of Pack 2. ## 3. The 12 most important line-level fixes

These are targeted corrections, not permission to rewrite the whole pack. **Replacements that retain client events still require source verification.** No replacement below should be used to paper over an unverified anecdote.

### 1. Correct the platform guarantee
**Pack 1 / Reel 1 on-screen hook**

> **Current:** “Meta will give you exactly what you ask for. That's the problem.”

**Replacement:**  
“Before we changed his Meta ads, we looked at what they were optimizing for.”

This gives WIA an actual action and removes the platform guarantee and pain-led framing.

### 2. Replace anthropomorphism with the actual mechanism
**Pack 1 / Email**

> **Current:** “Meta is very literal. It will do exactly what you tell it to do.”

**Replacement:**  
“The campaign objective tells Meta which action to optimize for, so we started by checking whether that matched what he wanted from his ads.”

### 3. Stop disparaging boosting
**Pack 1 / Reel 1 caption**

> **Current:** “Boosting a post is the easiest way to spend money on Meta. It's also one of the easiest ways to teach Meta the wrong lesson about who your couples are.”

**Replacement:**  
“When someone comes to us after boosting posts, we look at what those ads were designed to achieve before deciding what to change.”

### 4. Correct the lead-versus-booking claim
**Pack 1 / Reel 2 cover**

> **Current:** “The campaign spending the most was booking nothing”

**Replacement:**  
“We paused her highest-spend Meta campaign”

The reason belongs in the story, with the tracked-lead period stated accurately.

### 5. Remove the undefined performance comparison
**Pack 1 / Reel 3 on-screen hook**

> **Current:** “Our retargeting campaign was beating our lead campaign. Here's why that makes sense.”

**Replacement:**  
“Her retargeting results gave us a reason to look more closely at what those couples had already seen.”

### 6. Remove the false diagnostic rule
**Pack 2 / Email**

> **Current:** “If people leave within a few seconds, that usually points at the ad. If they look around and still don't reach out, that points at the page.”

**Replacement:**  
“Carmen looked at the visits alongside the ads and the page, rather than assuming the lack of inquiries came from one setting.”

### 7. Replace the course straw man
**Pack 2 / Email**

> **Current:** “That's the part of running your own ads that no course can do for you. The settings are learnable. The hard part is knowing which decision to make when something isn't working, and making it for the right reason.”

**Replacement:**  
“What mattered here was that Carmen could look at the member’s actual account and explain why she recommended the next step.”

## 4. What is strategically off

### A. The packs still confuse thought leadership with an instructional explanation

Whitney’s October 13 distinction was specific:

- Reflect on the real conversation.
- Show how WIA and the owner talked through it.
- Explain the next action for that client.
- Establish what an advertising partnership should provide.

Pack 1 instead teaches objectives, audiences and campaign architecture. Pack 2 teaches data sufficiency and post-click diagnosis. Adding “when we build campaigns” or “Carmen looked at” does not fix an instructional angle when the reader’s takeaway remains **“here’s what I should check in my account.”**

**Recommendation:** Keep the content centered on the decision WIA helped that particular owner make. Remove the reader self-audits and the generalized troubleshooting rules.

### B. The audience is repeatedly assumed to be dissatisfied or unsure

The accumulated reader is someone who:

- Spends money without inquiries.
- Thinks Meta brings the wrong people.
- Is afraid to keep an ad running.
- Worries a failure was their fault.
- Hopes their ads work.
- Needs running ads to “feel possible.”

That is not the established, growth-minded owner looking to double down. Those experiences can appear inside a real client story, but they should not define the entire campaign’s audience or emotional promise.

The corporate-events example also needs a clearer wedding-business connection. It can demonstrate support for a venue owner, but it should not become a corporate-campaign tutorial for a wedding audience.

### C. Pack 3 remains too venue-specific in its broader argument

Whitney explicitly approved specific venue examples but asked for a wider takeaway. Here, the conclusions remain:

- Walk through your door.
- Walk onto your property.
- Attend the tour.
- Compare other venues.

That excludes planners, photographers, entertainment businesses and other wedding pros at the precise point where the piece should broaden.

**Recommendation:** Retain the venue details inside the story, then connect the principle to what happens between an inquiry and a tour or consultation.

### D. Offer boundaries are not clear enough

**Accelerators:** The launch is correctly described in several places, but the packs disproportionately sell account troubleshooting and “someone alongside you.” They omit the finite guided-implementation period and sometimes imply unrestricted future access.

**ELEVATE:** Pack 3 mostly sells an unnamed advertising partnership. Where ELEVATE finally appears, follow-up implementation is presented as part of its approach without explaining the separate platform.

**LeadLock:** It is named only late in the carousel. The repeated “our team built it” language can make WIA sound like a full-service CRM, nurture and operations agency. The copy says automation does not replace the owner, but that disclaimer does not resolve unclear service scope.

**Recommendation:** Verify the actual offer attached to each story, then state the relevant mechanics once, plainly. If selling ELEVATE, use the current waitlist route; do not imply immediate onboarding or an application.

### E. The CTA plan has not been reconciled with the campaign structure

- **Pack 1:** No offer CTA, but save/share/follow/self-audit endings are repetitive. Not every piece needs an engagement instruction.

- **Pack 3:** Reel 1 and the carousel both carry offer CTAs, with repeated asks in their captions. That exceeds RR-42’s one offer CTA across the week’s three Instagram pieces.

The deliverables also contain three Reels plus a carousel but no graphic, while the supplied posting order is graphic, Reel, carousel. These may be alternatives in a content bank, but the pack needs to say which assets form the actual week.
