### 1. Let local client knowledge shape the map

**Central point:** Geographic targeting is more useful when it reflects the venue owner's experience with both where clients come from and how well inquiries from different areas fit, rather than reach alone.

**Purpose/type:** WARM · process

**Audience:** Wedding venues and other location-dependent wedding businesses weighing whether to expand geographic targeting when a region produces inquiries but uncertain fit.

**Intended shift:** Consider inquiry quality and the owner's market knowledge alongside geographic reach when deciding which areas to target.

**Why worthwhile:** The discussion shows a specific strategic judgment: Lauren described both clients and less-qualified inquiries from the valley cities, then identified stronger ideal-client regions. Whitney used that context to recommend opening targeting toward other areas while leaving those four cities out. That exchange makes client knowledge a visible input to WIA's advertising judgment.

**Strategic reason:** A process-led example can build trust in how WIA combines advertising review with a client's knowledge of their market. It does not claim the recommendation improved results.

**Source demonstration:** Lauren says the four valley cities send some clients, but she has seen price sensitivity and many inquiries she considered not very high quality; she contrasts those areas with regions she associates more with ideal clients. Whitney then recommends adding the Bay Area, LA, and San Diego while leaving off the four valley cities.

**Development direction:** Show the sequence from the owner's mixed experience with the four cities, to her view of stronger ideal-client regions, to Whitney's specific recommendation. Keep the distinction between some clients coming from an area and that area being a strong fit.

**Tension:** The valley cities bring some clients, but the owner's experience also raises questions about the fit of inquiries from a previous campaign there.

**Offer connection:** Not required for this direction.

**Nearest alternative:** That is a meaningful but separate direction from the same moment. The geography point is stronger for this batch because it centers the owner's market knowledge and a concrete collaborative targeting recommendation; the campaign diagnosis would require its own careful treatment of tracking and apparent results.

Claim boundaries:

- Keep Lauren's inquiry-quality and price-sensitivity observations attributed to her; they are not quantified comparisons or proof that geography caused inquiry quality.
- Keep Whitney's recommendation specific to this venue and market. Do not present these locations as a general targeting rule.
- The call supports the recommendation, not implementation or a resulting change in inquiry quality.

### 2. Reviews and buying research answer different questions

**Central point:** Reviews can show what clients say about their experience, while questions about why they chose a wedding business can provide a different kind of insight for shaping advertising messages.

**Purpose/type:** ATTRACT · thought_shift

**Audience:** Wedding business owners who have reviews or testimonials but want clearer insight into why couples chose them.

**Intended shift:** Recognize that reviews and decision-focused research can serve different purposes, and consider what additional questions could inform marketing messages.

**Why worthwhile:** Whitney describes asking new clients for market research and says clients respond with reviews instead. She distinguishes those inputs and connects buyer triggers to ad copy and landing pages. This offers a useful, specific perspective for owners who have testimonials but want to understand what influenced a couple's decision.

**Strategic reason:** The distinction creates a useful point of view for owners unfamiliar with WIA: it explains why advertising strategy may benefit from learning about buyer decisions, not only collecting praise. Keep Whitney's observation about client responses in its stated context rather than presenting it as an industry-wide pattern.

**Source demonstration:** Whitney says that when WIA asks new Elevate clients whether they have market research or data to share, they often offer reviews. She says knowing buyer types and triggers could inform ad copy and landing pages.

**Development direction:** Contrast the questions each input can answer: a review offers a client's account of their experience, while a question about why they chose the business seeks decision context. Use Whitney's onboarding example and her proposed connection to ad copy and landing pages without claiming the proposed survey workflow was launched.

**Tension:** A business may have reviews to share while still lacking the decision context it wants for marketing.

**Offer connection:** Not required for this direction.

**Nearest alternative:** That alternative is not suitable as a public offer or process claim from this exchange: the workflow is proposed, and the differentiator claim is not supported by competitor research. The selected point uses the supported distinction and buyer-message connection without implying implementation.

Claim boundaries:

- Do not generalize Whitney's account of WIA onboarding conversations into a claim about all wedding professionals.
- Do not imply reviews have no value; the point is that reviews and decision-focused research can serve different purposes.
- The exchange supports a proposed use for research, not a demonstrated improvement in ads, landing pages, or business outcomes.
