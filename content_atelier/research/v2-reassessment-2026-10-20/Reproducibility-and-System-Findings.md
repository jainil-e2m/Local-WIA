## 0. Summary

The packs in this folder were made by hand, but the method can be written down, and almost all of it fits the architecture we already have.

**What was missing.** The missing ingredient was never another input source or a better copywriting prompt. It was an explicit editorial move applied when ideas are chosen, and an evaluation that compares output with Whitney's real work.

**The move, in one line:** decide what WIA's market should believe this window, find one human moment from the week that proves it, test that the *point* (not just the subject) belongs to WIA, then tell one story through several doors.

**What it changes in the plan:**
- **Keep the 7.2 proposals.** Full-week input, a weekly brief, the register, account memory and identity are all needed.
- **Change three of them:**
  - the weekly brief should output beliefs to prove, not funnel slots;
  - synthesis should stop ranking ideas by how often they recur;
  - the critic must see Whitney's finals.
- **Add five new items.** Point tests in the strategist; a "human line" scene type; a document of Whitney's transformation exemplars; a blind comparison with her finals; and a five-minute weekly "what stood out" note from Whitney.

**What still needs people:**
- Whitney choosing at Gate 1;
- permissions and consent;
- her confirmation when a pack takes a public stance (Pack 3's AI view);
- a light voice pass on scripts she will record herself.

We should say that plainly to WIA.

## 1. The method, as a procedure

This is what was actually done for the v2 packs, in order. Every step is something a stage can do. The note after each step says how much of it is automatable today.

### Step 1. Purpose before proof

For the publishing window (late October to November), list four to six beliefs WIA wants its market to hold, and the offer each one serves.

**Sources:**
- the content plan (knowledge base document 14);
- belief statements (16);
- Savannah's strategy, including the verbatim ideas in its ATTRACT and CONVERT pillars (01);
- the offer sheet (05);
- the published register (13), so a belief can be re-proved with a new moment but not repeated with the same one.

**For Week 2 this produced:**
- budget follows proof ("anyone who tells you that you have to spend more on ads… just wants your money", Savannah);
- the most useful part of a strategy call isn't about the ads (UVP: "Most agencies run your ads in a vacuum…");
- strategy is the skill, buttons are the easy part ("thinking like a strategist is the hard part… We teach both", Savannah, CONVERT);
- ELEVATE buyers are already investing and sense there's more (Whitney, 29 June).

*Automatable: yes.* This is what E.B28 should output. Today it is drafted as funnel slots ("we need an ATTRACT piece"), which leads to picking the most-evidenced topic for each slot.

### Step 2. The moment hunt

Read the full calls for four kinds of moment, and ignore topics:

| Kind | Week 2 example |
|---|---|
| **A human line:** a sentence carrying feeling or a business self-description | "I don't know what I've done. I don't know what we changed." (T11) · "On paper… it's just all fine… but… massive room for improvement." (T10) · "the ugliest day of the venue" (T02) |
| **A counter-expected WIA decision:** something that cost WIA money, a sale or an easy win, or went against the industry default | Resetting a new client's Google budget at about half, and turning off the biggest Meta campaign (T01). Telling Ivor he probably doesn't need LeadLock (T10) |
| **A WIA practice in Whitney's own framing** | "for a call like this, we can get the most out of… talking… about the things that are kind of beyond the ads" (T02) · "our goal… is to help you think like a strategist" (T11) |
| **Something the owner knows that a dashboard doesn't** | Couples who book more than two weeks out often go elsewhere; Saturdays are weddings; there's no separate indoor ceremony space (T02) |

*Automatable: mostly.* It needs a scene type and a ranking boost (E.B31, section 3.4). This is the step where the human lines get drowned today, because they are a tiny share of the transcript text.

### Step 3. Pair a moment with a belief, and write the point in one sentence

| Moment | Belief | Point (one sentence, Whitney's register) |
|---|---|---|
| The Penny budget reset | Budget follows proof | How we approach a new client's ad budget, and why "spend more" isn't our first move. |
| The Diamond A strategy call | Beyond the ads | What our strategy calls actually cover, and why the most useful part often isn't about the ads. |
| Noam's AI assistant | Strategy is the skill | How we help wedding pros understand their own ad accounts, now that AI can change them in minutes. |

*Automatable: yes, with exemplars.* This is the creative step, and models do it well when shown Whitney's own moment-to-belief chains (L.B12).

### Step 4. The gate: five tests on the point

| Test | Pass | Fail (Week 2 example) |
|---|---|---|
| **Point, not subject** | The point is a WIA belief, a standard, or what working with WIA is like | "Meta optimises for the action you choose." (Its subject is WIA's work, but its point is a platform behaviour) |
| **Surprise** | An established owner who has run ads wouldn't already know it | Campaign objectives |
| **Takeaway** | The reader comes away thinking "I want a team that thinks like this" or "I hadn't looked at it that way" | "Save this for your engagement-season planning" (the reader goes off to fix their own account) |
| **Reader** | Established, already doing the work. No desperation, and never an offer or ads presented as frustrating | "We're spending money, and we're not getting anything" from a WIA member |
| **Fit** | Serves the offer calendar; doesn't undercut a live promotion; isn't a recent repeat | "After your audit, who does the work?" while Carmen's giveaway audits are running |

*Automatable: yes, as structured fields plus a verifier* (E.B30). The verifier can reject points whose grammatical subject is a platform or setting, and takeaways phrased as instructions to the reader.

### Step 5. Compress the story

The story gets one moment and at most two concrete details. Credit the right person, state the belief once, memorably, and end on a standard or an invitation. Everything that turns the story into a sequence of checks gets cut: stage-by-stage analysis, click data, a list of settings.

*Automatable: partly.* It's a writer instruction plus a check on detail density. This is where the 20 October Pack 3 went wrong, even though its angle was right.

### Step 6. Plan the pack: one idea, different doors

Each format enters the same idea differently:
- the email tells the whole story;
- Reel 1 is the story with the hook;
- Reel 2 is a second scene or the decision itself;
- Reel 3 is text on b-roll;
- the carousel goes deeper.

Never split the idea into sub-lessons to fill slots. If the idea only supports two Reels, make two.

*Automatable: yes.* It's a pack-plan schema in 5.B13.

### Step 7. Write in her register

- **Scripts** are modelled on her recorded Reel transcripts. The 18 August 10-week Reel and the $70K Reel are the best samples of her spoken voice.
- **Emails** are modelled on her sent emails.
- **Banned constructions** come from her team's ChatGPT corrections, not just the lint list:
  - paired contrasts ("It's not X, it's Y") at most once per piece;
  - no aphoristic kicker endings;
  - no staccato runs;
  - repeated ideas, never repeated lines across formats.

*Automatable: mostly.* Expect Gate 2 edits on scripts.

### Step 8. Verify

- Every quote against the transcript.
- Every "we built" against later records.
- Every person's status and every identity.
- One permission list per pack, with no more than five items.

*Automatable: yes.* This already exists in the ledger and verifier. Account memory adds the later records.

### Step 9. Evaluate against her work, not only a rubric

The blind comparison in the diagnosis (section 7) is the model: a judge that reads Whitney's real finals and her feedback first, then compares candidate packs.

*Automatable: yes, as a diagnostic* (E.X8). WIA's ratings remain the success measure, as the plan already says.

## 4. Could the planned system produce these three ideas? A walk-through

| v2 idea | What the system would need to see | Which stage finds it | Why it passes the new tests and the old idea fails |
|---|---|---|---|
| **How we approach a new client's ad budget** (T01) | The budget turns: 14:00 to 16:47 and 33:13 to 35:40 | A counter-expected decision scene (E.B31) paired with the brief's belief "budget follows proof" (E.B28) | Its point is a WIA judgment, and it surprises. "Meta takes instructions literally" fails both the point test and the surprise test |
| **What our strategy calls actually cover** (T02, plus later records) | Whitney's 42:03 line; the owner's human lines; what was built by 9 September | Full-call input (E.B27), human-line scenes (E.B31), account memory (3.B17), the UVP belief | Its point is the client experience and a standard. | **How we help wedding pros understand their own ad accounts** (T11, plus T07; the 24 September email as background) | Noam's human line; Whitney's "think like a strategist"; the office-hours "why" | A human-line scene paired with Savannah's verbatim CONVERT belief; optional cross-source support from account memory | Its point is the skill the Accelerators teach. The old office-hours pack fails the reader test |

**Honest assessment:**
- **Ideas: credible.** The planned system, with E.B27, E.B28 (changed), E.B30, E.B31 and L.B12, has a credible path to proposing all three. The first two are the strongest bets. The AI idea depends more on the pairing step, so expect it to appear in the ranked list but perhaps not at the top.
- **Copy: likely to need editing.** The packs' copy is the least reliably automatable part. A brief and pack generated from these cards should get to "publish after light edits" for emails and carousels, but scripts will need a voice pass. We shouldn't promise WIA otherwise.

## 6. Owner decisions this adds

1. **Week 2 as a benchmark.** Keep it out of prompts (recommended), or retire it.
2. **Founder notes.** Ask Whitney for a five-minute weekly "what stood out" note. Its value is high and its cost is low.
3. **Stance confirmation.** Cards that take a public position (for example, AI) carry a flag that requires Whitney's confirmation before development.
4. **Pack size.** Allow two Reels when an idea doesn't support three. The client asked for three strong angles a week, not for fifteen Reels.

## 8. From the tone and format review

After the owner reviewed v2, a second pass compared every format with Whitney's own finished pieces. The full findings are in section 8 of the diagnosis, and in section 14.8 of `Content-Atelier-Findings-and-System-Improvements.md`.

**What they mean for the system:**
- **Angle titles.** The strategist's title rule contradicts the client's own model, and should be replaced (rec 28).
- **The angle card.** It should be her tab, at her length (rec 29).
- **The format guide.** Knowledge base 09 already holds most format rules. It needs enforcing as checks at writing time, not loading as background (rec 30).
- **The writer prompt.** It needs a register per format (rec 31) and approved offer descriptors (rec 32).
- **The pack plan.** It should compose Reels as a story, a stance and a b-roll text Reel (rec 33).

**Human judgment needed:** limited. Every one of these can be written down and checked. What stays human is Whitney's final voice pass on scripts she records herself.
