# Why the WIA packs keep missing: a diagnosis

**Internal. Not for the client.** It names clients, prospects and private figures.
**Prepared:** 20 October 2026, for the 21 October presentation and the implementation plan.
**Companion files:**
- `WIA-Content-Packs-Week-of-8-14-June-2026-v2.md`: the rebuilt client packs.
- `Reproducibility-and-System-Findings.md`: what this means for the system.

The previous deliverables in `deliverables_2026-10-21/` are untouched, so they can be compared directly with the new packs.

## 0. The answer, on one page

**The question.** Why does our content still feel fundamentally different from what Whitney creates, when we have her conversations, strategy, feedback and ChatGPT history?

**The answer.** We start from the call and ask what in it is worth saying, then add WIA to the answer. Whitney starts from what she wants her market to believe about WIA, and uses one real moment as the proof. Every round so far, including the 20 October rebuild, kept the first method and improved its surface.

That shows up as four concrete differences:

| | What we have been doing | What Whitney does |
|---|---|---|
| **The point** | WIA is the *subject* ("we set up Meta campaigns to…"), but the *point* is a lesson about a platform or a procedure | The point is a belief about how a wedding business grows, or a standard for what a client should expect from WIA |
| **The layer of the call** | The technical layer: objectives, stages, sitelinks, click thresholds | The human layer: one sentence a client said, a feeling, a decision |
| **The reader** | The person who has the problem: boosters, a member whose campaign isn't working | The established owner who is already doing the work and senses there's more |
| **The selection** | Ideas backed by the most evidence and recurring across calls | Ideas that would surprise someone who has already run ads, and that serve what she's selling |

**Why it persisted.** Each round fixed the symptom named in the previous round's feedback. The rules describing the goal are already correct and already in the system:
- the plan's section 2.2 says "WIA is the subject", "zoom out" and "established owners";
- strategist v6 says the same, with a worked example.

But nothing tests the point of an idea, only its subject. So an idea can satisfy every rule literally and still teach a setting.

The 20 October rebuild is the clearest evidence of this:
- it had all eleven recordings, the knowledge base, the export and that prompt;
- it still chose "Meta takes your instructions literally" (platform education) and a step-by-step office-hours troubleshooting session.

Its own headline finding was input completeness: excerpts instead of full calls, no synthesis, no memory. Those fixes are necessary, but this run shows they are not sufficient.

**Where the problem sits:**
1. **Opportunity selection and strategic interpretation (the angle stage).** This is the main one.
2. **Pack planning.** Three Reels per idea, each required to be distinct, splits one story into micro-lessons.
3. **Copy.** AI constructions and forensic detail.

The source material was never the problem. The week contains three strong, Whitney-shaped ideas, and they are in the new packs.

### 2.2 Where her judgment shows: what she corrects

Her corrections in the export are mostly about positioning and audience, much less about wording.

**Positioning:**

**Audience:**
- "That hook speaks to someone who's in a place of desperation. That is not our ideal client."
- "We do want people who are making sales, not struggling."
- "We need to speak to those with a growth mindset, not a lack mindset."
- "Your opening makes it sound like advertising is always frustrating… definitely not a position we want to take."

**Craft:**
- "I'd really like to see the captions you write be more wedding industry specific."

- "By repeat, I meant repeat ideas, not repeat language."
- "You took way too much liberty… Stay true to the first version."

**What these corrections show:**
- **She pre-selects the human moment herself.** Her raw input to ChatGPT is usually her own summary of a call, and it already names what mattered: "What stood out most to me on the call was that they told me…" Our pipeline has to do that selection, and it doesn't know to.
- **Her process runs from purpose to proof.** On 8 June the market-research training became marketing only after she decided what it should prove about WIA.
- **Her own ChatGPT named our failure in words she endorsed.** On 13 October, in her account: "They're analyzing the conversations to identify what was discussed, when they should be analyzing them to identify what the conversations reveal about how WIA helps wedding businesses succeed… we need to show people more of what we actually do. Not just tell them what they should be doing differently… That's the difference between demonstrating knowledge and demonstrating expertise in action." Her reply was a list of examples: "how we help wedding pros refine their landing page messaging… our process for keyword research… how we help venues position themselves… what [Zadie] was impressed with."

### 2.3 Her rules for the reader, combined

From the 13 October call and her written note:
- "I met with a venue owner recently who was having this problem… does that sound familiar to you? This is how we talked through it… That's what your expectation should be of working with… an advertising partner… as opposed to us telling them, here's what you need to do to be able to diagnose your own problem."
- "In all cases, the ideas are zoomed out, less specific, and far less educational/academic sounding."

"Zoom out" is about the point, not the audience. She is happy for the example to be specific: "I'm good with specificity there". So the specificity she objects to is specificity of the lesson.

## 3. Where the 20 October packs failed, pack by pack

### Pack 1 "Meta takes your instructions literally" (ATTRACT)

**The point is a platform behaviour.**
- The core perspective is "Meta optimises for the action you choose. Choose the inquiry."
- Anyone who has run Meta ads knows campaign objectives exist, so it fails the surprise test for the audience Whitney wants.
- The takeaway is do-it-yourself:
  - "Save this for your engagement-season planning."
  - "Send this to the wedding pro friend who's about to boost their engagement-season post."

**The audience is inverted.** The pack's lead scene is Ivor (T10). In the transcript, Ivor:
- runs an elopement business doing "$500 to $700 or so a year" (weddings);
- spends "$100,000 or so a year in ads";
- has built his own automated booking flow ("It's like going through a line at Chipotle");
- said: "on paper… it's just all fine… but I think there's massive room for improvement."

That is Whitney's ELEVATE buyer, word for word ("already trying, but… know in their gut there is missing potential"). The pack recast him as "a wedding pro… 'I feel like I'm boosting, you know?'", a beginner's cautionary tale for the "Stop Boosting Posts" series. The one line chosen from a 35-minute call was the only line where Whitney explains a platform.

**There is a positioning conflict.** The pack says WIA asks Meta for the lead "through a lead form or an inquiry form on a landing page." Knowledge base document 07 records Whitney on 6 June: "We do not recommend lead form ads on Meta. We send traffic to a long form ads landing page to help them self-qualify." She did mention lead forms on T10, but her stated policy matters more for public copy.

**The week's strongest WIA judgment became a footnote to the Meta lesson.** On The Penny call (T01), Whitney:
- reset a new client's Google budget at about half because "Google can sometimes just assume that you're just okay with paying more per lead";
- turned off the Meta campaign spending the most (over $500, no tracked leads): "it doesn't make sense to spend just to spend";
- planned to "start generating leads at a lower budget… and then start to scale up from there."

An ads agency lowering a new client's spend is surprising, trust-building, and close to the founder-forward idea in Savannah's strategy ("Anyone who tells you that… you have to spend more on ads is BS"). The old pack used it as evidence that "Meta takes instructions literally."

### Pack 2 "Office hours: turn it off, or give it more time?" (WARM)

**The hook is a WIA member describing a failing WIA-supported campaign.** "We're spending money, and we're not getting anything." That breaks two of Whitney's explicit rules: never present advertising or an offer as frustrating, and never write to desperation.

**The body is a troubleshooting procedure, written in the third person.** It walks through the checks in order:
- click-through rate as a signal of interest;
- a 75 to 100 click checkpoint;
- visit recordings to tell an ad problem from a page problem;
- account-level sitelinks;
- a leftover wedding banner;
- a thank-you page redirect.

This is the "here's what you need to do to be able to diagnose your own problem" structure Whitney rejected on 13 October, now presented as "what Carmen did."

**There is a privacy risk.** The member is the anonymised $22K-to-$70K case-study client. A detailed account of her corporate campaign adds linkage risk for little gain.

**There is internal context against it.** T03 records an Accelerator member who struggled quietly and later wrote that she was "really unimpressed". Content about Accelerator campaigns not working is the wrong public story right now.

### Pack 3 "When more inquiries aren't enough" (CONVERT)

This is the pack closest to Whitney's instruction. She liked the angle on 13 October. Its failure is subtler, and it matters because it shows that following her literal description isn't enough.

**The point is a service claim.** "A good advertising partner should care what happens to your inquiries" is true of every agency's sales page. It isn't a belief that anyone would disagree with or remember.

**The content is still organised as a diagnostic sequence.** It runs: stages tracked in LeadLock, then the owner's context, then follow-up email click data, then rain-plan photos, then ideas, then the build. Carousel slides 4 to 8 repeat the investigative steps of the 13 October carousel.

**Forensic detail piles up until the story reads like a case report:**
- the weather email's clicks;
- the iPad;
- the two-week window;
- Saturdays;
- Sundays;
- staff with babies;
- the indoor ceremony space.

Whitney's own stories use one or two concrete details.

**The kicker lines are the constructions her team bans:**
- "None of that replaces her. It keeps her in the conversation…"
- "It doesn't replace her. It just makes sure couples hear from her…"
- "It's automated, but it's still her."

### Across all three packs

- **Format bloat produced micro-lessons.** The document runs to 16,100 words, with three Reels per idea that each had to be distinct. So Reels became sub-lessons: Pack 1 Reel 3 on retargeting, Pack 3 Reel 2 on the rain plan. Whitney's own campaigns tell one story, through a different door per format.
- **The front matter is a feedback checklist.** It is a table mapping each criticism to a fix. That is honest, but it is also a symptom: each criticism was treated as a separate box to tick.
- **There are aphoristic AI kickers:**
  - "But a click isn't an inquiry. And it's definitely not a booking."
  - "Meta is great at following instructions. Somebody has to write good ones."
  - "It's the same platform with completely different instructions, and the instructions are where the strategy lives."

  The project lint passes these, because they aren't on the list, but the team's ChatGPT corrections reject exactly this register ("AI common phrase").

## 4. Why the gap persisted

### 4.1 Every round fixed the last symptom

| Round | What was produced | What WIA said | What the next round changed |
|---|---|---|---|
| Week 1 angles | Topic extraction ("Give distinct wedding markets distinct landing page context") | "1's across the board… missing what those conversations demonstrate" | WIA fields added to angles |
| 13 Oct angles and packs (written by the developer in Whitney's ChatGPT) | Angles about WIA's review process; packs walking through the review | Angles "night and day"; packs "a little bit more like how-to educational… leans more into like DIY"; hooks not punchy; voice not hers | Narrate "what we did"; problem-led subjects; casual scripts |
| 20 Oct rebuild | Every criticism answered in a table | (The owner's judgment: the same mistakes) | (This document) |

Each fix was correct and local. None changed the selection criterion, so the ideas themselves never improved.

### 4.2 The rules describe the goal; nothing tests the point

Plan section 2.2 and strategist v6 already say the right things. Pack 1 satisfies each literally:
- WIA is the subject: "we build Meta campaigns…";
- the audience is zoomed out to all wedding pros;
- the reader is described as established.

And it still teaches campaign objectives.

Strategist v6's main filter is step 2: "name the WIA move… A scene with no WIA move and no belief is an educational takeaway." But every technical scene contains a WIA move, so the filter passes everything. The missing test is about the reader's takeaway. After reading, would an established owner think "I should change this in my account"? That's a fail. Or "I want a team that thinks like this" / "I hadn't looked at it that way"? That's a pass.

### 4.3 Ranking by evidence strength picks the wrong ideas

The 20 October selection scored "Source strength: is it backed by more than one moment or more than one call?" and built "through-lines" from several calls. That systematically favours what gets discussed most in account calls, and that is campaign mechanics: Meta's three-phase structure appears in T01, T02 and T10.

Whitney's six finals are each built on a single moment. An idea's evidence needs to be true and specific. It doesn't need to be frequent.

### 4.4 The human lines are a tiny share of the transcript

Calls between an ads team and its clients are mostly technical by volume. The sentences that carry an idea are a handful of lines:
- **T10:** 186 lines, and the one that defines Ivor is a single line.
- **T11:** "I don't know what I've done. I don't know what we changed."
- **T02:** "the ugliest day of the venue", and Whitney's "we can get the most out of… talking… about the things that are kind of beyond the ads."

Any process that summarises, counts or retrieves by topic similarity drowns them. Whitney's raw input to ChatGPT already contains the selection ("what stood out most to me…"), and ours doesn't.

### 4.5 We studied her rules, not her moves

The previous ChatGPT-export analysis is careful and long (84K characters), and the knowledge base's editing guide is good. Both turn her finals into constraints: what not to repeat, which hooks she rejected, banned phrases.

Neither captures the transformation from raw moment to published piece, which is where the judgment lives:
- knowledge base document 15's exemplars are truncated snippets;
- there is no worked "her summary → her published version" chain anywhere in the system.

### 4.6 The critics couldn't see what mattered

The GPT-6.1 Sol critic scored the 20 October packs 2 to 3 out of 5 without the transcripts or any of Whitney's finals. The lint checks phrases. No evaluator compared the packs with her actual work, or asked whether an experienced advertiser would already know the point.

### 4.7 Packs require format completeness, so ideas get split

Requiring one email, three Reels and a carousel per idea, all distinct, pushes the writer to find five sub-points. When the idea is thin, the sub-points become tactics.

The same week, the same evidence discipline, and the same format (an email, three Reels with captions, a carousel with a caption) are kept. One Reel in each pack is now a text-on-b-roll Reel, which is how Whitney often posts ("You can't book clients who never find you"). The document is about 10,500 words against 16,100.

|---|---|---|---|

It is Savannah's founder-forward idea, proven by a real call. |

Forensic detail cut: no stage analysis, no email click data. |
| Noam: AI assistant; four good leads, then quiet; "I don't know what I've done"; Whitney's "think like a strategist". Office hours: "I wanted to understand the why" | Noam in one Reel; office hours as troubleshooting | **Pack 3 (CONVERT): "How we help wedding pros understand their own ad accounts, now that AI can change them in minutes."** The point is that AI makes it easy to change your ads and easy to lose track of what changed; understanding your own account is what the Accelerators build | Timely, a real stance (backed by Whitney's 24 September reply to Ali's AI-agent note), and it sells the Accelerators on judgment rather than features |

### Line-level comparisons

**Opening a Reel on the same Penny moment:**
- **20 Oct:** "When we start with a new client, one of the first things I look at is what their ads are already doing. And this summer, with a venue we'd just started working with, the Meta campaign spending the most money hadn't produced a single tracked lead."
- **New:** "One of the first things we did for a new venue client this summer was lower her ad budget. Which, I know, is not what you'd expect to hear from an ads agency."
- **Why:** the first opens on a platform finding. The second opens on WIA doing something unexpected, which is the reason to keep watching.

**The Diamond A carousel's first slide** (Whitney asked for "our ads brought in more inquiries… but there's still a layer beyond that"):
- **20 Oct:** "When more inquiries and more tours still aren't moving the needle. Our ads were bringing this venue more of both. Here's what we looked at next."
- **New:** "Our ads brought her more inquiries and more tours. Then we spent her strategy call talking about everything else."
- **Why:** both follow her note. The first promises an investigation (what we looked at). The second promises a different kind of partner.

**The Diamond A closing principle:**
- **20 Oct:** "That's the standard we hold ourselves to… a good advertising partner should care what happens next, bring ideas that fit your business, and build what's in their lane."
- **New (email):** "A lot of agencies will run your campaigns and send you a report on cost per lead. Those numbers matter, and we look at them closely every month. But some of the most valuable work happens when we understand your business well enough to help more of those inquiries become tours, consultations and conversations."
- **Why:** the first could be any agency's values page. The second positions WIA against the agency norm, as Whitney's own 10 June caption does, and is grounded in her words on the call ("we can get the most out of… talking… about the things that are kind of beyond the ads"), and it's memorable.

**The Accelerator promise:**
- **20 Oct (Pack 2 email):** "That's the part of running your own ads that's hard to learn from a video. The settings can be taught. The judgment comes from working through real decisions on your own account…"
- **New (Pack 3 email):** "When you can rewrite every headline and adjust every setting in a few minutes, it's very easy to lose track of which change did what. The hard part was never making the change. It's knowing which one is right for your business, and being able to look at your account afterwards and understand what happened."
- **Why:** the old line had the right idea buried at the end of a troubleshooting story. The new pack makes it the point, and grounds it in what actually happened to a real owner rather than in a slogan (an earlier draft used "AI made the doing fast; the deciding is the skill", which the blind review rightly called stock; see section 7).

## 6. What I'm not sure of, and what still needs Whitney

- **These packs are not validated by WIA.** I judged them against her published work, and an independent reviewer compared both sets blind (section 7). Neither is a substitute for her.
- **Pack 3 depends on her stance on AI.** The evidence is her calm, non-hostile handling on the Noam call and in the 24 September email. She should confirm she's happy to take a public position.
- **Pack 1 depends on The Penny being comfortable,** and any result line depends on the account's current state. A 28 September note says "It looks like the penny we need to take a look at."
- **Pack 2 uses identifying details:** the indoor ceremony space and "ugliest day". Ali should be asked.
- **Week 2 has almost no client outcomes.** Whitney's strongest finals are outcome stories, and these packs substitute standards for outcomes (as she suggested on 13 October). An outcome-rich week will produce stronger content than this one can.
- **The internal calls hold real philosophy, but most of it can't be published.** For example, Whitney's refusal to "nickel and dime" full-service clients (T08), and "use us as guinea pigs" before a client (T09). Most of it comes tangled with an unhappy client or named people. I used none of it in public copy.

## 7. Independent blind review

**How it was run.** A separate agent read three things first:
- five of Whitney's finals, verbatim from the export;
- the editing guide;
- her 13 October call, and her rewrite tab in the Angle Review.

It then judged the 20 October packs ("Set A") and the first draft of the v2 packs ("Set B"). It wasn't told which set was which, or that either was newer. Scores are 1 to 5, and the verdict column is the reviewer's prediction of what Whitney would decide.

| Pack | Point belongs to WIA | Surprise | Audience | Strategic use | Sounds like her | Free of AI tells | Hooks | Her likely decision |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **20 Oct** Meta takes instructions literally | 2 | 2 | 2 | 3 | 3 | 2 | 3 | **Reject the idea** ("a tactic lesson any Meta tutorial could carry") |
| **20 Oct** Office hours | 3 | 2 | 1 | 3 | 2 | 2 | 2 | **Reject the idea** ("the DIY how-to she already turned down"; leads an Accelerator pack with a member's zero inquiries) |
| **20 Oct** When more inquiries aren't enough | 4 | 3 | 4 | 4 | 3 | 3 | 4 | Light edits (cut the repeated stage and build lists; Reel 2's "belongs in your ads" isn't supported) |
| **v2 draft** Spend less | 4 | 4 | 4 | 4 | 3 | 2 | 4 | Light edits |
| **v2 draft** Sundays | **5** | 3 | 4 | 4 | **4** | 3 | 4 | Light edits, with one factual fix to the hook ("the best idea in either set") |
| **v2 draft** AI | 3 | 3 | 3 | 4 | 3 | 2 | 3 | **Rewrite the copy, keep the idea** ("'Use AI for the doing. Build the judgment for the deciding.' is a LinkedIn line") |

**The reviewer's overall verdict:** "Set B, clearly on the ideas and moderately on the copy. Neither set is publishable without edits." On the same material, the 20 October set teaches "Meta optimises for the objective you choose," while the v2 set shows "an ads agency said yes to spending less". It also reads Diamond A as "what we fixed" where v2 reads it as "how we work with you."

**What the review caught in my own draft, and what I did about it.** These go in the diagnosis because they're the same failure modes, in smaller form:

1. **A hook that overclaimed who decided.**
   - **The draft:** "Our first recommendation was to spend less." But our team asked how she felt about her budget, and she said she wanted it lower.
   - **Now:** "We lowered her ad spend before launching anything new," with "we asked… she'd love to bring it down" in the body, and a publish note that forbids changing it back.
2. **A hook that inflated a detail.**
   - **The draft:** "We spent most of that call talking about her Sundays."
   - **Now:** "we spent most of the call on everything except her ads, her Sundays included", which is what the transcript shows.
   - Her "ugliest day" phrase is no longer quoted.
3. **A stock idea-sentence in Pack 3.**
   - **The draft:** "AI made the doing fast; the deciding is the skill."
   - **Now:** the copy is built on what actually happened to her ("five campaigns… I don't know what we changed") and on Whitney's own words from the call ("something's working, and then it stops working, and you don't really know why"). The insight is now "AI makes it easy to change things, and just as easy to lose track of what changed."
   - The subject no longer says the AI "ran" her ads; it helped set them up.
   - The ELEVATE client's AI-agent email (slide 7) is cut, because it blurred the two offers.
4. **Kicker density.** About ten aphoristic closers were cut or rewritten across Packs 1 and 3. At most one paired contrast remains per piece, never in a hook. The lint now shows zero hits.
5. **An invented platform claim.** "Slashing a budget overnight can shock a campaign as much as overspending can" is replaced with what Whitney said: it would have been "too drastic."
6. **The same title repeated as subject, cover and carousel title.** Each format now has its own title.

**What the review means for the system.** Two of these six problems were hooks whose claims went beyond the evidence (who decided; how much of a call something took). A rule-based lint can't catch that. A ledger-backed hook-fidelity check can, so it's added to the copy checks in the system findings (E.B24, section 3.3).

The review was run once, on the first draft. The fixes above were made against its specific findings. **They haven't been re-reviewed, and Whitney's judgment is still the only real test.**

## 8. Tone and format review (after the owner's review of v2)

The owner reviewed the v2 packs and agreed with the evidence and direction, but not with the tone, the titles, or the format of the angles, briefs and packs. So I went back to the export for Whitney's finished pieces, and compared format by format:
- her approved $22K-to-$70K carousel (4 August);
- her 10-week carousel (13 August);
- her 10 June email, carousel and caption;
- her 1 October approved caption;
- her recorded Reel transcripts;
- knowledge base 09, the format guide.

The findings are below. The packs were rewritten against them, and the version before this review is kept in `superseded/`.

**T1. Angle titles should describe WIA's work plainly.**
- Every angle in Whitney's preferred-angles tab is a descriptive title of WIA's work: "How we help wedding venues position their value…", "What our keyword research process looks like, and why client conversations are part of it", "What goes into our wedding venue advertising strategy beyond creating attractive ads".
- Strategist v6 says the opposite: "A title names the idea or the tension in it, not the fact that WIA does something. At most one title in the slate may open with 'Why we', 'What we', 'How we' or 'We'."
- The first v2 draft followed the v6 style and used hook titles ("Her ads were working, so we talked about her Sundays"). In her model, the hook belongs in the copy, and the angle title says what WIA does.

**T2. The angle card should use her tab's fields, at her length.**
- Her fields: content pillar / type, audience, why this matters, WIA's expertise or process, evidence in the referenced call, editorial guardrail / next step.
- She uses one to three sentences per field. The v2 draft's cards were long internal evidence dumps.

**T3. The brief is part of the deliverable.**
- The client reviewed a brief structure on 13 October and criticised its content (how-to drift), not its structure.
- The first v2 draft dropped the brief altogether. The owner restated the deliverable: an angle, a brief, an email, three Reels and a carousel.

**T4. Savannah's framework has to be visible in every caption.**
- She checks it constantly: "Did you actually follow the caption structure I prescribed?" (25 Jun), "Does this follow Savannah's framework?" (15 Sep, 1 Oct), "Are you aware of the content/caption formula…" (4 Aug).
- The step most often missing is problems, desires and symptoms: the reader's own situation, in wedding-specific detail ("They're opening multiple tabs… comparing packages…"), before the story. The v2 draft captions mostly retold the story.

**T5. The register depends on the format.**
- "A little more casual, a little less polished" was her note on Reel scripts (13 October, 19:38).
- Her sent emails and approved captions are clean, confident and declarative. The v2 draft applied the casual register everywhere ("Okay, so…").
- Her recorded Reels open on a crisp first line: "70K in new wedding bookings…", "If I can't tell from looking at your website…", "What would happen to your wedding business if…".

**T6. Offers are named, explained, and sold without apology.**
- "I'm looking for conversions. I'd like to sell in the caption but in a very intentional, non-sleazy way" (10 Jun).
- She uses fixed descriptors: "ELEVATE, our full-service advertising management program"; the Accelerators "for venues and wedding professionals who want to manage their own advertising".
- She positions WIA against "most agencies" ("Most agencies jump straight into targeting… We go deeper."), but never against the reader ("I really don't want this to be about how wedding pros are getting their marketing wrong").
- The v2 draft barely named an offer.

**T7. Her carousels have a recognisable shape.**
- **Length and slide 1:** 10 to 12 slides, and slide 1 is an insight about the reader ("You don't need another reminder to 'show up consistently.'").
- **The story** takes two to four slides.
- **The reader's own situation and the reframe** take several slides ("How can I keep up…" becomes "How much of my marketing still requires me…").
- **The close:** a belief slide ("We believe every wedding business deserves that kind of clarity"), then a CTA that fits both DIY and done-for-you readers ("Whether you're looking for someone to manage your ads… or… learn how to manage them yourself… DM us 'ADS'").
- **Voice and density:** "we" and "us" throughout (4 Aug), and real text on every slide.
- The v2 draft's carousels spent most of their slides on operational steps.

**T8. Many of these rules were already written down, and still weren't applied.**
- Knowledge base 09 (the format guide) already records several:
  - email send metadata (DATE, SEND TIME, RECIPIENTS);
  - flowing email paragraphs rather than bullets;
  - "-Whitney" on first-person captions;
  - covers planned as a varied set;
  - real text on every carousel slide;
  - the caption framework.
- The v2 draft broke all of them, because the writer treated the guide as background rather than as a checklist. This is the same pattern as V9: rules that exist but aren't enforced at the moment of writing.

**What changed in the packs:**
- **Angles:** descriptive titles in her tab's fields.
- **Briefs:** restored, using the 13 October structure with Savannah's narrative outline.
- **Captions:** rebuilt on Savannah's framework, with the reader's situation before the story.
- **Emails:** send metadata, flowing paragraphs, offers named with her descriptors, CTAs varied (reply, reply, sales-page link).
- **Scripts:** open on a crisp line.
- **Reels:** each pack now has a story Reel, a stance Reel and a b-roll text Reel.
- **Carousels:** reshaped to her pattern, in "we", with a belief slide and a two-path CTA.

The evidence, the claims and the ideas are unchanged.
