# Pack 1 · ATTRACT

## Content angle

### How we approach a new client's ad budget, and why "spend more" isn't our first move

**Content pillar / type:** ATTRACT · Founder-forward and industry authority. This is Savannah's idea that anyone who says you have to spend more on ads to make more "just wants your money," shown through a real decision.

**Audience:** Established wedding pros who are already investing in Google or Meta, whether they run their ads themselves or work with someone, and whose budget has grown faster than their results.

**Why this matters:** The most common advice in our industry, and from the ad platforms themselves, is to spend more. But a bigger budget only helps once you know what your current budget is actually doing.

**WIA's expertise or process:** When we start with a new client, we look at what their existing campaigns are producing before we talk about spending more. We stop what isn't earning its place, reset what has drifted, and put more money behind what proves itself.

**Evidence in the referenced call:** This comes from a new ELEVATE venue's strategy call.
- **The budget.** We asked how the director felt about her budget. She said it had "jumped up quite a bit" without "way better, stronger leads."
- **Google.** Her campaigns were performing. But you explained that a climbing budget can lead Google to "assume that you're just okay with paying more per lead," and reset Google on a fresh campaign at about half the daily budget.
- **Meta.** You turned off the campaign spending the most (over $500, no tracked leads): "it doesn't make sense to spend just to spend."
- **The plan.** "Start generating leads at a lower budget… and then start to scale up from there."

**Editorial guardrail / next step:**
- Her Google ads were working, so say "reset," never "fixed."
- No budgets and no venue name.
- No result without her permission.
- Never criticise whoever built the earlier campaigns.

## Content brief

**Central point.** More budget is something your ads should earn. Before we put more money behind a client's advertising, we find out what their current spend is actually doing.

**Strategic purpose.** ATTRACT. Reach established owners who are already spending on ads, and build trust that WIA isn't an agency chasing bigger budgets. Desired response: *"That's the kind of team I'd trust with my ad budget."*

**Audience and reason to care.** Owners whose spend has crept up a little at a time while their inquiries haven't noticeably improved, and who are tired of platforms suggesting a bigger daily budget. They believe in advertising and want it to grow their business. They just don't want to waste money doing it.

**WIA perspective.** We're big believers in investing in growth. But ads amplify whatever is already there.
- If a campaign is working, more budget can help it reach more of the right couples.
- If it isn't, more budget just means it spends more, faster.

So the budget should follow the proof.

**Supporting example.** The Penny's ads strategy call:
- 14:00 to 16:47: the budget.
- 31:03 to 35:40: targeting, and the campaign turned off.
- 27:05 to 42:37: the rebuilt Meta structure.

**Narrative outline.**
- **Hook:** An ads agency lowering a new client's budget.
- **Problems, desires, symptoms:** Your budget has crept up. Traffic looks good. Your inquiries aren't noticeably better. The platforms keep suggesting you spend more.
- **Main point:** Budget should follow proof. Find out what each campaign is doing before you scale it.
- **Transformation:** Knowing what every part of your budget is doing, and having a real basis for deciding when to spend more.
- **CTA:** Follow on Instagram. Reply to the email.

**Format and creative approach.**
- **Email:** the whole story, a clear statement of how WIA approaches budget, and a question back to the reader.

  1. the reader's situation;
  2. the story;
  3. the principle;
  4. a CTA for both ways of working with WIA.

**CTA and essential boundaries.**
