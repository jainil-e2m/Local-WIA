## Strategic assignment

**Audience:** Wedding venues and service businesses with multi-step sales conversations who fear automated messages will feel impersonal or arrive at the wrong moment.

**Objective:** Show how a repeatable follow-up process can retain human judgment and make a sales-workflow conversation feel useful.

**Funnel & pillar:** Warm → Convert · Unique Processes · Solution Selling

**Reader promise:** See a practical way to combine timely inquiry handling with control over what happens next, and identify one workflow worth reviewing.

**Belief shift:** From “I must choose between manual follow-up and a fully timed sequence” to “automate repeatable actions around the stages I deliberately choose.”

**What makes this angle distinct:** This is a sales-process design lesson about triggers and human review, not a product feature launch, migration success story, or generic speed-to-lead claim.

**Natural offer fit:** LeadLock. The final invitation follows the CTA direction below.

## Core insight

A thoughtful sales system makes timely actions repeatable while leaving room for the judgment a wedding inquiry deserves.

## Audience pain

You want inquiries answered promptly, but worry that automatic messages will ignore availability, questions, or the conversation you have already had.

## Message spine

1. Recognise the tension between keeping up with inquiries and making every exchange feel considered.
2. Introduce the real request: send the first email, then let the team choose when the next sequence begins.
3. Explain a stage-based workflow with explicit ownership, pauses, and checks for reply or booking.
4. Before moving tools, review what each message is supposed to accomplish and preserve the parts that serve the buyer.

## Narrative framework

**Hook:** Start with the customer request: send the first inquiry email automatically, but let the owner choose when later messages begin.

**Problem:** An owner wants reliable follow-up without messages arriving after a conversation has changed. Name the tension between memory and an inflexible timer.

**Main point:** Map the buyer’s next decision, identify the human review point, and attach repeatable actions to a meaningful stage. Describe this as a workflow concept.

**Transformation:** The owner can define what should happen, who decides, and which events require a pause or a different next step.

**CTA:** Invite readers to identify the follow-up they still control manually. A LeadLock conversation can follow once configuration and scope are confirmed.

### Email

An empathetic explanation of how repeatable actions and human judgment can work in the same inquiry system.

**Reader job:** Choose which decision needs their review and which action can follow consistently.

**Length:** 400–550 words; one conceptual inquiry journey and one optional, approved commercial next step.

**Subject-line directions — proposed, not approved copy:**

- Keep control of the next conversation
- What should trigger the next email?
- An inquiry system with room for judgment

**Opening direction:** Introduce a request our team discussed, not a conversation Whitney personally had. Paraphrase anonymously by default and clearly state that the workflow is being designed.

**Outline:**

1. Validate the concern about a sequence sending the wrong message after a meaningful reply.
2. Explain the first-response-plus-human-review request.
3. Map a fictional inquiry: acknowledgment, availability/fit review, chosen next stage, and a relevant message.
4. Name pause and exception checks that the implementer must confirm; do not present an unverified feature tutorial.
5. Ask what the reader needs to decide personally before the next follow-up, then offer a LeadLock discussion only with confirmed scope and destination.

**Email CTA:** Primary: reply with the follow-up that still needs personal judgment. Optional Convert ending: an approved LeadLock inquiry invitation; choose one ending, not both.

### Carousel

An eight-slide conceptual workflow showing where an immediate response, human review, selected stage, and pause checks belong.

**Reader job:** Map one inquiry sequence before copying it into a CRM.

**Slide 1 · Hook — Let the conversation set the follow-up**

Introduce responsiveness with control over the next step.

**Visual direction:** A simple inquiry path with a clearly marked review point.

**Slide 2 · Problem — The timer does not know the whole conversation**

A reply, unavailable date, or booked appointment can change what is relevant. Use a hypothetical scenario.

**Visual direction:** Message timer beside a new reply; label it a workflow example.

**Slide 3 · Main point — Acknowledge the inquiry**

Describe the role of a first response: confirm receipt and set an accurate next-step expectation.

**Visual direction:** Illustrative acknowledgment card; no availability or response-time promise.

**Slide 4 · Main point — Choose the human review point**

Decide who checks availability, fit, and existing conversation before selecting the next stage.

**Visual direction:** Owner-review checkpoint with a named responsibility placeholder.

**Slide 5 · Main point — Give the next stage a purpose**

A chosen sales stage can trigger the relevant message. Confirm actual configuration before teaching click-by-click steps.

**Visual direction:** Concept diagram labelled “configuration to confirm,” not a fake product screenshot.

**Slide 6 · Main point — Define the pauses and exceptions**

Specify what should happen after a reply, booking, or unsuitable inquiry; these are implementation questions.

**Visual direction:** Exception checklist with questions, not feature-complete checkmarks.

**Slide 7 · Transformation — Move the process, not just the old emails**

Review each email’s job and trigger before migrating a sequence.

**Visual direction:** Old email → buyer need → chosen trigger → new workflow.

**Slide 8 · CTA — Which follow-up needs your judgment?**

Invite a comment or an approved LeadLock inquiry, selecting the ending that fits the intended funnel stage.

**Visual direction:** One invitation only; commercial URL added after confirmation.

**Caption direction:** State that this is a workflow-design example based on a customer request. Clarify that detailed behaviour depends on configuration; avoid a rollout-success claim.
