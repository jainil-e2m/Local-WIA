## Carousel
- Length. 7 to 12 slides, usually 9 or 10 for a story (plan section 4.3, reconciled 22 October 2026). She held one to 10 (B-strategy-3, 2026-06-10); approved carousels ran 9, 11 and 12 slides (A-social-2, A-social-3). Slide 1 carries the title's promise; the story takes two to four slides; then the reader's situation and the reframe, a belief slide, and a CTA slide for both DIY and done-for-you readers.
- Substance. Real text on every slide: she follows marketers who post "7-8 slides with 5-9 sentences per slide" (A-social-2, 2025-12-29). "I really don't like carousels that barely have any text on each slide." (B-strategy-2). No "fluffy transition slide" (A-social-3).
- Build slide by slide and lock each slide (A-social-3, 2026-08-04). No repeated phrase across slides (A-social-1, 27 Dec 2025).
- Patterns: a slide-1 hook that differs from the reel and the email; a case-study carousel retelling one client story slide by slide (nine slides, Aug 2026); a perspective-shift carousel ("You don't need another reminder to 'show up consistently.'", 12 slides, Aug 2026); an evergreen thought piece that "does not need to mention an offer at all" (A-social-2, 2025-12-31); a launch carousel that names the offer on slide 1 and describes what is included on the following slides (A-social-1, 2025-10-29_690289f8).
- Evergreen and pinnable carousels can double as ads, so dates go on the second-to-last slide or in the caption (A-social-2, 2026-02-05_6983f0ce).

## Email (list broadcast)
- Send metadata she writes on every draft: DATE, SEND TIME, RECIPIENTS (a named segment), SUBJECT, PREVIEW TEXT (C-email-1).
- Cadence and timing. At least one email a week; "Tuesday also happens to be when our emails usually perform best" (A-social-2, 2026-03-26_69c5403c). Broadcasts go out a week apart; evergreen emails also join the Investment Guide nurture (A-social-2).
- Stands alone. No Instagram references and no assumed context, because the list includes leads from Google and ChatGPT who never see Instagram (A-social-2, Email A, Jan 2026; rule F07).
- Subject lines are specific, observational and ones she would open. She writes most final subject lines herself: "The question we ask every new client"; "The most common follow-up problem I see"; "More leads won't fix this..."; "Being good isn't enough if couples can't find you"; "I screwed up our IG account".
- Body. "Hi {{contact.first_name}}," then: an opening scene or observation; the argument, in short paragraphs; proof or a client story; the principle; the offer, if any, as the solution; a reply-based CTA. Flowing paragraphs rather than bullets ("Keep it flowy and natural", C-email-2). Graphics are rare ("we actually rarely use graphics in our WIA emails"). Use contractions.
- CTA. Reply with a keyword or an open question. Never "DM us" in an email (rule F05). "I read every response myself" appears in a sent email (A-social-3, 31 Jul 2026).
- Sign-off. "Talk soon, Whitney" for most first-person emails. "xo, Whitney" for personal stories. "Daniela & Whitney" or "XO, Daniela & Whitney" for launches and giveaways. "Sincerely," and "Excited to hear from you" also appear (attachments B5).

## First-touch text
- What it is. First-touch text is what a cold reader sees before deciding to keep going: the email subject and preview, every Reel cover and on-screen hook, the first b-roll screen and carousel slide 1. The five rules below apply to every one of them (R59).
- Specific. It names the concrete subject (ads, Google, Meta, the budget, inquiries, bookings, the landing page), so a cold reader knows what it is about without the rest of the piece. A line lifted from a client or a call keeps its subject: a question about "this" or "it" says what "this" is.
- Slide 1 carries the promise. The carousel's title, or its promise as an insight about the reader, is on slide 1. A bare quote, a teaser or a slide that only makes sense after slide 2 is not a slide 1.
- Subject lines state WIA's counter-expected point or decision, or the reader's own question or problem, in plain first-person or observational words. Never a narration of what a third party did (a client, a platform, an AI tool). Two to four options, best first; the preview text finishes the thought, it does not repeat it.
- No insider terms. WIA's program internals (the insider terms in document 05: office hours, the VAULT, VIP Day, the kickoff call and the rest) never appear in first-touch text; in a script, an email or a caption each appears at most once, explained in plain words ("we met with one of our Accelerator members recently"). Offer names are not insider terms: they carry their approved descriptors (document 05).
- Who did what. A story beat keeps the actor the record names (the client, her own tool or assistant, WIA's strategist), or the fact Whitney attested at a gate. Specificity outranks sibling distinctness: when two openings in a pack or a week would collide, the later one changes its angle and keeps its subject.
- Her own subject lines, sent 2026 (patterns, not formulas; never reuse one): "More leads won't fix this..."; "The goal isn't going viral"; "Visibility isn't something you earn." (preview: "You engineer it."); "The question we ask every new client" (preview: "I'm always surprised by the answer"); "It feels like our sales are on autopilot."; "She didn't post on Instagram for 10 weeks"; "What I taught my clients this week"; "If your ads aren't performing like they should, read this". A quote, an observation or a plain first-person statement, mostly without emoji, never a "What happens next?" tease.
- Covers and on-screen hooks she wrote or chose (patterns, not formulas): covers "For a wedding business, every touchpoint matters more." (her line, July 2026), "The best ad strategies start BEFORE the ads." (October 2026), "Google Reps Aren't Your Marketing Strategist" and "Are Your Google Ads Actually Working?" (October 2026); b-roll text hooks "You can't book clients who never find you." (May 2026) and "If you're seeing this, this is your sign to create a more predictable inquiry pipeline for your wedding business." (May 2026); carousel slide 1 "You don't need another reminder to 'show up consistently.'" (August 2026). Each names its subject (ads, Google, couples finding you, the inquiry pipeline) in the first words.

## Instagram caption
- Length. Long captions are welcome. A July 2026 brief asked for about 200 to 300 words; most approved captions run 150 to 300 words. Depth is valued: "I do not just want one sentence per slide" applies to captions as well (A-social-2).
- Structure seen in posted captions, for example "You can't optimize what you can't measure." (Aug 2026), "There's a difference between wanting to do less marketing and wanting your marketing to depend less on you." (Aug 2026), and "Generating more leads is great." (Jun 2026): A one-line hook: an observation, a client quote, or a belief. The problem or desire, in the reader's world. What WIA sees, believes or did. The client story or proof, anonymised. The broader principle. CTA.
- CTA. Instagram captions carry the CTAs, usually a DM keyword ("DM me 'ADS.'", "DM us ELEVATE"). Some captions in a week have none. On 27 Jul 2026 a three-post week ran "2 clips without CTA and the third case study clip with CTA" (A-social-3).
- Sign-off. "-Whitney" or "XO, Whitney" on first-person founder captions (A-social-2, 2026-01-14_6967eeb2; A-social-3, 18 Aug 2026).
- Podcast clips end on the podcast link (A-social-3, 2026-07-28).

## One-to-one email and DM (sales, clients, partners)
- Length. Routine replies run 25 to 120 words. She writes long only to explain strategy or share experience.
- Openers: "It was so nice meeting with you today." / "It was great connecting with you!" / "No worries at all…" / "Great question!"
- Closings: "Talk soon," "Best," "Sincerely," "Take care,". Never "xx".
- Shape. One sentence of why when explaining strategy. End qualifying emails on one open question: "it should end on a question, nothing more" (E-sales-1, 2026-09-02_6a9899db). Ask a curious diagnostic question rather than pitch ("Just out of curiosity, what is your plan in the meantime?"). No hype and no retention push. Never summarise the client's message back to them.
- Application replies follow her nine-step framework: gratitude; a specific acknowledgement; reflect and normalise; show that ads solve the stated problem; one layer of depth; a soft sell tied to their concern; honest readiness; one question; stop (C-email-2, 2026-09-08_6aa050ab; E-sales-1).
- Welcome DM (from 3 Aug 2026): "Hi! Thanks so much for the follow. 😊 This is Whitney. (Real human, not a bot 😂) Really glad you're here. What brought you to our page?" (E-sales-1, 2026-07-14_6a563e5f).

## Rules that apply to every format
- One idea, many formats, one hook per format. Sibling pieces repeat the idea, never the language (rejection rules F01, F02).
- Caption framework (from 25 Jun 2026): Savannah's Hook / Problems-Desires-Symptoms / Main Point / Transformation / CTA (B-strategy-3, 2026-06-25_6a3d3a29). Whitney's own recollection is "hook, problem, transformation, solution (our accelerators)" (A-social-3, 2026-09-15_6aa98079), so the offer often appears as the solution before the CTA.
- Who speaks: "I" and a personal sign-off for Whitney's own experience; "we" when both co-founders or the brand speak; the sign-off follows whoever the CTA names (rule V11).
- No em dashes in new copy; minimal emojis; no hashtags in finals (rules V01, V13).
- Time-bound details (spots, deadlines, prices) never go on a designed asset that should stay evergreen; they go in the caption or the email (A-social-1, 2025-11-18_691cc2e6).
