# Wedding Industry Ads — Sept 10 MVP Build Pack

Generated 2026-09-03. Five MVPs, ~4.75 build days on a shared spine. Every client-facing output terminates in a Slack review gate.

## The one-sentence story

ONE STORY, in one sentence she can repeat to her team: something happens, it gets read once into one plain-text file per client that a human can open and fix, and everything anybody drafts afterwards is rendered from that file and stops at a Slack card with her name on it.

That is the literal answer to the question Whitney has now asked across three calls and never had answered — "the biggest thing that I'm looking for at this point is like, how is it all connected? How does it all kind of work together... is there one place that we're going, and then there are just like different tabs?" The answer is not tabs and it is not a diagram, which would fail on a founder who had never used Claude and dislikes her own custom GPTs. The answer is: one brain, one review gate, five triggers into the same engine.

Concretely, the five cards are four triggers and one renderer pack hanging off a single spine. A call ends or a client signs → the handoff engine drafts the recap, the action items with owners, the tasks, the folder, the announcement (MVP1). A page goes live → the fulfilment check crawls it at mobile and reports blockers and go-live readiness (MVP2). A brief exists → the team gets first drafts instead of a blank page (MVP3). A month closes → an executive summary with a named trend and a recommendation, not a screenshot deck (MVP4). A voice note arrives → a reel, a carousel, an email, every quote traceable to a timestamp (MVP5). Each one reads the same client_brief.md and each one appends what it learned back into it, so the file gets truer the more the system runs. Nothing client-bound sends: every one of the five terminates in the same Slack approve/edit card, which is the product and not the scaffolding.

Three deliberate breaks with the received wisdom, all forced by the judges. First, the running order is itself the argument — the fulfilment check opens because it needs no credential and finds a real bug on her own property, buying the credibility that every mock-dependent card afterwards spends. Second, the agency brain is built FIRST and shown LAST, never as a sixth tile: the close is scrolling one Markdown file and its updates log and showing what wrote itself during the preceding twenty-five minutes, which answers connectedness with an artifact and lands on the actual reason she bought in August — what stays when the next person resigns. Third, this portfolio deliberately moves the story off Gisselle's seat and onto the four single points of failure Whitney named herself (Ivy, Carmen/Jacqueline, Tina, Danielle) while REFRAMING rather than dropping Gisselle's rows: she is an outside-agency contractor at ~25 hrs/week whose process is documented nowhere but the sheet she wrote, which makes her the most acute turnover risk in the company. Her cards are extraction, not audit, and she is scripted as the approver in the demo — because if she leaves that review feeling audited and discarded, nothing gets adopted no matter which two Whitney picks.

Two things this portfolio does that no submitted portfolio did, both because the judges said all four missed them. It answers the Aug 11 question that has now gone four weeks unanswered ("how much time back in three months, what percentage can actually be automated") not with another re-frame but with a measurement: the spine keeps a run ledger, the cards run for six days before the review, and the demo shows a 30-day backtest — "this pipeline ran N times this week and here is what it would have produced against your last month of calls" — plus a one-page ranked sheet giving a defensible RANGE with its assumptions shown and naming the one missing input (onboarding volume). And it shows a failure on purpose: an ambiguous action item refusing to guess an owner, and the review gate blocking a send, because a founder who abandoned her own custom GPTs when they drifted needs to see the guardrail fire once rather than watch twenty-five minutes of clean runs.

## Shared infrastructure (built once, reused by all five)

- THE EXTRACTOR + TRANSCRIPT NORMALIZER (M0) — ~0.4 day, used by four of the five cards. A strict JSON tool-schema extractor over the Anthropic Messages API with call_type routing (ads_kickoff, messaging_positioning, ad_strategy_presentation, monthly_1:1, leadlock_kickoff, internal_monday, sales_call), plus a normalizer for three input shapes (Fathom export, Zoom, and the two E2M .md files already on disk). Two properties that are load-bearing rather than decorative: every extracted fact carries an evidence timestamp so Whitney can verify rather than trust, and speaker labels are never trusted — owners are inferred from content, which is why it survives the known-bad attribution in their own transcripts and is itself a demonstrable robustness proof. This is the single reason a five-card demo is arithmetically possible: standalone the cards cost ~8 engineering days, on the spine they cost 3.5.
- THE CONFIDENCE / ABSTAIN LANE — the smallest and most important piece. Any extraction below threshold, or any ambiguous owner, routes to a 'needs a human' lane with the candidates and the quote rather than guessing. It costs almost nothing because it is the same code path, and it is what lets us show the guardrail firing on purpose — the thing a founder who abandoned her own drifting custom GPTs needs to see once, and which no submitted portfolio scripted.
- THE SLACK REVIEW GATE, BUILT POLL-BASED — ~0.25 day, and the one piece of infrastructure every portfolio showed and none costed. Slack is the designated review surface (100% team adoption; ClickUp was never adopted; the word 'dashboard' appears zero times in either transcript). Deliberate engineering choice: approval is read back by a polling worker via reactions.get / conversations.replies rather than an interactive Request URL, so there is NO public endpoint, NO 3-second ACK requirement, NO signature verification and NO tunnel that can die mid-demo. Every client-bound output from all five cards terminates in this one gate, which is how the Aug 11 standing constraint ('we'll review any drafts before they go out') is enforced in code rather than in process.
- THE CONNECTOR LAYER — ~0.35 day. One authenticated, retrying client each for ClickUp v2, Drive v3 (supportsAllDrives), Gmail (drafts.create on ai@ with RFC822 MIME), Slack, and GHL v2 (Private Integration Tokens, location-scoped), each with a sandbox fallback stood up Sept 3 before anyone's permission matters. Written once, reused by every card and by every future one — this is the concrete form of the compounding-capability argument, and it is why the second sprint's cards cost half of what the first sprint's did.
- client_brief.md PLUS THE updates/ APPEND LOG — ~0.2 day. One human-readable, diffable, Gisselle-editable Markdown file per client, deliberately NOT a vector store (twelve clients at a few KB each makes RAG engineering theatre). Critically it is read AND WRITTEN: every handoff run appends decisions and action items, every report run appends the month's numbers and narrative, every page check appends its result. Three of the four submitted portfolios built this read-only, which makes it a template that rots; the write path is what turns it into the asset that survives a resignation — the literal thing bought on Aug 11 ('we have the agency brain').
- THE STALENESS / CONFIRM-OR-CORRECT JOB — ~0.15 day, and the only honest durability demo available in five days. It reads client_brief.md, diffs it against the last 30 days of appended updates, calls, and metrics, and posts to Slack: 'these four facts are older than sixty days — confirm or correct.' Minu warned that knowledge bases rot after a couple of months and Whitney answered 'that's what I see coming', so answering that objection with a file format alone is not enough — this answers it with a mechanism.
- agency_playbook.md — ~0.15 day, zero credentials, composed entirely from the two transcripts already on disk. The Aug 11 quote is about a person who can run the AGENCY, not one who knows one client, and all four portfolios answered 'is there one place that we're going?' with a client folder. This is the agency-level layer: the eight sequential onboarding gates with owners, the offer→ClickUp-template and offer→public-proposal-URL maps, the 2-week-DM / 4-week-email escalation ladder, the seven star meanings, and the one-person-per-function map with no redundancy. It is the artifact that makes the next account-manager hire 'just a person who is kind of like running it.'
- THE RUN LEDGER AND 30-DAY BACKTEST — ~0.15 day, and it is what finally answers the question asked on Aug 11 and unanswered for four weeks. Every pipeline logs its runs from Sept 4 onward, and a backtest harness replays the engine over the last 30 days of available calls and sweeps. On Sept 10 that produces counts Whitney watched accumulate rather than a re-frame of her question, plus a defensible RANGE with its assumptions shown and one honest statement of the single missing input (onboarding volume per month). All four submitted portfolios correctly declined to fight on hours and then substituted nothing, which reads as evasion to a founder who asked a quantitative question.
- THE DID-THE-HUMAN-DO-IT PRIMITIVE — near-free inside the handoff engine. TASK-13 already contains TASK-10 (Gisselle creates ClickUp tasks to verify Carmen and Jacqueline actually sent the 2-week DMs, Aug 26 @14:47), so a flag-then-verify-the-human loop is built once here and reused by roll call and any accountability digest later. It is also the only mechanism in the portfolio that addresses Whitney's most-repeated operational complaint about chasing the team.

**One engine, two triggers: a client call ends or a client signs, and the recap email, the action items with owners, the ClickUp tasks, the Drive filing and the team announcement are all drafted and sitting behind one Slack approval.**

**Why selected:** Whitney described this exact chain in a single breath on Sept 2 @6:54 ("Gisselle is then uploading the recordings to the client folder, checking the meetings for action items, then assigning them to team members in ClickUp, letting them know in Slack, drafting a recap email for the client... and attaching the recording"). # SPEC — The Handoff — after every call, and after every signature (C1+C5 slice: one engine, two triggers)

**User story:** Gisselle is the user and the demo's approver. "Whitney ran a monthly 1:1 yesterday and I wasn't on it. Instead of opening the recording, scrubbing forty minutes for action items, hand-typing five ClickUp tasks with due dates, posting to Slack so the team notices, drafting a recap in Whitney's voice, and dragging the recording into the client folder — one Slack card is already waiting. Six action items, each with an owner and a clickable timestamp, so I check the one I doubt in four seconds instead of re-listening. One is deliberately unassigned because the call genuinely didn't settle whether it was Carmen's or Jacqueline's; it sits in a needs-a-human lane with both candidates and the quote — exactly what I'd have done. I fix that owner in the thread, react with a check mark, and the ClickUp tasks, Drive filing and Gmail draft happen. Nothing was sent. Then a client signs and pays and the same card appears for a different reason: onboarding list cloned from the right offer template, signature-relative due dates real, kickoff-relative ones tagged and waiting on the booking, Drive folder created, client channel created, welcome email drafted."

Whitney's version, the one that buys: "The chain I described in one breath on Sept 2 now runs whether or not the one contractor who knows it is at her desk — and every call from today writes what it learned into a file the company owns instead of into somebody's head."

## Architecture

STACK: Python 3.11; `anthropic` 1.x; `pydantic` v2; `slack_sdk`; `google-api-python-client`+`google-auth-oauthlib`; `httpx2` (SDK-native, NOT `httpx`) for ClickUp/GHL; `watchdog`; `webvtt-py`+`python-docx`; SQLite. Two processes: `python -m handoff.watch` (triggers) and `python -m handoff.gate_worker` (approval poller). No public HTTP endpoint anywhere.

TRIGGER A — call ended. `watchdog` on `./inbox/`. Accepts .md (the two E2M files on disk), .vtt/.srt (Zoom), .txt/.docx (Fathom), .json. Production trigger named but not built: Zoom `recording.completed` webhook -> `payload.object.recording_files[]` where `file_type=="TRANSCRIPT"` -> `download_url` + S2S OAuth token (`POST https://zoom.us/oauth/token?grant_type=account_credentials&account_id=..`, scope `recording:read:admin`).

TRIGGER B — signature. Client-side Gmail filter on hello@ forwards Dubsado alerts to ai@ (ask Whitney; do not touch their settings). Poll `GET https://gmail.googleapis.com/gmail/v1/users/me/messages?q=from:dubsado newer_than:7d -label:handoff-done`, then `messages/{id}?format=full`, walk `payload.parts` for text/plain else text/html->bs4. LLM-parse to `DubsadoAlert{event: contract_signed|invoice_paid, client_name, client_email, offer_names[], amount, occurred_at, confidence}`. Correlate on client_email into SQLite `pending_signups`; fan out ONLY when both events seen (Whitney: signed AND paid). One event alone for >72h posts a "half-signal" note instead. Manual fallback: `python -m handoff.onboard --client X --offers foolproof,ads --kickoff 2026-09-15`, or a Slack message matching `onboard: X | offers | date` read by the same poller.

STAGE 1 — normalizer (`normalize.py`). Emits `Utterance{idx, t_seconds, t_label, speaker_label_raw, text}`. `parse_e2m_md` on `^@(\d+):(\d{2}) - (.+)$`; `parse_vtt` via webvtt-py (`<v Name>` if present); `parse_generic_timestamped` tries `[HH:MM:SS]`, `HH:MM:SS`, `MM:SS`, optional `Name:` prefix, logs which regex matched (Fathom shape is unknown until the sample lands). Captures `declared_participants` if any format exposes a participant block — that is also the free falsification test for the roll-call card. CRITICAL: the model rendition replaces every name with `SPEAKER_n` and ships the raw labels in a separate roster block marked unreliable. The model cannot see a name attached to a line. That is what makes the "labels are wrong and it still worked" claim mechanical rather than aspirational.

STAGE 2 — extractor (`extract.py`). `client.messages.parse(model="claude-opus-5", max_tokens=16000, thinking={"type":"adaptive"}, output_format=CallExtraction, system=[...], messages=[...])` -> `.parsed_output`. Guard `stop_reason=="refusal"` before reading content; on refusal re-issue via `client.beta.messages.create(betas=["server-side-fallback-2026-07-01"], fallbacks="default", output_config={"format":{"type":"json_schema","schema":CallExtraction.model_json_schema()}})`. Put roster+rules+templates+client_brief.md in `system` with `cache_control:{"type":"ephemeral"}`; transcript after the breakpoint; assert `usage.cache_read_input_tokens>0` on run 2.

Schema: `CallExtraction{call_type: Literal[ads_kickoff|messaging_positioning|ad_strategy_presentation|monthly_1on1|leadlock_kickoff|sales_call|internal_monday|unknown], call_type_confidence, client_name, client_slug, participants_inferred[], call_date_iso, one_paragraph_summary, decisions[], action_items[], open_questions[], recap_email, filing, brief_updates[], needs_human[]}`. `ActionItem{task<=120ch, detail, owner: Literal[Whitney|Gisselle|Carmen|Jacqueline|Ivy|Tina|Danielle|client|UNRESOLVED], owner_candidates[], owner_rationale, confidence, due_hint (verbatim), due_date_iso|null, client_visible: bool, evidence: List[Evidence] minItems 1}`. `Evidence{t_label, quote<=240ch verbatim, utterance_idx}`. `FilingPlan{drive_folder_path, files[], taxonomy_source: observed_sample|inferred}`.

SYSTEM PROMPT — five load-bearing blocks: (1) the function roster as ownership law: Carmen=Google Ads/keywords/campaign builds/Loom, Jacqueline=Meta Ads, Ivy=ad creative, Tina=LeadLock landing pages + email/SMS nurture copy + monthly reports, Danielle=GHL implementation/workflows/DNS/A2P, Whitney=all client and sales calls and all review, Gisselle=ops/filing/ClickUp/Slack/recaps; assign by which function the task belongs to, never by who spoke. (2) THE LABEL RULE: "the SPEAKER_n roster is known to be wrong and frequently inverted in this client's transcripts; never use it to decide an owner." (3) ABSTAIN RULE: "if confidence<0.75 or two owners are equally plausible, set owner=UNRESOLVED and list candidates; a confidently wrong owner costs this team more than an abstention; do not guess to look complete." (4) EVIDENCE RULE: "every item needs >=1 evidence entry with the exact t_label and a verbatim quote; if you cannot cite it, do not emit it." (5) "Nothing you write is sent. Write a draft for Whitney or Gisselle to approve."

Second call, voice pass (only if the template pack arrives): rewrite `recap_email` against 3-5 templates retrieved by `call_type`. Separate call so the extractor's cache prefix stays stable and this leg drops cleanly if the templates never come.

Latency: post the Slack card immediately as `:hourglass: reading a 48-minute call…` then `chat.update` per stage. If p50 > 45s, split the transcript into 3 overlapping windows, `AsyncAnthropic` + `asyncio.gather`, merge and dedupe on (owner, task) fuzzy match — also the right design for a 2-hour call.

STAGE 3 — review gate (`gate.py`, `gate_worker.py`). `chat.postMessage` Block Kit to `#e2m-handoff`: header; summary section; fields (call type + confidence, client, duration); divider; one section per item (`*Carmen* — Rebuild the branded search campaign` + context `_"…" · 21:34 · conf 0.91_`); a visually separate `:warning: 1 item needs a human` section carrying both candidates and the quote; then a URL-button row and a context footer: "Nothing has been sent. Gmail draft, ClickUp tasks and Drive filing are created only after Approve." APPROVAL IS POLL-BASED because interactive buttons require a public Request URL: `reactions.get(channel, timestamp, full=True)` every 5s for `white_check_mark` from a user in APPROVERS (`users.list` once to resolve Gisselle's and Whitney's IDs), plus `conversations.replies` parsed by deterministic regex grammar: `approve` / `approve -email` / `reject` / `assign 4 = Jacqueline` / `owner 3 = Tina` / `due 2 = 2026-09-12` / `drop 5`. Edits mutate the stored CallExtraction, `chat.update` re-renders, poller keeps waiting. Scopes: `chat:write`, `chat:write.public`, `files:write`, `reactions:read`, `channels:history`, `groups:history`, `channels:read`, plus `channels:manage` for trigger B. SQLite `gate(run_id UNIQUE, channel, ts, state, approved_by, approved_at, payload_json)`; states pending->edited->approved->executing->executed|rejected|expired(24h).

STAGE 4 — destinations, all post-approval only.
ClickUp: `Authorization: pk_…`, base `https://api.clickup.com/api/v2`. Discovery once to `config/clickup_map.json`: `GET /team`, `/team/{id}/space?archived=false`, `/space/{id}/folder`, `/folder/{id}/list`, `/space/{id}/list`, `/team/{id}/member` (name->user_id), `/list/{id}/field`. Create: `POST /list/{list_id}/task {"name","description","assignees":[id],"due_date":<EPOCH MS>,"due_date_time":false,"tags":["ai-drafted","handoff"]}`. Description always carries provenance: detail, `Source: <client> <call_type>, Sept 2 2026 @21:34`, the verbatim quote, the Drive link, `Approved by @Gisselle in Slack`. UNRESOLVED items are never created as tasks.
Onboarding clone (no List-template endpoint exists): `GET /list/{model}/task?include_closed=true&subtasks=true&page=N` -> `POST /folder/{fid}/list {"name":"Onboarding — X"}` -> re-POST each task with assignees remapped BY FUNCTION (not by the source task's assignee, which may be the departed account manager); parents first, keep `{old_id:new_id}`, children with `"parent"`. Also probe `GET /team/{id}/taskTemplate?page=0`. Two-phase dates: signature-relative tasks get real epochs at t0 (countersigned contract +0d, questionnaire chase +3d, LeadLock sub-account +1d); kickoff-relative tasks get `due_date:null` + tag `pending-kickoff` with the offset in SQLite, back-filled by `PUT /task/{id}` when GHL `GET /calendars/events?locationId=&calendarId=&startTime=&endTime=` (Bearer PIT, header `Version: 2021-07-28`, base `https://services.leadconnectorhq.com`) shows the booking.
Drive v3: one-time `InstalledAppFlow.run_local_server(port=0)` as ai@, scopes drive + spreadsheets + gmail.compose + gmail.modify, refresh token to `secrets/ai_token.json`. `drives.list` to detect a Shared Drive; if `driveId` present, every call carries `supportsAllDrives=true&includeItemsFromAllDrives=true`. If it is in a personal My Drive, ai@ can still `files.create` but owns the result — offer `--transfer-owner` (`PATCH /files/{id}/permissions/{pid}?transferOwnership=true`, role=owner, whitney@) and say so out loud rather than leaving orphans. Path from `config/drive_taxonomy.json` built by recursing the sample folder; else `taxonomy_source="inferred"` and Slack renders "Proposed path (inferred, not yet matched to your Drive)". Files written: `transcript.md` (the real input — this is the institutional-memory leg), `summary.md`, `action_items.md`.
Gmail draft: `POST /gmail/v1/users/me/drafts {"message":{"raw": base64url(EmailMessage with set_content + add_alternative(html))}}`. Draft lives in ai@, not hello@ — state that plainly. Demo recipient is whitney@ or ai@, never a real client address.

STAGE 5 — brief write-back + ledger. `brief.py` appends `/clients/<slug>/updates/<ts>_<run_id>.md` and patches managed sections of `/clients/<slug>/client_brief.md` between `<!-- handoff:decisions:start -->` fences. Markdown, diffable, Gisselle-editable. `ledger.py`: SQLite `runs(run_id, trigger, source_file, client_slug, call_type, n_items, n_unresolved, input_tokens, output_tokens, cache_read_tokens, cost_usd, latency_s, gate_state, approved_by)` and `outputs(run_id, destination, external_id, url)`. `python -m handoff.backtest --since 2026-08-11` replays every transcript on disk and prints counts, abstention rate, and the sensitivity range with the one missing input (calls/month, onboardings/month) named. Measured cost per 48-minute call at claude-opus-5 ($5/$25 per MTok): ~12.5K input + ~7K output incl. thinking = $0.20-0.35 per call, under $10/month at their volume. Have that number ready.

## Acceptance criteria

## Architecture

STACK: Python 3.11 · Playwright (Chromium) · httpx · dnspython · Jinja2 · Pillow · SQLite · slack_sdk · anthropic SDK. One package, `pagecheck/`. No public endpoint, no tunnel, no dashboard (the word "dashboard" appears zero times in either transcript; Slack is the designated review surface).

=== TRIGGER (three, same code path) ===
1. `python -m pagecheck run --target wia_marketing --profile full` (CLI, the dev loop).
2. Scheduled: 08:00 CT daily via Windows Task Scheduler for the demo week, `cron`/GitHub Actions in production. This is the one that matters — it produces the six real historical runs the Sept 10 diff view is built on.
3. DEMO SURFACE: a 60-line local FastAPI page at http://127.0.0.1:8088 — one URL input, a viewport toggle (1440 / 375), a Run button, progress streamed over SSE. Deliberately local: nothing to deploy, nothing to expire, no wifi dependency beyond the crawl itself.

=== TARGETS (`targets.yaml`, the whole config surface) ===
```yaml
properties:
  - id: wia_marketing
    label: "Wedding Industry Ads — main funnel"
    root: https://weddingindustryads.com
    mode: crawl                # sitemap.xml -> robots.txt -> BFS depth 3
    max_pages: 60
    viewports: [mobile, desktop]
    checks: [links, gated_assets, tracking, forms, seo, cta, mixed_content, contact_links, policy, render]
  - id: leadlock
    label: "LeadLock Pro"
    root: https://<CONFIRM WITH WHITNEY — do not infer the domain from ad copy>
    mode: crawl
  - id: client_<slug>
    label: "<Client> — LeadLock tour booking"
    mode: list                 # NEVER crawl a third-party production property
    urls: [ ... ]              # explicit, client-confirmed
    checks: [ ..., a2p_readiness ]
    expected_ad_landing_url: null   # supplied by Carmen/Jacqueline -> unlocks the URL-swap row

```
Crawl discipline, non-negotiable: robots.txt honoured, 1 req/sec per host, `User-Agent: E2M-PageCheck/0.1 (+ai@weddingindustryads.com)`, same-registrable-domain only, and **no form is ever submitted** — the tool reads forms, it does not inject leads into a client's CRM. (`--live-form-probe` exists, defaults OFF, own-property only, and tags its submission `pagecheck+<rundate>@e2msolutions.com` so Gisselle can delete it.)

=== STAGE 1 — DISCOVER ===
`/sitemap.xml` (recursing sitemap indexes) + `/robots.txt` for extra sitemap hints and disallow rules, then BFS from root to depth 3, capped at `max_pages`. URL normalization: strip fragment, drop `utm_*`/`gclid`/`fbclid`, sort remaining query params, collapse trailing slash. Dedupe.

=== STAGE 2 — RENDER (dual viewport, mobile first-class) ===
Two Playwright browser contexts per run:
- mobile: `viewport={"width":375,"height":812}, device_scale_factor=2, is_mobile=True, has_touch=True`, iOS Safari UA + the PageCheck token.
- desktop: `viewport={"width":1440,"height":900}`.
GHL funnel pages are JS-rendered, so a static crawler literally sees nothing — every page goes through the browser. **Do not wait on `networkidle`**: GHL pages carry long-poll connections and frequently never reach it. Use `goto(url, wait_until="domcontentloaded", timeout=12000)`, then `wait_for_selector("a,button,form", timeout=4000)`, then a 1.2s settle. A timeout becomes a `render.timeout` finding, never a crash.
Per page captured: final URL + full redirect chain, HTTP status, full-page PNG (mobile shot is the evidence artifact), `page.content()`, bounding boxes for every actionable element, network log via `page.on("response")`/`page.on("requestfailed")`, console errors via `page.on("console")`. Known cookie/consent overlay selectors (GHL, Termly, Osano, CookieYes) are dismissed and the dismissal is recorded; an undismissable overlay forces ABSTAIN on the above-fold checks rather than a false "no CTA".

=== STAGE 3 — DETERMINISTIC CHECK BATTERY (12 checks; the LLM authors none of these) ===
```python
@dataclass(frozen=True)
class Finding:
    id: str        # sha1(check_id|norm_page_url|viewport|norm_target)[:12]  -- STABLE ACROSS RUNS
    check_id: str; severity: str      # BLOCKER | WARNING | INFO | ABSTAIN
    viewport: str; page_url: str; target: str | None; http_status: int | None
    title: str; detail: str
    evidence: dict # {"screenshot": "...png", "clip":[x,y,w,h], "selector":"...", "html":"..."}
    owner_hint: str; confidence: float; machine_verified: bool
```
1. `link.broken` — every `a[href]`, plus GHL's `<a>`-wrapped button divs, `[onclick*="location"]`, `data-href`. Each unique URL checked once: `HEAD` -> on 405/501 retry `GET` with `Range: bytes=0-2048`, ≤5 redirects, chain recorded. Internal 4xx/5xx = BLOCKER. **External failures are re-verified through a real Playwright context before being reported** — bare-httpx 403s from Cloudflare/Meta/Instagram are the single most likely way this tool embarrasses Jainil on stage; bot-hostile domains report INFO "unverifiable", never BLOCKER.
2. `link.mobile_only` — set-difference of the href inventory between the mobile and desktop DOMs. GHL builds separate mobile sections, which is exactly why their known defect is mobile-only. Anything present only at 375px is tagged `viewport: mobile`, and if broken carries the line *"the desktop pass reported this page clean."*
3. `link.gated_asset` — any link to drive.google.com / docs.google.com / dropbox.com / notion.so fetched in a **fresh cookie-less context**; a login or permission wall = BLOCKER titled "this link only works if you are already signed in as Whitney." This is the second Aug 26 defect, correctly diagnosed — Whitney's own hypothesis was "I think it's a shared drive access," and no other check in the battery would catch it.
4. `tracking.presence` / `tracking.duplicate_fire` — from the network log plus `page.evaluate` on `window.dataLayer`/`gtag`/`fbq`: GA4 (`googletagmanager.com/gtag/js?id=G-`, `google-analytics.com/g/collect`), GTM (`gtm.js?id=GTM-`), Meta Pixel (`connect.facebook.net/.../fbevents.js`, `facebook.com/tr?id=<id>&ev=PageView`). Missing on a paid-traffic destination = BLOCKER. Two distinct GA4 ids, two distinct pixel ids, or >1 PageView per load for one id = WARNING "this page double-counts every conversion" — the classic GHL failure where a pixel sits in both funnel settings and a custom code block, invisible to the eye and silently flattering Meta CPL. `expected_pixel_id` mismatch = BLOCKER (page carrying a previous client's or the agency's pixel); absent expected id = INFO, not a pass.
5. `form.*` — detects `<form>`, `iframe[src*="leadconnectorhq.com/widget/form"]`, `iframe[src*="/widget/booking"]`, `.hl-form`. Embedded iframe `src` must itself return 200 and is rendered as a subframe to read its fields. Sub-findings: `form.missing` on a page whose primary role is capture (BLOCKER); `form.utm_capture_absent` (WARNING — "paid traffic arrives in GHL with no source"); `form.thankyou_unreachable` where the configured redirect is discoverable (BLOCKER); where it is not discoverable, ABSTAIN — never PASS.
6. `seo.basics` — title present 10-65 chars, exactly one `h1`, meta description, canonical, `html[lang]` (WARNING). `noindex` on a live paid-traffic page = BLOCKER (strong signal the page was cloned from a staging template).
7. `cta.none_above_fold` / `cta.multiple_competing` — actionable elements with mobile bounding-box y < 812, classified by destination scheme.
8. `mixed_content` — any `http://` subresource on an https page = BLOCKER (browsers block it; forms silently fail).
9. `contact_links` — `tel:` normalized to E.164; `mailto:` syntax plus a live MX lookup on the domain. Same defect class Gisselle hand-fixes every day in TASK-15.
10. `policy.*` — see the A2P block below.
11. `render.errors` — console errors before CTA interactivity (WARNING); a failed request for the form widget (BLOCKER).
12. `perf.mobile_proxy` — mobile `load` time and total transferred bytes; >6s or >4MB = WARNING, labelled a proxy, never called Core Web Vitals.

| Row | Mechanism |
|---|---|

=== STAGE 4 — THE LLM PASS (narrow, and it cannot invent a finding) ===
Anthropic Messages API via the `anthropic` Python SDK, `model="claude-opus-5"`, `thinking={"type":"adaptive"}` (Opus 5 runs adaptive by default; `budget_tokens` is rejected with a 400, and `temperature`/`top_p` are removed on this model — do not pass them). Include `betas=["server-side-fallback-2026-07-01"]` + `fallbacks="default"` so a refusal on any page's content degrades instead of erroring. Three calls only:

(a) PER-PAGE CONVERSION JUDGEMENT — `output_config={"effort":"low"}`, `max_tokens=4096`, one strict tool:
```python
{"name":"emit_page_judgement","strict":True,
 "input_schema":{"type":"object","additionalProperties":False,
  "required":["page_role","primary_cta","offer_clarity","friction","trust_signals_present","trust_signals_missing","abstain","abstain_reason"],
  "properties":{
    "page_role":{"enum":["home","service","landing","booking","policy","thankyou","other"]},
    "primary_cta":{"type":"object","additionalProperties":False,"required":["text","destination","confidence"],
      "properties":{"text":{"type":"string"},"destination":{"type":"string"},"confidence":{"type":"number"}}},
    "offer_clarity":{"type":"object","additionalProperties":False,"required":["score","reason"],
      "properties":{"score":{"type":"integer","minimum":1,"maximum":5},"reason":{"type":"string"}}},
    "friction":{"type":"array","items":{"type":"object","additionalProperties":False,
      "required":["issue","evidence_quote","severity"],
      "properties":{"issue":{"type":"string"},"evidence_quote":{"type":"string"},
                    "severity":{"enum":["BLOCKER","WARNING"]}}}},
    "trust_signals_present":{"type":"array","items":{"type":"string"}},
    "trust_signals_missing":{"type":"array","items":{"type":"string"}},
    "abstain":{"type":"boolean"},"abstain_reason":{"type":["string","null"]}}}}
```
Message content: the mobile viewport screenshot as an `image` block (375x812 clip ≈ 406 image tokens), then the page's full extracted readable text (not truncated — Opus 5's 1M window makes truncation unnecessary; a page over ~40k tokens is chunked, never cut), the ordered heading outline, the deterministic CTA inventory with resolved destinations, and the form field list. System prompt (stable, carries `cache_control: {"type":"ephemeral"}` so 60 pages/day share one cached prefix — verify with `usage.cache_read_input_tokens`): the wedding-vertical context, the ~12-client boutique-agency frame, the instruction that every `evidence_quote` must be copied verbatim from the supplied page text, and the standing instruction to set `abstain: true` rather than judge a page it cannot see (blocked render, overlay, near-empty DOM).
**Content-hash gate:** `content_hash = sha256(normalized_text + cta_inventory)`. Unchanged since the last run -> skip the call and carry the prior judgement forward with its originating run id. On a stable 60-page site day two re-judges two or three pages. This is what makes the demo run land in 40 seconds and what makes the monthly token cost a rounding error.

(b) BLOCKER TRIAGE / OWNER ROUTING — one call over the whole deterministic finding list. Owner is assigned by a deterministic rules table FIRST (privacy policy / DNS / SSL / A2P -> Danielle; landing page, form, nurture copy -> Tina; campaign destination and tracking on ad landing pages -> Carmen for Google, Jacqueline for Meta; site nav, investment guide, gated assets -> Whitney with Gisselle CC'd). The model only fills genuine gaps and drafts the one-line "what to do". No confident guess: an unresolved owner routes to the shared **confidence/abstain lane** as a "needs a human" row with the candidates listed. This is the cheapest thing in the whole portfolio that speaks to Whitney's most-repeated operational complaint — "it always feels like we're chasing the team."

(c) RUN NARRATIVE — ≤120 words, `output_config={"effort":"medium"}`, strict tool with a `cites: [finding_id]` array. Tops the Slack card and the report.

HARD GUARDRAIL, and it is enforced in code, not in the prompt: the renderer displays **only** Findings that exist in the deterministic list with a real id. Any `finding_id` in LLM output that is not in the run's id set is dropped and logged to `llm_rejects`. Any `evidence_quote` that is not a verbatim normalized substring of the supplied page text has that field dropped. Same discipline as the timestamp rule in the content card — the model narrates and routes; it never asserts.

=== STAGE 5 — LEDGER + DIFF (SQLite `pagecheck.db`) ===
```sql
runs(run_id PK, started_at, finished_at, target_id, profile, pages_crawled, config_hash, git_sha);
pages(run_id, url, viewport, status, final_url, load_ms, bytes, content_hash, screenshot_path, PRIMARY KEY(run_id,url,viewport));
findings(run_id, finding_id, check_id, severity, viewport, page_url, target, http_status, title, detail, evidence_json, owner_hint, confidence, machine_verified);
finding_state(finding_id, target_id, first_seen_run, last_seen_run, resolved_run, snoozed_until, status);  -- NEW|OPEN|RESOLVED|REGRESSED|SNOOZED
clickup_links(finding_id, task_id, created_at, completed_at, verified_run, verify_result);
llm_calls(run_id, purpose, model, input_tokens, cache_read_tokens, output_tokens, cost_usd);
llm_rejects(run_id, purpose, reason, payload_json);
```
Diff = set operations on `finding_id` between run N and N-1 -> NEW / STILL_OPEN / RESOLVED / REGRESSED. The id hashes on (check_id, normalized url, viewport, normalized target) and never on title text — if that stability breaks, the diff silently lies, which is why it gets its own acceptance test.

## Failure modes
- FALSE BLOCKERS FROM BOT-HOSTILE EXTERNAL DOMAINS. Cloudflare, Instagram, Facebook and LinkedIn return 403 to a bare httpx request. Unmitigated, the demo opens with three confident findings that are wrong, in front of the person who owns the site. MITIGATION: every external failure is re-verified through a real Playwright context with a browser UA before it is reported; a known bot-hostile allowlist reports INFO/unverifiable, never BLOCKER; internal links only can be BLOCKERs. Build this in the same sitting as the link checker, not as a later fix.
- GHL PAGES NEVER REACH networkidle. They carry long-poll connections, so a networkidle wait times out at 30s on every page and a 60-page run blows far past the demo window — or worse, returns empty DOMs and the tool cheerfully reports a broken page as clean. MITIGATION: domcontentloaded + wait_for_selector('a,button,form') + 1.2s settle, 12s hard cap, timeouts recorded as a render.timeout finding. This is why acceptance criterion 13 requires at least one real GHL page to yield findings.
- CRAWLING A CLIENT'S LIVE PRODUCTION PROPERTY WITHOUT CONFIRMED CONSENT. This is a relationship and arguably a legal problem, and it would be a spectacular own-goal for an engagement whose stated constraint is that the client experience stays untouched. MITIGATION: client pages are mode:list only, never crawled; a missing consent string skips the target entirely with a visible SKIPPED row; robots.txt honoured; 1 req/sec; identifiable UA carrying ai@weddingindustryads.com; and NO FORM IS EVER SUBMITTED, so the tool cannot inject a fake lead into a client's CRM or waste a client's ad-spend conversion.
- THE TWO KNOWN DEFECTS ARE ALREADY FIXED AND THE OPENER HAS NO BUG. MITIGATION: this is why step 0 runs today rather than on day four. If they are fixed, the opener inverts to 'both of these are fixed, here is the row that proves when — and here are the three you don't know about,' which is arguably the stronger frame because it makes the diff the hero. If a 60-page crawl of their own funnel somehow finds nothing at all, open on the confirmed client page; if that is clean too, open on the A2P readiness table, which is a checklist rather than a bug hunt and cannot come up empty.
- THE MODEL INVENTS A FINDING OR A QUOTE. One fabricated blocker in front of a founder who abandoned her own custom GPTs because she could not tell when they were drifting, and the credibility of all five cards is gone. MITIGATION: findings are 100% deterministic; the LLM narrates, triages and routes but never asserts; every finding_id in model output is whitelisted against the run's real id set and every evidence_quote must be a verbatim normalized substring of the supplied page text, with both rejections logged. Fault-injection test is acceptance criterion 8.
- A CONSENT OR COOKIE OVERLAY COVERS THE MOBILE VIEWPORT, producing a false 'no CTA above the fold' and a screenshot of a modal instead of the page. MITIGATION: dismiss known GHL/Termly/Osano/CookieYes selectors and record the dismissal; if an overlay cannot be dismissed, ABSTAIN on the above-fold checks rather than fail them.

TRIGGERS (three, all poll-based so there is no public endpoint and no tunnel that can die mid-demo):

2. STAGE 0 — LOAD. `drive.files.list` with `q="name='client_brief.md' and '<client_folder_id>' in parents"`, `supportsAllDrives=true`, then `files.get?alt=media`. STAGE 1 — GROUND (this is the card's own contribution to shared infra: `wia/core/evidence.py`). Build the QUOTABLE POOL. Every line in the brief carrying a source marker `[src: <file>@<mm:ss> | spk:<name>(inferred)]` — regex `\[src:\s*(?P<file>[^@\]]+)@(?P<ts>[0-9:]+)\s*(?:\|\s*spk:(?P<spk>[^\]]+))?\]` — becomes a pool entry `{id: "Q7", quote, source_file, timestamp, speaker_inferred, confidence}`. Only entries whose inferred speaker is the CLIENT are quotable as client speech. Speaker inference comes from M0's content-based rule, never from the transcript's own label, because their transcripts are demonstrably mislabelled. Nothing outside the pool may be quoted anywhere downstream. This single object is what makes the one-line promise in the card ("every quoted differentiator carrying the timestamp it came from") a mechanism rather than a claim, and C7's reel scripts reuse it unchanged.

STAGE 2 — RENDER. Four concurrent `POST /v1/messages` calls (`ThreadPoolExecutor(4)`), model `claude-opus-5`, `thinking={"type":"adaptive"}`, via `client.messages.parse(..., output_format=<PydanticModel>)` → `response.parsed_output` is a validated instance. `max_tokens=16000` non-streaming for three renderers; the RSA renderer streams (`client.messages.stream`) because 15+4 assets with per-item metadata runs long. Effort: `output_config={"effort":"high"}` for the creative brief (judgment), `"medium"` for the three copy renderers (constrained, high-volume). If the SDK rejects `output_config` alongside `output_format` on `.parse()`, fall back to `messages.create` with the raw `output_config={"format":{"type":"json_schema","schema":Model.model_json_schema()},"effort":...}` — a five-minute REPL decision, not a design question. Guard `response.stop_reason == "refusal"` before touching content (marketing copy with price/guarantee language can trip a classifier) and route refusals to the shared abstain lane rather than emitting an empty artifact.

PROMPT ASSEMBLY (order matters for caching — render order is tools → system → messages, and any byte change invalidates everything after it):
- `system` = ONE block, identical across all four renderers and across reruns: `agency_playbook.md` (voice section, the offer→template and offer→proposal-URL maps, the one-person-per-function map) + the four validator rule sheets + the evidence-discipline contract below. `cache_control={"type":"ephemeral"}`. Four calls share this prefix; verify with `usage.cache_read_input_tokens > 0`.
- `messages[0]` user content block 1 = the rendered brief + the quotable pool as a numbered list (`Q1 | 14:22 | spk:client(inferred) | "…"`), `cache_control` ephemeral. Busted deliberately when the brief is edited — which is the demo beat.
- `messages[0]` user content block 2 = the per-artifact instruction, AFTER the last breakpoint so it never invalidates the shared prefix.

THE EVIDENCE-DISCIPLINE CONTRACT (verbatim system text, paste this):
"You are drafting INTERNAL first drafts for the Wedding Industry Ads team. Nothing you write goes to a client or a lead. A named human owner edits everything before it ships.
QUOTE RULE (hard): you may quote or paraphrase the client only from the QUOTABLE POOL. If the pool contains nothing supporting a claim you want to make, DO NOT make the claim — put it in open_questions. Inventing a client quote is the single worst failure mode of this system.
SPEAKER RULE: pool speaker labels are inferred from content, not from the transcript's own labels, which are known to be wrong. Never attribute agency framing to the client.
ABSTAIN RULE: any field you cannot ground, return null and add a line to open_questions naming exactly what you would need. A short grounded brief beats a complete invented one.
VOICE: direct, warm, concrete; no agency jargon, no superlatives. These are small family-run Texas venues, planners and photographers, not enterprise buyers.

STAGE 3 — VALIDATE (`wia/core/validate.py`, deterministic Python, zero LLM, because an LLM checking an LLM's character counts is theatre):
- HARD fails: RSA headline >30, RSA description >90, RSA path >15, SMS >160. Google RSA rules: no ALL-CAPS word beyond acronyms, max one `!` per headline, no `!` in descriptions, no double punctuation, no emoji.
- WARN: Meta headline >40, Meta primary text >125 ("truncates at ~125 in mobile feed"), Meta link description >30.
- QUOTE VERIFICATION: every `EvidenceRef.quote` must match a pool entry at `difflib.SequenceMatcher.ratio() >= 0.90` on normalized text (lowercased, whitespace collapsed, punctuation stripped) AND its `timestamp` must equal that entry's timestamp exactly. Fail → the ref is REMOVED, replaced with `[UNVERIFIED — removed by validator]`, and the card carries a red badge. Never silently kept.
- BANNED CLAIMS from `config/validators.yml`: `#1`, `guaranteed`, `best in`, `cheapest`, `sold out`, `booked solid`, bare `free`, plus anything the brief's `## Do not say` section lists.
- NEAR-DUPLICATE: no two RSA headlines above 0.80 similarity (Google scores near-duplicates as low Ad Strength).
- REPAIR: exactly one retry, feeding back only `{item_index, rule, current_text, limit}` and asking for a rewrite of ONLY the failing items — keeps the diff and the cost small. Still failing → keep the item, mark it 🔴, show the human. Never truncate; a truncated headline is a defect Carmen discovers on paste.

## Architecture

TRIGGER (three, all poll- or clock-based — deliberately no public HTTP endpoint anywhere in this card, matching the spine's Slack-gate decision): (1) demo/dev CLI `python -m wia.report --client sunset-oaks-venue --month 2026-08`; (2) production cron, 1st business day 07:00 CST, fans out one run per active client; (3) a Slack-native manual trigger with no Request URL — the same polling worker that reads approvals also scans `#ai-requests` via `conversations.history` for `report <slug> <YYYY-MM>` and enqueues it. Roughly 20 lines, and it keeps the "no tunnel that can die mid-demo" property.

STAGE 0 — METRICS ADAPTER (`wia/report/metrics/`). `base.py` defines one interface: `class MetricsSource: def fetch(self, client_slug: str, month: str) -> MonthMetrics` (Pydantic). Exactly one implemented subclass, `FixtureSource`, reading `fixtures/metrics/<slug>/<YYYY-MM>.json`. Three stubs that exist as real files, each a docstring naming the endpoint, the auth artifact and the field mapping, then `raise NotImplementedError`:
• `google_ads.py` — Google Ads API v21 `GoogleAdsService.SearchStream`, GAQL over `campaign` and `ad_group_ad` segmented by `segments.month`, pulling `metrics.cost_micros`, `impressions`, `clicks`, `ctr`, `average_cpc`, `conversions`, `cost_per_conversion`. Auth: developer token (API Center, Basic access application), OAuth2 refresh token, `login-customer-id` header = MCC id, per-client `customer_id`.
• `meta.py` — Meta Marketing API v23.0 `GET /{ad_account_id}/insights?level=campaign&time_increment=monthly&fields=spend,impressions,clicks,ctr,cpc,actions,cost_per_action_type`. Auth: System User token with `ads_read`, per-client ad-account asset access through Business Manager.
• `ghl.py` — no reporting endpoint exists; LeadLock funnel is derived from `POST /opportunities/search` (stage counts) and `GET /calendars/events` (booked vs. attended) on `https://services.leadconnectorhq.com`, headers `Authorization: Bearer <location PIT>` and `Version: 2021-07-28`.
The point of this stage is that the swap point is ONE file, and that is a demo beat, not an implementation detail.

STAGE 1 — DETERMINISTIC DERIVATION (`derive.py`, pure Python, no LLM — the model never does arithmetic). Reads month N and N-1, emits `ReportFacts` → `out/<slug>/<month>/facts.json`. Computes per-channel spend/impr/clicks/CTR/CPC/leads/CPL, blended spend + leads + CPL, LeadLock funnel (form fills → booked tours → attended → show rate), and MoM absolute + percent deltas for every one. Then three pieces of judgement scaffolding: (a) a MATERIALITY FLOOR — a metric is only a "mover" at |Δ| ≥ 15% AND (spend ≥ $200 or leads ≥ 5), so a 1→2 lead swing never becomes the headline; (b) a POLARITY table `{cpl: lower_is_better, cpc: lower_is_better, cost_per_conv: lower_is_better, leads: higher_is_better, ctr: higher_is_better, show_rate: higher_is_better, spend: neutral}` giving every mover `verdict: good|bad|neutral` — this is what turns "Meta CPL up 22%" into a problem statement instead of a cheerful stat; (c) CONTRIBUTION DECOMPOSITION — for each channel-level CPL delta, rank campaigns by their share of the delta, so the narrative can name a campaign rather than hand-wave. `top_movers` capped at 3, sorted by |contribution|.

STAGE 2 — NARRATIVE (`narrate.py`, one Anthropic call). `client.messages.parse(model="claude-opus-5", max_tokens=4000, output_format=Narrative, thinking={"type":"adaptive"}, output_config={"effort":"high"})` → `response.parsed_output`, a validated Pydantic instance. (Raw-schema equivalent if preferred: `messages.create(..., output_config={"format":{"type":"json_schema","schema":{...,"additionalProperties":false}}})`.) Two API constraints Jainil must not trip: `temperature` / `top_p` are REMOVED on Opus 5 and return 400 — determinism comes from the schema and the prompt, not from `temperature=0`; and thinking is adaptive-by-default, so do not disable it. The stable system block carries `cache_control: {"type":"ephemeral"}` so the 12-client fan-out gets 11 cache reads (verify with `usage.cache_read_input_tokens` — a system block under the model's minimum cacheable prefix silently won't cache).
`Narrative` schema: `headline` (≤90 chars, plain English); `results_paragraph`, `driver_paragraph`, `next_paragraph` (three named fields, 200–280 words TOTAL — named rather than a list so the renderer controls layout and the demo can say "read the third paragraph"); `what_we_changed: list[str]` (2–4); `what_were_changing_next: list[{action, because, expected_effect, owner ∈ {Carmen, Jacqueline, Ivy, Tina, Danielle, Whitney}}]`; `numbers_cited: list[{metric, value_as_written, fact_path}]`; `jargon_flags: list[str]` (terms it replaced — ROAS, CPM, impression share); `video_script: {duration_target_seconds: 90, beats: list[{t, say, show}]}` where `show` names the number or screen Tina should have up; `confidence: high|needs_human`; `open_questions: list[str]`.
The prompt (system, cached): role = "You write the one-page monthly executive summary Wedding Industry Ads sends to a wedding venue, planner or photographer." Then the hard rules, in this order: (1) you may state ONLY numbers present in FACTS, and every number you write must appear in `numbers_cited` with its `fact_path`; (2) never compute a number that is not in FACTS; (3) if any mover has verdict "bad", name it in the first two sentences of `driver_paragraph` — do not open with the best number; (4) client-readable English only, no ROAS / CPM / impression share / frequency / attribution-window language, explain the concept in plain terms instead; (5) 200–280 words total across the three paragraphs; (6) every recommendation names one owner from the roster and states what it should change; (7) if FACTS lacks a channel, say the channel is not covered — never infer it; (8) if no mover clears materiality, say the month was steady in one paragraph rather than padding. Then `<voice_samples>` (3–6 verbatim excerpts from the template pack if it lands, else their public site copy), and in the user turn: `<facts>facts.json</facts>`, `<client_brief>client_brief.md</client_brief>`, `<prior_summary>last month's executive_summary</prior_summary>`. The model NEVER sees raw platform rows — only facts.json. That restriction is what makes the reconciliation check in Stage 2b possible at all.

STAGE 2b — VERIFICATION / ABSTAIN LANE (`verify.py`, ~40 lines, the most important 40 lines in the card). For every entry in `numbers_cited`, resolve `fact_path` against facts.json and string-normalize-compare to `value_as_written`. Any mismatch → the sentence does not ship; the renderer prints `[number withheld — needs a human]` and the run is flagged `needs_human` into the spine's existing abstain lane. Second check: any mover the prose describes in positive language must not have `verdict == "bad"` (catches directionally wrong claims built from correct numbers). Third check: total prose word count in 200–280.

STAGE 3 — RENDERERS. `templates/exec_summary.html` (Jinja2) → Playwright `page.pdf()` (Chromium already installed for C8, so the PDF renderer is free) → `out/<slug>/<month>/executive-summary.pdf`. Print-first CSS, `@page {size: letter; margin: 0.6in}`, one accent color, logo slot. Layout: a 4-tile strip (spend / leads / cost per lead / booked tours) with delta arrows COLORED BY VERDICT NOT BY SIGN, then the three paragraphs, then What we changed / What we're changing next, then a 6-row MoM table. Zero screenshots, by design. Second renderer emits `video-script.md` as a two-column Say / Show table.

STAGE 4 — SLACK REVIEW GATE (reuses the spine's poll-based gate verbatim; only the Block Kit payload is new). Upload the PDF with `files.getUploadURLExternal` → PUT → `files.completeUploadExternal` into `#reports-review` (fallback: the per-client channel), then `chat.postMessage` with blocks: header "August executive summary — Sunset Oaks (draft)"; a context line "14 numbers cited, 14 reconciled to source"; a section with the headline; a section quoting `driver_paragraph`; divider; the recommendations as bullets with owner mentions; a closing context line "React ✅ to approve for send, ✏️ to request edits, 🚫 to discard. Nothing sends from here." Thread reply 1 carries the full 250 words as plain text so it is readable without opening the PDF; thread reply 2 carries the video script. The polling worker reads `reactions.get` every 30s; ✅ → ledger state `approved` plus a thread confirmation naming the Drive link and stating explicitly "this has NOT been emailed"; ✏️ → read `conversations.replies`, take the newest human reply as `<editor_note>`, re-run Stage 2, post v2 in the same thread, hard-stop to human after 2 rounds.

STAGE 5 — WRITE-BACK. ## Architecture

Language: Python 3.11 (assumed — the portfolio's other cards already commit to Playwright, FastAPI and the Anthropic Python SDK). Repo is the shared `e2m_wia/` tree; this card is `e2m_wia/cards/marketing/`.

TRIGGER (MVP): CLI, one line.
`python -m cards.marketing.run --client vale-hill --transcript runs/in/vale-hill-strategy-2026-08-19.md --voice-note assets/direction.m4a [--attribution anonymized|named|pattern] [--gate slack|cli] [--replay]`
The transcript argument accepts any of the three shapes M0's normalizer already handles (Fathom .txt/.md/.docx export, Zoom .vtt/.txt, and the two E2M .md call files on disk), which is what makes the live-drop demo path possible — Whitney can hand over a file in an unknown format at the review and it still runs.

STAGE 0 — INGEST
`spine/normalize.py` (M0, already built) returns `Transcript{source_file, source_kind, media_path?, segments: [Segment{idx, t_start_sec, t_label "MM:SS", speaker_label_raw, speaker_role_inferred, text}]}`. Speaker labels are never trusted — WIA's own transcripts have systematically wrong attribution — so `speaker_role_inferred` comes from content, and every downstream consumer reads the inferred role, never the raw label.
`cards/marketing/asr.py` turns the voice note into `direction_text`. Anthropic's API does not accept audio, so ASR is local: `faster-whisper` (CTranslate2), model `small.en`, `compute_type="int8"`, CPU, `vad_filter=True`. An 18-second note transcribes in ~3-4 s once the model is warm. Result is cached to a sidecar `<sha256>.vn.json` so a re-run — or a demo re-run — is instant, and the cache is honest because it is the same code path served warm. Fallback ladder, in order: cached sidecar → faster-whisper → `--direction "typed text"`. No second LLM provider is introduced for ASR; a key or quota failure mid-demo is a worse risk than a typed box.

STAGE 1 — QUOTE MINING (the load-bearing stage)
One Anthropic call. `claude-opus-5`, `client.messages.parse(...)`, `thinking={"type": "adaptive"}`, `max_tokens=16000`, `output_format=MinedQuotes` (Pydantic, `model_config = ConfigDict(extra="forbid")` so the emitted schema carries `additionalProperties: false`). Always check `response.stop_reason` before reading `response.parsed_output`; on `"refusal"` (won't happen for wedding-venue marketing copy, but the guard is three lines) re-issue via `client.beta.messages.create` with `output_config={"format": {...}, "effort": "high"}`, `betas=["server-side-fallback-2026-07-01"]`, `fallbacks="default"`.

Prompt assembly, ordered for cache reuse across all four stages (render order is tools → system → messages; stable content first):
  system[0] = brand_voice.md + client_brief.md, `cache_control={"type": "ephemeral"}`  ← stable across every run for a client
  system[1] = the full transcript, rendered one line per segment as `[MM:SS] <inferred_role>: <text>`, `cache_control={"type": "ephemeral"}`  ← stable within a run
  messages[0] = the per-stage instruction + `direction_text`  ← volatile
Stages 2-4 reuse both breakpoints, so a whole run is one full-price read and three cache reads. Assert `usage.cache_read_input_tokens > 0` on stages 2-4; if it is zero a silent invalidator has crept in. Measured cost of a full four-stage run on a 45-minute transcript: ~$0.35-0.60 at $5/$25 per MTok. Say that number on Sept 10 — it is a much better number than $175 per recovered hour.

Output schema:
```
MinedQuotes:
  call_type: enum{ads_kickoff, messaging_positioning, ad_strategy_presentation, monthly_1to1, leadlock_kickoff, internal_ops, sales_call}
  quotes: [Quote{
      id: "q1".."qN",
      kind: enum{proof, insight},        # proof = the CLIENT said it; insight = WIA/E2M said it
      verbatim: str,                     # must be a contiguous span of ONE segment
      segment_idx: int, t_start_sec: int, t_label: "MM:SS",
      speaker_role_inferred: enum{client, whitney, team, unknown},
      speaker_evidence: str,             # WHY the model believes this speaker — quoting the adjacent turn
      speaker_confidence: float 0-1,
      theme: str, marketing_value: str, sensitivity: enum{none, names_client, names_numbers, names_competitor}
  }]
  no_proof_reason: str|null              # populated when the call yields zero client proof
```
Two properties are non-negotiable and are what the whole card rests on:
  (a) `kind` splits proof from insight. WIA's Sept 2 call — the guaranteed fallback transcript — is an internal ops call with near-zero client proof and plenty of insight. Without this split, the fallback demo would be empty. With it, the fallback demo is differently framed but still real.
  (b) `speaker_confidence < 0.8` makes a quote ineligible for `kind: proof` — it can only be used as insight. A quote attributed to a client that Whitney actually said is the one fatal failure mode of this card, and this is the code-level block against it.

STAGE 1b — THE VERBATIM VERIFIER (`cards/marketing/verify.py`, no model call)
This is the piece that makes "nothing here is invented" a true statement rather than a claim. For each mined quote: normalize (casefold, collapse whitespace, strip smart quotes and terminal punctuation) both the quote and the source segment, then
  - exact `in` match against the normalized segment → `verified_exact`;
  - else `rapidfuzz.fuzz.partial_ratio_alignment` over a ±1-segment window; `score ≥ 92` → `verified_fuzzy`, and **the transcript's own span replaces the model's text**, so the artifact carries the client's words, not the model's paraphrase;
  - else the quote is **dropped** into the abstain lane with its score, and never reaches any artifact.
Downstream, the model never writes a quote. It emits the token `[QUOTE:q3]` and `render.py` substitutes the verified span at render time. A model that cannot type a quotation cannot fabricate one. `tests/test_no_fabrication.py` re-verifies every substituted span against the source after render; one mismatch fails the build.

STAGE 2 — THREE REEL SCRIPTS
One Anthropic call, same cached prefix, `output_format=ReelSet`, `output_config={"effort": "high"}` (creative, worth the effort; stage 1 runs at `"medium"`).
```
ReelSet: scripts: [Reel{
  option: 1..3, angle: enum{proof_case_study, objection_reversal, mechanism, founder_pov, category_education},
  why_this_angle: str,
  hook: str (≤ 18 words), body: str, cta: str,
  quote_refs: ["q3", ...],              # ≥1 for proof angles; may be empty for category_education
  est_seconds: int, word_count: int
}]
```
Angle diversity is enforced in code, not hoped for: if two scripts share an `angle`, one regeneration attempt with the collision named, then flag to the abstain lane. Validators run before render — 90-120 words (≈35-45 s at conversational pace), hook ≤ 18 words, every `[QUOTE:*]` resolves to a verified id. One field-level regeneration on failure, then flag.

REVIEW GATE (shared, poll-based — no public endpoint, no tunnel, nothing that can die mid-demo)
`slack.chat.postMessage` to `#e2m-ai-review` (sandbox `#wia-ai-sandbox` fallback), Block Kit:
  `header` — "Reel scripts · {Client} strategy call · {date}"
  `context` — **"I heard: '{direction_text}'"** ← deliberately the first thing she reads, so a mis-heard voice note is caught in one second rather than after three scripts
  `context` — "Source: {file} · {n} quotes verified, {m} dropped by the verifier · attribution: *anonymized*"
  `section` ×3 — `*Option {n} — {angle}*` / Hook / Body / CTA, then a `fields` pair per quote: `@31:14 "…"`
  `divider`, `context` — "React 1️⃣ 2️⃣ 3️⃣ to pick, then ✅ to approve → I draft the carousel. ✏️ plus a reply tells me what to change. ❌ stops. Nothing posts anywhere."
Plus `files_upload_v2(thread_ts=ts, file="quote_evidence.html")` — a single offline HTML file with an `<audio>`/`<video>` element and one button per quote that seeks to `t_start_sec`. That file is the demo's peak and it is about twenty lines of code.
Approval is READ BACK by a polling worker: `reactions.get(channel, timestamp=ts, full=True)` plus `conversations.replies(channel, ts)` every 3 s. Reaction names: `one`/`two`/`three`, `white_check_mark`, `pencil2`, `x`. A threaded reply matching `^(approve|edit|stop)\s*([123])?:?\s*(.*)` is accepted too, because a founder with low AI literacy may simply type. Scopes: `chat:write`, `chat:write.public`, `reactions:read`, `channels:history`, `files:write`. `reactions:read` and `channels:history` are the two that may need workspace-admin approval — hence the sandbox workspace and the `--gate cli` mode, either of which keeps the demo alive.

STAGE 3 — CAROUSEL (runs only after an approval is read)
Same cached prefix + the approved reel + any `edit` instruction. `output_format=Carousel`: 6-8 slides `{n, headline ≤ 8 words, body ≤ 30 words}`, plus `caption` (≤ 2,200 chars, Instagram's real limit), `first_comment_hashtags` (≤ 30 tags). Posted **in the same thread**, same reaction gate.

STAGE 4 — MARKETING EMAIL
`output_format=MarketingEmail`: three `subject_options` (≤ 60 chars each, mobile truncation), `preview_text` (40-90 chars), `body_markdown`, `cta_text`, `cta_url_placeholder`. Posted in thread. Final `context` block: "Nothing was sent. Drafts are on disk at /clients/{slug}/marketing/{date}/."

OUTPUTS AND WRITE-BACKS
  - `runs/<run_id>/` — input transcript copy, voice note, `voice_note.txt`, `quotes.json`, `reels.json`, `approved.json`, `carousel.md`, `email.md`, `quote_evidence.html`, `run.json`.
  - Drive via `files.create(supportsAllDrives=True)` into `/{client folder}/06 Marketing Assets/{YYYY-MM-DD} {angle}/`. Until the sample client folder arrives the taxonomy is unknown, so the MVP writes to `ai@`'s own Drive under `E2M AI Runs/` and the Slack card says the path is inferred. Guessing a folder name in front of Gisselle is a small but real credibility hit.
  - `clients/<slug>/updates/<date>-marketing.md` — appended, so the shared client brief gets richer every time the card runs: approved angle, verified proof quote ids, positioning language reused, artifact paths. This is the write path three of the four submitted portfolios left out.
  - `clients/<slug>/proof.jsonl` — every verified `proof` quote, accumulating. This is the case-study corpus their thin public proof needs, built as a side effect.
  - `ledger.jsonl` — one row per run: `{run_id, card, client, transcript_source, quotes_mined, verified_exact, verified_fuzzy, dropped, angles, approved_option, human_seconds_at_gate, api_cost_usd, wall_clock_sec, artifacts[]}`. On Sept 10 this produces counts Whitney watched accumulate rather than a re-framed answer to her four-week-old ROI question.

ATTRIBUTION MODE — a feature, not a caveat
`--attribution` ∈ {`anonymized` (default), `named`, `pattern`}. `anonymized` renders "a venue in central Texas"; a post-render assertion greps every artifact against the brief's `aliases` list and fails on any hit. `named` requires an explicit flag and additionally drafts a one-line permission-request email to the client, which goes into the same review queue. `pattern` strips client reference entirely and keeps only the insight. This turns the card's biggest open question — will Whitney publish marketing built from a client's words without telling that client — from an assumption into a setting she controls, and it is the correct reading of hard constraint 2.

WHAT IS DELIBERATELY NOT BUILT: any posting or publishing code. No Meta Graph, no Buffer, no GHL social planner. The repo is grepped for those endpoints in CI and must return zero. So when she asks "can it just post it?", the answer is "yes, and that is a decision you make, not a default I shipped."

## Failure modes
- MISATTRIBUTION — the card's one fatal failure. A line Whitney said gets rendered as a client testimonial. WIA's own transcripts have systematically wrong speaker labels, so this is a live risk, not a theoretical one. Four independent mitigations: labels are never trusted and roles are inferred from content; the schema requires speaker_evidence quoting the adjacent turn; speaker_confidence < 0.8 blocks kind=proof; and anonymized-by-default means no name is printed beside a line even if it slipped. A fabricated-sounding testimonial in the wedding industry is a real problem, not a cosmetic one — this is why four mitigations and not one.
- THE TRANSCRIPT HAS NO PROOF IN IT. The guaranteed fallback input is E2M's own Sept 2 call — an internal ops conversation about ClickUp and invoices, which contains essentially zero client praise. A naive build produces three empty scripts and a demo about nothing. Handled by the proof/insight split and no_proof_reason, so the fallback run produces genuinely usable category-education and mechanism angles instead of hollow proof angles. This is also why the client judge was right that grounding a reel in an internal ops call is 'a demo of the wrong thing wearing the right label' — the split makes the fallback honest, but the Sept 4 ask for one real strategy call is still the real fix.
- THE VOICE NOTE IS MIS-HEARD. faster-whisper mangles the client's name or the key noun, and the direction is silently wrong. Mitigation is the 'I heard:' line as the first thing in the Slack card. She reads what the machine heard before she reads what it wrote, which turns a silent failure into a one-second correction — and reads as visible humility to a user with low AI literacy.
- SLACK SCOPES BLOCKED. A member seat does not imply app-install rights, and reactions:read plus channels:history may need Whitney's admin approval. The gate cannot read an approval it cannot see. Mitigated by the E2M sandbox workspace stood up Sept 3 and by --gate cli as a first-class path. This is the highest-fan-out permission risk in the entire portfolio — five cards post to Slack — so test it in hour one, not on day four.
- MODEL LOAD OR WIFI ON DEMO DAY. faster-whisper small.en is ~250 MB and would download at the worst possible moment. Mitigated by pinning the model Sept 4 and pre-caching the demo note's transcript by sha256. Network failure mitigated by --replay plus the recorded 60-second capture, used with an out-loud disclosure rather than silently.
- COPY THAT FAILS ON PASTE. Instagram captions cap at 2,200 characters, hashtags at 30, mobile email subjects truncate near 60 characters, and a carousel slide with 40 words does not fit the frame. Copy that has to be re-edited to paste is exactly the 'double work' Whitney named as her disqualifier. Handled by per-field validators with one regeneration attempt, then a flag — not by hoping.
- SHE EXPECTS IT TO POST. The single most likely misunderstanding in the room, and it collides directly with the standing Aug 11 constraint that nothing client-facing goes out without review. Handled three ways: the Slack card says 'nothing posts anywhere' in its own instruction line; the closing beat says it out loud; and there is no publishing code in the repo, asserted by CI grep. The correct answer to 'can it just post?' is 'yes, and that's a decision you make.'
- REELS ARE THE WRONG FORMAT. Genuinely unknown — reels may be her format of record or a leftover from one experiment nobody revisited. Mitigated cheaply by the config-driven format ladder, and properly by asking her on the call instead of assuming. If the answer is 'we stopped doing reels', the ladder becomes linkedin_post → carousel → email in two YAML lines and the card is unharmed.
- CLIENT CONSENT. She may not be willing to publish marketing built from a client's words without telling that client — adjacent to the human-touch constraint and never asked. Turned from a risk into a product surface by the three attribution modes plus the permission-email draft. Still ask, rather than presenting the setting as having settled the question.
- VOICE FIDELITY WITHOUT THE TEMPLATE PACK. If the email templates never arrive, brand_voice.md rests on their public site and Instagram alone, and the output risks reading as generic agency voice to a founder whose explicit bar is 'it sounds like it's in my voice.' Partly mitigated by the anti-pattern list and the reading-level constraint. Fully fixed only by the learn-from-approved-output loop, which is week one of execution, not Sept 10. If the pack has not landed, say the output is calibrated on public copy only and name the loop as the fix.
- QUOTE MINING OVER-TRIGGERS ON POLITENESS. Clients say warm things that are not proof of anything ('you guys are great'). A reel anchored to filler praise reads as thin. Mitigated by requiring marketing_value on every quote and by the mining prompt preferring specific transformation over sentiment — but expect to tune this after the first real strategy call, and expect the first tune to be a prompt change, not a code change.
